## PPCM Definition

Peripartum cardiomyopathy (PPCM) is an idiopathic heart failure that develops **toward the end of pregnancy or within months after delivery** in women without prior structural heart disease. It is characterized by **severe left‑ventricular systolic dysfunction** (LVEF usually < 45%).

---

## PPCM Clinical Criteria

| Feature | Typical Value |
|---------|---------------|
| **Left‑ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF)** | < 45 % |
| **Timing** | Late pregnancy (≥ 34 weeks) or postpartum (≤ 6 months) |
| **Prior heart disease** | None identified |

---

## PPCM Risk Factors

- **Maternal age**: > 30–35 years  
- **Parity**: multiparity or grand multiparity  
- **Ethnicity**: higher incidence in African descent  
- **Pregnancy complications**: pre‑eclampsia, eclampsia, gestational hypertension, long‑term beta‑agonist tocolysis  
- **Multiple gestation**: twins or higher order  
- **Co‑morbidities**: hypertension, obesity, diabetes  
- **Substance use**: tobacco, alcohol  

---

## PPCM Differential Diagnosis

| Category | Differential Conditions |
|----------|--------------------------|
| **Pregnancy‑related** | Pre‑eclampsia / eclampsia; normal third‑trimester/postpartum physiologic changes |
| **Pre‑existing/unmasked heart disease** | Dilated cardiomyopathy; valvular disease (e.g., mitral stenosis); congenital heart disease |
| **Acquired cardiac pathology** | Acute viral myocarditis; ischemic heart disease / spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD); Takotsubo cardiomyopathy |
| **Non‑cardiac / pulmonary** | Pulmonary embolism; severe anemia; pneumonia/ARDS |

---

## VTE Genetic Risk Factors

- **Factor V Leiden mutation**  
- **Prothrombin G20210A mutation**  
- **Protein C, Protein S, or antithrombin deficiencies**

---

## VTE Medical Condition Risk Factors

- **Cancer** (especially during chemotherapy)  
- **Heart disease** (e.g., heart failure)  
- **Previous VTE**  
- **Obesity** (↑ BMI)  
- **Hormonal therapy** (estrogen‑containing contraceptives or HRT)  
- **Pregnancy & postpartum period**  
- **Major surgery or trauma** (orthopedic procedures, significant injury)

---

## VTE Lifestyle & Situational Risk Factors

- **Immobility**: long‑distance travel, bed rest, sedentary lifestyle  
- **Smoking**  
- **Hospitalization**: prolonged stay, ICU  
- **Age**: risk increases with age  
- **Medical devices**: central venous catheters, pacemakers  

---

## VTE Inflammatory & Other Factors

- **Autoimmune disorders**: lupus, inflammatory bowel disease  
- **Dehydration** (↑ blood viscosity)  
- **Certain medications** that alter coagulation
