
## Transesophageal Echo in Critical Care

Transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) is a low-risk, high-yield diagnostic and monitoring tool in the ICU, particularly when transthoracic windows are limited or when detailed assessment of posterior structures (atria, valves, aorta, LA appendage) is required. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3] It provides superior visualization of cardiac structures, enables serial hemodynamic monitoring, and guides procedural interventions (e.g., cannula positioning, volume responsiveness). [^1_1][^1_4]

***

## TEE Probe Levels and Standard Views

TEE imaging is organized by **probe level** (upper esophageal, mid-esophageal, transgastric, descending aorta) and **multiplane angle** (0°–180°). The ASE/SCA guidelines define **28 standard views** for a comprehensive exam. [^1_5][^1_6]

### 1. **Upper Esophageal (UE) Views** (≈20–25 cm from incisors)

| View | Multiplane Angle | Structures Visualized | Clinical Utility |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **UE Great Vessels** | 0° | Ascending aorta, main PA, right PA | Great vessel anatomy, dissection |
| **UE Aortic Arch** | 0° (turn probe right on exit) | Aortic arch, branches | Arch pathology, coarctation |
| **UE Pulmonary Artery** | 40–50° | AV, main PA, RVOT, LA | RVOT obstruction, PA anatomy |
| **UE Aortic Valve** | 40–50° | AV, LAA, LA | AV morphology, LAA thrombus |
| **UE Pulmonary Veins** | Variable | Right \& left pulmonary veins | TAPVR, anomalous drainage |

[^1_7][^1_5][^1_6]

***

### 2. **Mid-Esophageal (ME) Views** (≈30–35 cm from incisors)

This is the **workhorse level** for most ICU TEE exams.


| View | Multiplane Angle | Structures Visualized | Clinical Utility |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **ME 4-Chamber** | 0° | LV, RV, LA, RA, MV, TV | Global LV/RV function, valvular assessment |
| **ME 2-Chamber** | 60° | LV (anterolateral \& inferior walls), LA, MV | LV regional function, MV pathology |
| **ME Long-Axis (3-Chamber)** | 120–140° | LVOT, AV, LA, MV, ascending aorta | LVOT obstruction, AS, MR |
| **ME Bicaval** | 90° (turn right) | SVC, IVC, RA, RAA, interatrial septum | Volume status (IVC/SVC), ASD, RA thrombus |
| **ME RV Inflow-Outflow** | Variable (rotate from 4-chamber) | RV inflow, TV, RVOT, PV | RV function, TR, RVOT obstruction |
| **ME Aortic Valve Short-Axis** | 30–50° | AV (3 cusps), LA | AV morphology, stenosis/regurgitation |
| **ME Mitral Commissural** | 60–70° | MV commissures, LA, LV | MV pathology, endocarditis |
| **ME LAA View** | 90° (turn left) | LAA, LA, LUPV | LAA thrombus (AF, cardioversion) |

[^1_7][^1_5][^1_8][^1_9][^1_6][^1_10]

**Key ICU pearls:**

- **ME 4-chamber** is the starting view for most focused exams; assess RV:LV ratio, septal motion, and global function. [^1_8][^1_9]
- **ME Bicaval** is ideal for assessing volume status via SVC/IVC collapsibility. [^1_4]
- **ME RV Inflow-Outflow** is critical for RV function, TR severity, and RVOT assessment. [^1_1][^1_9]

***

### 3. **Transgastric (TG) Views** (≈35–40 cm, advance into stomach)

| View | Multiplane Angle | Structures Visualized | Clinical Utility |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **TG Basal Short-Axis** | 0–20° | LV base, MV, RV, TV | LV/RV function, MV/TV morphology |
| **TG Mid-Papillary Short-Axis** | 0–20° | LV (papillary muscles), RV | LV regional function, volume status |
| **TG Apical Short-Axis** | 0–20° (advance further) | LV apex, RV apex | Apical wall motion, LV/RV function |
| **TG Long-Axis** | 90° | LV, MV apparatus | MV pathology, LVOT |
| **TG RV Inflow-Outflow** | Variable (rotate right from basal SAX) | RV, TV, RVOT, PV | RV function, TR, RVOT gradients |
| **TG RV Basal View** | 0–20° (rotate right) | RV basal segments | RV regional function |

[^1_11][^1_5][^1_12][^1_6][^1_10]

**Key ICU pearls:**

- **TG views** are the **best TEE views for assessing LV and RV systolic function** (analogous to TTE parasternal short-axis). [^1_11]
- **TG RV Inflow-Outflow** allows Doppler across the pulmonary valve for PS/PR assessment. [^1_11]
- **Deep TG views** provide accurate aortic valve gradients (AS assessment). [^1_11]

***

### 4. **Descending Aorta Views** (pullback from stomach to esophagus)

| View | Multiplane Angle | Structures Visualized | Clinical Utility |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Descending Aorta Long-Axis** | 90–110° | Descending thoracic aorta, left pleural space | Aortic dissection, aneurysm, pleural effusion |

[^1_7][^1_6]

## RV Function Assessment: TEE vs TTE Parameters

RV assessment is **challenging** due to its complex geometry (triangular inlet, trabeculated apex, crescentic shape), but essential in critical care (ARDS, PE, RV failure post-cardiotomy, sepsis, ECMO). [^1_13][^1_14][^1_15]

### **Core RV Functional Parameters** (Applicable to Both TTE and TEE)

| Parameter | Normal Value | Mild Dysfunction | Moderate Dysfunction | Severe Dysfunction | Measurement View | Notes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **TAPSE** (mm) | ≥17 | 14–17 | 10–13 | <10 | A4C (TTE) / ME 4-chamber (TEE) | Longitudinal function; angle-dependent; load-sensitive |
| **RV S′** (cm/s) | >9.5 | 9–11 | 6–8 | <6 | TDI at lateral tricuspid annulus (A4C/ME 4-chamber) | Less load-dependent than TAPSE; correlates with RVEF |
| **RV FAC** (%) | ≥35 (men ≥30, women ≥35) | 34–37 | 30–33 | <30 | A4C (TTE) / ME 4-chamber or TG SAX (TEE) | Global systolic function; requires good endocardial definition |
| **RV Free Wall Strain** (%) | >−20 (more negative = better) | −18 to −21 | −14 to −17 | >−14 | A4C (TTE) / ME 4-chamber (TEE) | Sensitive to early RV dysfunction; afterload-sensitive |
| **3D RVEF** (%) | >50 | 45–50 | 35–45 | <35 | 3D echo (TTE/TEE) | Gold standard if available; requires good image quality |
| **TAPSE/PASP** (mm/mmHg) | >0.55 | 0.41–0.55 | 0.30–0.40 | <0.30 | A4C + TR jet (TTE/TEE) | RV-PA coupling; prognostic in PH, PE, ARDS |

[^1_13][^1_16][^1_17][^1_18][^1_19][^1_14][^1_20][^1_21][^1_22]

***

### **RV Structural Parameters** (Dilation/Remodeling)

| Parameter | Normal | Abnormal (Dilation) | Measurement View |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **RV Basal Diameter** | <42 mm | >42 mm | A4C (TTE) / ME 4-chamber (TEE) |
| **RV Mid-Cavity Diameter** | <33 mm | >33 mm | A4C (TTE) / ME 4-chamber (TEE) |
| **RVEDD/LVEDD Ratio** | <0.9 | >0.9 | A4C (TTE) / ME 4-chamber (TEE) |
| **RVEDA/LVEDA Ratio** | <0.6 | >0.6 (mild), >1 (severe) | A4C (TTE) / ME 4-chamber (TEE) |
| **Septal Flattening (D-sign)** | Absent | Present (systolic or diastolic) | Parasternal SAX (TTE) / TG SAX (TEE) |

[^1_23][^1_19][^1_24][^1_25][^1_21]

***

### **RV Afterload Assessment**

| Parameter | Normal | Abnormal | Clinical Context |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **PA Acceleration Time (AccT)** | >100 ms | <100 ms | ↑ RV afterload (PE, PH) |
| **PA Doppler Envelope** | Rounded | Notched/mid-systolic deceleration | ↑ PVR, RV-PA uncoupling |
| **TR Jet Velocity** | <2.8 m/s | >2.8–3.4 m/s (mild PH), >3.4 m/s (severe PH) | Estimate PASP |
| **RVOT VTI** | >18 cm | <18 cm | ↓ RV stroke volume |

[^1_26][^1_24][^1_21]

## RV Assessment in Specific Pathologies

### **1. Acute Pulmonary Embolism (PE)**

**Echo Findings Suggestive of RV Strain:**

- **RV/LV end-diastolic diameter ratio >1** (A4C) [^1_27][^1_25]
- **RV basal diameter >42 mm** or **mid-cavity >33 mm** [^1_24]
- **TAPSE <16 mm**, **S′ <10 cm/s**, **FAC <35%** [^1_23][^1_24]
- **McConnell sign** (akinesia of RV free wall with preserved apex) [^1_27]
- **Septal flattening** (D-sign) in systole [^1_24][^1_21]
- **TR jet velocity >2.8 m/s** (PASP >40 mmHg) [^1_24]
- **PA AccT <100 ms** [^1_24]

**Prognostic Value:**

- TAPSE <16 mm and RV/LV >1 are associated with increased mortality and need for thrombolysis. [^1_23][^1_27]

***

### **2. ARDS and Mechanical Ventilation**

**Pathophysiology:** ↑ PVR from hypoxia, hypercapnia, high PEEP → RV afterload mismatch.

**Echo Findings:**

- **RVEDA/LVEDA >0.6** (early), **>1** (severe) [^1_21]
- **TAPSE <17 mm**, **S′ <9.5 cm/s**, **FAC <35%** [^1_21]
- **Septal dyskinesia** (paradoxical motion) [^1_21]
- **PA AccT <100 ms** [^1_26]
- **RVOT VTI ↓** (↓ RV stroke volume) [^1_26]

**Clinical Pearls:**

- TEE is preferred in ARDS (poor TTE windows, prone positioning). [^1_28][^1_26]
- Serial TEE can guide PEEP titration, recruitment maneuvers, and ECMO initiation. [^1_28][^1_26]

***

### **3. Cardiogenic Shock (LV Failure with Secondary RV Dysfunction)**

**Echo Findings:**

- **Biventricular dilation** (RVEDA/LVEDA >0.6)
- **TAPSE <17 mm**, **S′ <9.5 cm/s**
- **Septal flattening** (volume overload pattern)
- **TR severity** (functional, due to annular dilation)

**Differentiation from Primary RV Failure:**

- **LV dysfunction** (LVEF <40%) is the primary driver.
- RV dysfunction is **secondary** (improves with LV unloading).

***

### **4. Post-Cardiotomy RV Failure**

**Echo Findings:**

- **RV dilation** (RVEDA >18 cm²)
- **TAPSE <14 mm**, **S′ <8 cm/s**, **FAC <30%** [^1_23]
- **Severe TR** (annular dilation, leaflet tethering)
- **Septal motion abnormalities** (paradoxical or delayed)

**TEE Advantages:**

- Superior visualization of RV, TV, and PA after sternotomy (TTE windows limited). [^1_29]
- Guides inotrope/vasopressor titration, RVAD/ECMO cannula positioning. [^1_1]

***

### **5. Pulmonary Hypertension (Chronic)**

**Echo Findings:**

- **RV hypertrophy** (wall thickness >5 mm)
- **RV dilation** (basal >42 mm, mid >33 mm)
- **TAPSE <17 mm**, **S′ <9.5 cm/s**, **FAC <35%**
- **RV free wall strain >−20%** (less negative)
- **TR jet velocity >3.4 m/s** (PASP >50 mmHg)
- **PA AccT <100 ms**, **notched Doppler envelope** [^1_24]
- **TAPSE/PASP <0.30** (severe RV-PA uncoupling) [^1_16]

***

### **6. Sepsis and Vasoplegic Shock**

**Echo Findings:**

- **RV dysfunction** in 30–50% of septic shock (TAPSE <17 mm, S′ <9.5 cm/s) [^1_15]
- **RV dilation** (RVEDA/LVEDA >0.6)
- **Septal flattening** (acute cor pulmonale)
- **Hyperdynamic LV** (early), **biventricular failure** (late)

**Clinical Pearls:**

- RV dysfunction in sepsis is associated with ↑ mortality and prolonged ventilation. [^1_15]
- TEE guides fluid responsiveness (SVC/IVC collapsibility) and inotrope selection. [^1_4]

***

## TEE-Specific RV Assessment Views

| View | Parameter Assessed | Technique |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **ME 4-Chamber** | TAPSE, S′, FAC, RV/LV ratio, septal motion | M-mode at lateral tricuspid annulus (TAPSE); TDI at annulus (S′); trace RV endocardium (FAC) |
| **ME RV Inflow-Outflow** | RVOT diameter, TR jet, RVOT VTI | Align Doppler through RVOT; measure VTI for RV stroke volume |
| **TG Basal/Mid SAX** | RV FAC, septal motion, RV/LV area ratio | Trace RV and LV endocardium at end-diastole and end-systole |
| **TG RV Inflow-Outflow** | TR severity, RVOT gradients, PV Doppler | Rotate probe right from TG basal SAX; align Doppler through TV/RVOT/PV |
| **ME Bicaval** | SVC collapsibility (volume status) | M-mode or visual assessment of SVC diameter variation with respiration |

[^1_1][^1_11][^1_9][^1_10][^1_19]

***

## Practical ICU TEE Algorithm for RV Assessment

```
1. Start with ME 4-Chamber (0°)
   - Assess RV:LV size ratio (RVEDA/LVEDA)
   - Measure TAPSE (M-mode at lateral annulus)
   - Measure S′ (TDI at lateral annulus)
   - Trace RV endocardium for FAC

2. Rotate to ME RV Inflow-Outflow
   - Assess TR severity (color + spectral Doppler)
   - Measure RVOT VTI (for RV stroke volume)
   - Estimate PASP (TR jet velocity + RAP)

3. Advance to TG Basal/Mid SAX (0–20°)
   - Confirm RV:LV area ratio
   - Assess septal motion (D-sign)
   - Re-measure FAC (cross-check with ME 4-chamber)

4. Rotate to TG RV Inflow-Outflow
   - Assess PV Doppler (AccT, envelope shape)
   - Measure RVOT gradients (if PS suspected)

5. Pullback to ME Bicaval (90°, turn right)
   - Assess SVC/IVC collapsibility (volume status)
```

## Common Pitfalls and Exam Traps

| Pitfall | Why It Matters | How to Avoid |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **TAPSE angle dependency** | Underestimates RV function if M-mode cursor not aligned with annular motion | Align cursor parallel to lateral tricuspid annulus motion (not perpendicular) |
| **FAC requires good endocardial definition** | Poor RV endocardial tracing → inaccurate FAC | Use ME 4-chamber + TG SAX; optimize gain/depth; use contrast if needed |
| **Load sensitivity of TAPSE/S′** | TAPSE ↓ with ↓ preload (hypovolemia) even if intrinsic RV function normal | Interpret in clinical context; use TAPSE/PASP ratio for RV-PA coupling |
| **Septal flattening can be diastolic or systolic** | Diastolic = volume overload; Systolic = pressure overload (PE, PH) | Assess timing of septal flattening in cardiac cycle (systole vs diastole) |
| **TR jet underestimation** | Poor Doppler alignment → underestimated PASP | Use multiple views (ME 4-chamber, ME RV inflow-outflow, TG RV inflow-outflow) to optimize TR jet alignment |

[^1_13][^1_19][^1_14][^1_24][^1_21]

## Topic Pearls

- **TEE is superior to TTE** in ICU for RV assessment due to better windows (especially in ARDS, post-sternotomy, obese patients). [^1_1][^1_29][^1_28]
- **Core RV function parameters:** TAPSE ≥17 mm, S′ >9.5 cm/s, FAC ≥35%, RV free wall strain >−20%. [^1_16][^1_18][^1_19][^1_21]
- **RV dilation:** RVEDA/LVEDA >0.6 (mild), >1 (severe); RV basal diameter >42 mm. [^1_24][^1_25][^1_21]
- **RV afterload:** PA AccT <100 ms, notched PA Doppler envelope, TR jet >2.8 m/s suggest ↑ PVR. [^1_26][^1_24]
- **RV-PA coupling:** TAPSE/PASP <0.30 indicates severe uncoupling (poor prognosis in PE, PH, ARDS). [^1_16][^1_21]
- **TEE views for RV:** ME 4-chamber (TAPSE, S′, FAC), ME RV inflow-outflow (TR, RVOT VTI), TG SAX (FAC, septal motion), TG RV inflow-outflow (PV Doppler). [^1_1][^1_11][^1_9][^1_10]
- **Pathology-specific patterns:** PE (RV/LV >1, McConnell sign), ARDS (septal dyskinesia, PA AccT <100 ms), PH (RV hypertrophy, TAPSE/PASP <0.30). [^1_26][^1_27][^1_21]
