## Therapeutic Plasma Exchange (PLEX/TPE) in ICU

**Exam-Style High-Yield Notes**

***

## Definition \& Mechanism

- **PLEX (Therapeutic Plasma Exchange / TPE)** = extracorporeal removal of patient plasma (containing pathogenic substances) with replacement by donor plasma or albumin.
- **Mechanisms:**
    - Removes: autoantibodies, immune complexes, cytokines, complement, toxins, abnormal proteins (e.g., IgM in macroglobulinemia), free hemoglobin, ultra-large vWF multimers.
    - Replaces: coagulation factors, albumin, ADAMTS13 (in TTP), etc., depending on replacement fluid.
- **Volume exchanged:** Typically **1–1.5 × total plasma volume (TPV)** per session; TPV ≈ 40 mL/kg × (1 − hematocrit). [^1_1][^1_2]

***

## Modalities of PLEX

| Modality | Principle | Pros | Cons | ICU Utilization |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Centrifugal PLEX** | Blood separated by density; plasma removed, cells returned | - Efficient for large-volume exchanges<br>- Good for cellular disorders (e.g., RBC exchange in SCD)<br>- Less anticoagulant needed | - Requires specialized apheresis machine<br>- More hemodynamic shifts<br>- Higher cost/complexity | TTP, SCD crisis, severe autoimmune with high Ig load |
| **Membrane (filtration) PLEX** | Plasma filtered across semipermeable membrane (like hemodialysis) | - Can be done on standard CRRT machines<br>- Better hemodynamic tolerance<br>- Widely available in ICU | - Lower clearance of very large molecules vs centrifugal<br>- Membrane clogging possible | Sepsis, ALF/ACLF, vasculitis, cytokine storm |
| **Selective / Immunoadsorption** | Specific removal of IgG/IgM via adsorption columns | - Highly selective<br>- No need for plasma replacement<br>- Less transfusion risk | - Limited availability<br>- Expensive<br>- Specific indications only | Refractory autoimmune, transplant rejection, some neuro-immune disorders |

> **In most ICUs:** Membrane PLEX via CRRT platform is common; centrifugal PLEX via apheresis unit for TTP, SCD, neuro indications.

***

## Indications in ICU (ASFA-Based Classification)

### 1. **Absolute (Category I) – First-line therapy**

- **Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP)**
    - Removes anti-ADAMTS13 antibodies; replaces ADAMTS13.
    - Daily PLEX until platelets normalize, hemolysis resolves.
- **Guillain–Barré Syndrome (GBS)**
    - Faster recovery, reduced ventilator days, improved ambulation. [^1_3][^1_2]
- **Myasthenic Crisis / Severe Myasthenia Gravis**
    - Pre-op optimization, severe bulbar/respiratory involvement. [^1_3]
- **Hyperviscosity Syndromes** (e.g., Waldenström macroglobulinemia with IgM spike)
- **Acute Liver Failure (ALF)** – High-volume PLEX as first-line or bridge to transplant.
    - Improves survival with native liver; low-volume PLEX also effective with fewer complications. [^1_4][^1_5]


### 2. **Relative (Category II) – Second-line / adjunctive**

- **ANCA-associated Vasculitis** with:
    - Diffuse alveolar hemorrhage (DAH)
    - Severe RPGN (creatinine ≥300 µmol/L or rapid decline)
    - PEXIVAS: no mortality benefit overall, but still considered in severe DAH/rapidly progressive disease. [^1_1][^1_6]
- **Anti-GBM Disease (Goodpasture’s)**
    - Especially with pulmonary hemorrhage or severe renal involvement.
- **Neuromyelitis Optica (NMO) / Severe Steroid-Refractory Demyelination**
    - Rescue for steroid-unresponsive relapses. [^1_7]
- **Catastrophic Antiphospholipid Syndrome (CAPS)**
- **Sickle Cell Disease (SCD) with Multiorgan Failure / Severe Crisis**
    - Grade 2B recommendation; may prevent need for ECMO in selected cases. [^1_8]
- **Acute-on-Chronic Liver Failure (ACLF)**
    - Meta-analysis: improved 30-day, 90-day, and 1-year survival vs standard medical therapy. [^1_9]


### 3. **Rescue / Experimental (Category III/IV)**

- **Septic Shock** (especially with multiorgan failure)
    - Some observational data suggest benefit; RCTs mixed.
    - ASFA: **not recommended** in children without TAMOF; uncertain in adults. [^1_10][^1_11]
- **Cytokine Storm Syndromes** (e.g., HLH, severe SIRS)
- **Refractory Cardiorespiratory Failure** post-arrest (case reports in SCD, etc.) [^1_8]

***

## Replacement Fluids

| Fluid | Composition | Indication |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP)** | Contains coagulation factors, ADAMTS13, complement | TTP, coagulopathy, ALF, bleeding risk |
| **5% Albumin + Saline** | No coagulation factors | Most autoimmune/neuro indications where factor replacement not needed |
| **Plasma + Albumin mix** | Tailored to need | Vasculitis, ALF, complex cases |

> **TTP:** Must use **FFP** (or cryo-poor plasma) to replace ADAMTS13.
> **ALF:** FFP preferred for factor replacement; low-volume PLEX reduces TRALI/TACO risk. [^1_4][^1_5]

***

## Dosing \& Regimen (ICU Practical)

- **Volume:** 1–1.5 × TPV per session
- **Frequency:**
    - **Daily** in TTP, severe DAH, anti-GBM until control
    - **Alternate daily** in GBS, myasthenia, vasculitis
- **Duration:**
    - TTP: until platelets >150k × 2 days, LDH down, no new schistocytes
    - GBS: 5 exchanges over 1–2 weeks
    - Myasthenia: 3–5 sessions
    - ALF/ACLF: protocol-dependent (often 3–5 sessions) [^1_4][^1_9]

***

## Contraindications \& Cautions

### Absolute / Strong Relative

- **Hemodynamic instability** (uncontrolled shock)
- **Active uncontrolled sepsis** (especially in ALF protocols) [^1_4][^1_5]
- **Severe coagulopathy with active bleeding** (unless FFP replacement used carefully)


### Relative

- Recent GI bleed
- Severe thrombocytopenia (<20k) without coverage
- Inability to secure adequate vascular access

> **Pregnancy:** NOT a contraindication (e.g., used in acute hepatitis A with ALF). [^1_4]

***

## Complications of PLEX

- **Vascular access-related:** infection, thrombosis, bleeding, inadvertent arterial puncture
- **Transfusion-related:**
    - TRALI (Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury)
    - TACO (Transfusion-Associated Circulatory Overload)
- **Metabolic:**
    - Citrate toxicity → hypocalcemia (arrhythmias, tetany)
    - Hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia
- **Immunologic:**
    - Removal of beneficial antibodies/immunoglobulins → infection risk
    - Allergic/anaphylactoid reactions to plasma
- **Hemodynamic:** hypotension during exchange

> **Monitoring:** Ca²⁺, K⁺, Mg²⁺, coagulation profile, hemodynamics, signs of TRALI/TACO. [^1_5][^1_1]

***

## Key Trials \& Evidence (High-Yield for Exams)

- **PEXIVAS (2020):** ANCA vasculitis with severe renal/pulmonary involvement
    - No significant reduction in death/ESKD with PLEX; increased serious infections at 1 year.
    - Still considered in **severe DAH, rapidly progressive renal failure** by KDIGO 2024. [^1_1][^1_6]
- **ALF/ACLF Meta-analyses (2023–2025):**
    - 30-day RR ~0.70, 90-day RR ~0.81, 1-year RR ~0.85 mortality reduction with PLEX. [^1_9]
- **GBS \& Myasthenia:**
    - PLEX = faster recovery, less ventilation, improved functional outcome vs IVIG/steroids alone in crisis. [^1_3][^1_2]

***

## Clinical Pearls (ICU Focus)

- **TTP + ICU:** If thrombocytopenia + MAHA + organ ischemia → start PLEX **immediately**; do not wait for ADAMTS13 level.
- **DAH + Vasculitis:** PLEX daily until bleeding stops; use FFP as replacement if coagulopathic.
- **ALF with INR ≥4 or encephalopathy:** Early PLEX improves native liver survival; low-volume PLEX safer and resource-sparing. [^1_4][^1_5]
- **Sepsis:** Avoid routine PLEX; consider only in trials or highly selected cytokine storm/TAMOF scenarios. [^1_10]
- **Citrate toxicity:** Watch for perioral tingling, QT prolongation; give IV calcium gluconate during PLEX.
