
## Tackling Arrhythmia in ICU: Critically Ill Patients

Arrhythmias in the ICU are common and often secondary to reversible critical illness factors. Management prioritizes hemodynamic stability, rapid identification of the rhythm, and aggressive correction of underlying triggers before rhythm-specific therapy. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3]

***

## 1. Assessment Framework: The "4H + 4T" Approach

### Initial Steps

1. **Confirm it's a real arrhythmia** (not artifact from movement, electrical interference, or poor leads). [^1_2]
2. **Assess hemodynamic stability immediately**:
    - Pulse, BP, perfusion (capillary refill, mental status)
    - Signs of myocardial ischemia (chest pain, ECG changes)
    - Heart failure (dyspnea, pulmonary edema)
    - Cerebral perfusion (confusion, reduced GCS) [^1_1][^1_4]
3. **Determine**: Is the patient compromised by the arrhythmia or the underlying condition? [^1_2]

### Reversible Causes (ICU-Specific)

Use the **"4H + 4T" mnemonic** adapted for ICU:


| Category | Specific ICU Triggers |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Hypoxia** | Respiratory failure, PE, pneumothorax, inadequate ventilation [^1_2][^1_5] |
| **Hydrogen (Acidosis)** | Metabolic acidosis (lactic, ketoacidosis), respiratory acidosis [^1_2] |
| **Homeostasis (Electrolytes)** | **K⁺ <3.5 or >5.5**, **Mg²⁺ <1.8**, Ca²⁺ abnormalities [^1_1][^1_3] |
| **Hypothermia** | Core temp <35°C (post-cardiac arrest, exposure) [^1_1] |
| **Toxins/Drugs** | Inotropes (dobutamine, norepinephrine), antiarrhythmics (QT-prolonging), digoxin, beta-blockers, CCBs, narcotics, antiemetics [^1_1][^1_6][^1_5] |
| **Tamponade** | Cardiac tamponade (post-procedure, trauma) [^1_2] |
| **Tension pneumothorax** | Post-central line, trauma, barotrauma [^1_2] |
| **Thrombosis** | ACS, PE, stroke [^1_2][^1_5] |

**Additional ICU-specific triggers**:

- Sepsis/SIRS [^1_1]
- Hypovolemia/hypervolemia [^1_2]
- Coronary ischemia [^1_2]
- Pulmonary artery catheter or central line irritation [^1_1][^1_2]
- Post-operative state (especially cardiothoracic surgery) [^1_7][^1_5]
- Alcohol withdrawal ("holiday heart") [^1_5]
- Thyrotoxicosis [^1_7]
- Raised intracranial pressure [^1_8]

***

## 2. Classification \& Types

### By Rate

- **Tachyarrhythmias**: HR >100 bpm
- **Bradyarrhythmias**: HR <60 bpm


### By Origin

- **Supraventricular**: Sinus tachycardia, AF, AFL, AVNRT, AVRT, MAT, JT
- **Ventricular**: PVCs, VT (monomorphic, polymorphic, torsades), VF


### By QRS Width

- **Narrow complex** (<120 ms): SVT (unless pre-existing BBB)
- **Wide complex** (≥120 ms): VT (until proven otherwise), SVT with BBB, pre-excited AF, hyperkalemia, drug toxicity [^1_3][^1_9]

***

## 3. Tachyarrhythmias: Diagnosis \& Management

### A. Narrow Complex Tachycardias (QRS <120 ms)

#### **Sinus Tachycardia**

- **Diagnosis**: Regular, P waves before each QRS, rate 100–150 bpm
- **ICU cause**: Pain, fever, hypovolemia, sepsis, anemia, PE, thyrotoxicosis, inotropes
- **Management**: Treat underlying cause (NOT beta-blockers in sepsis/hypovolemia) [^1_3]


#### **Atrial Fibrillation (AF) / Atrial Flutter (AFL)**

- **Diagnosis**:
    - AF: Irregularly irregular, no P waves, fibrillatory baseline
    - AFL: "Sawtooth" P waves (II, III, aVF), often 2:1 or 3:1 block → HR ~150 bpm
- **Common ICU triggers**: Sepsis, hypoxia, post-op, inotropes, electrolyte imbalance, PE, alcohol withdrawal [^1_7][^1_5]

**Management Algorithm**:


| Hemodynamic Status | Action |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Unstable** (hypotension, shock, ischemia, HF, altered mental status) | **Immediate synchronized cardioversion** (120–200J) → sedate if possible [^1_3][^1_7] |
| **Stable** | **Rate control preferred** in most ICU patients: <br>-  **Metoprolol**: 2.5–5 mg IV q6h (avoid in decompensated HF, asthma) <br>-  **Diltiazem**: 0.25 mg/kg IV bolus → 5–15 mg/hr infusion (avoid in HF, hypotension) <br>-  **Amiodarone**: 150 mg IV over 10 min → 1 mg/min × 6h → 0.5 mg/min (if beta-blocker/CCB contraindicated) <br>**Target HR**: 80–110 bpm (not <60) [^1_3][^1_10] |

**Rhythm control** (selected cases: new-onset <48h, young, no structural heart disease):

- Amiodarone 150 mg IV → 1 mg/min × 6h → 0.5 mg/min
- Consider cardioversion if fails

**Anticoagulation**:

- CHA₂DS₂-VASc score guides long-term need
- In acute ICU: assess bleeding risk (surgery, trauma, coagulopathy)
- If cardioversion planned and AF >48h or unknown duration → TEE to exclude LA thrombus or anticoagulate × 3 weeks

**Pearl**: In sepsis, prioritize source control, fluids, vasopressors, electrolyte correction BEFORE aggressive rate control. [^1_3]

#### **AVNRT / AVRT (PSVT)**

- **Diagnosis**: Regular, narrow complex, rate 150–250 bpm, P waves buried or retrograde
- **Management**:

1. **Vagal maneuvers**: Carotid massage (if no carotid disease), Valsalva
2. **Adenosine**: 6 mg rapid IV push → 12 mg if no response (diagnostic \& therapeutic) [^1_3]
3. **Beta-blocker or CCB** if adenosine fails
4. **Cardioversion** if unstable

***

### B. Wide Complex Tachycardias (QRS ≥120 ms)

**Golden Rule**: **Any wide-complex tachycardia in ICU = VT until proven otherwise.** AV nodal agents (adenosine, verapamil, diltiazem) can cause hemodynamic collapse if given to VT. [^1_3][^1_7]

#### **Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)**

- **Diagnosis**:
    - Regular, wide QRS (>120 ms), rate >100 bpm
    - AV dissociation, capture beats, fusion beats → specific for VT
    - Polymorphic VT (changing QRS axis) → think **torsades de pointes** (prolonged QT, low Mg²⁺)
- **Management**:

| Hemodynamic Status | Action |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Unstable** (hypotension, shock, ischemia, altered mental status) | **Immediate defibrillation** (200J, escalate) → CPR if pulseless [^1_3][^1_7] |
| **Stable monomorphic VT** | **Amiodarone**: 150 mg IV over 10 min → repeat once if needed <br>**Lidocaine**: 1–1.5 mg/kg IV bolus (alternative, especially in ischemia) <br>**Procainamide**: 15 mg/kg IV (avoid in HF, hypotension) [^1_3][^1_7] |
| **Polymorphic VT / Torsades** | **Magnesium sulfate**: 2 g IV over 1–2 min (even if Mg normal) <br>**Correct K⁺** to 4.0–4.5 mEq/L <br>**Stop QT-prolonging drugs** <br>Overdrive pacing or isoproterenol if bradycardia-dependent [^1_3] |

**Post-cardioversion/defibrillation**:

- Address reversible causes (ischemia, electrolytes, hypoxia)
- Consider amiodarone infusion (1 mg/min × 6h → 0.5 mg/min)
- Evaluate for ICD if recurrent/sustained VT [^1_11]


#### **Pre-excited AF (WPW)**

- **Diagnosis**: Irregular, wide complex, varying QRS morphology, very rapid (>200 bpm)
- **AVOID**: Adenosine, beta-blockers, CCBs, digoxin (can accelerate conduction via accessory pathway → VF)
- **Management**:
    - **Unstable**: Cardioversion
    - **Stable**: **Procainamide** 15 mg/kg IV or **Ibutilide** (if available)
    - **Amiodarone** controversial (may be safe in some cases, but avoid if pre-excitation obvious)

## 4. Bradyarrhythmias: Diagnosis \& Management

### Definition

HR <60 bpm; clinically significant if causing symptoms (hypotension, syncope, confusion, ischemia, HF). [^1_12][^1_8]

### Types \& ECG Features

| Type | ECG Features | ICU Causes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Sinus bradycardia** | Regular, P before QRS, rate <60 | Athletes, hypothermia, hypothyroidism, beta-blockers, CCBs, digoxin, narcotics, increased ICP, hypoxia [^1_1][^1_6][^1_12] |
| **Sinus arrest / pause** | Missing P-QRS-T, pause not multiple of P-P interval | Sinus node dysfunction, drugs, ischemia |
| **SA exit block** | Pause = multiple of P-P interval | Sinus node disease |
| **1st-degree AV block** | PR >200 ms, all P conducted | Drugs (beta-blockers, CCBs, digoxin), ischemia, Lyme disease [^1_8] |
| **2nd-degree AV block (Mobitz I / Wenckebach)** | Progressive PR lengthening → dropped QRS | AV nodal disease, drugs, inferior MI, increased vagal tone |
| **2nd-degree AV block (Mobitz II)** | Fixed PR, intermittent non-conducted P | Infra-nodal disease, anterior MI → high risk of progression to complete block |
| **3rd-degree (complete) AV block** | No P-QRS relationship, escape rhythm (narrow if junctional, wide if ventricular) | Ischemia, fibrosis, drugs, post-op, Lyme, hypothermia, hyperkalemia [^1_1][^1_12][^1_8] |

### Assessment

1. **Hemodynamic stability**: Hypotension, shock, ischemia, HF, altered mental status
2. **12-lead ECG**: Identify type, QRS width of escape rhythm
3. **Reversible causes**:
    - Drugs (beta-blockers, CCBs, digoxin, amiodarone, metaraminol) [^1_1][^1_6]
    - Hyperkalemia (K⁺ >6.5, peaked T, wide QRS, sine wave) [^1_8]
    - Hypothermia [^1_1]
    - Hypothyroidism [^1_1]
    - Ischemia (especially inferior or anterior MI) [^1_12][^1_8]
    - Increased ICP (Cushing reflex) [^1_8]
    - Lyme carditis [^1_8]

### Management Algorithm

**Step 1: Unstable (hypotension, shock, ischemia, HF, altered mental status)**

- **Atropine**: 0.5 mg IV q3–5 min (max 3 mg) [^1_12]
- **If no response**:
    - **Transcutaneous pacing** (immediate, until transvenous)
    - **Transvenous pacing** (definitive temporary)
    - **Vasopressors**:
        - **Epinephrine**: 2–10 mcg/min infusion
        - **Dopamine**: 5–20 mcg/kg/min infusion [^1_12]
- **Special cases**:
    - **Hyperkalemia**: Calcium gluconate 10% (10 mL IV), insulin + dextrose, sodium bicarbonate, dialysis
    - **Beta-blocker/CCB overdose**: Calcium, glucagon, high-dose insulin, lipid emulsion [^1_8]
    - **Digoxin toxicity**: Digoxin-specific antibody (Fab)

**Step 2: Stable**

- **Treat reversible causes** (stop offending drugs, correct K⁺/Mg²⁺, rewarm, treat ischemia)
- **Observation** if asymptomatic sinus bradycardia (e.g., athletes, sleep)
- **Temporary pacing** if high-grade block (Mobitz II, complete heart block) even if stable (risk of progression) [^1_12]

**Temporary Pacemaker Settings**:

- **Rate**: 80 beats/min
- **Current**: Start at 0 mA, increase until capture (usually 0.5–1.5 mA)
- **Mode**: VVI or DDD (depending on need) [^1_12]

## 5. Device Therapy: TPI, PPI, AICD, CRT-D Decision Making

### Terminology

- **TPI**: Temporary Pacemaker Insertion (transvenous or transcutaneous)
- **PPI**: Permanent Pacemaker Insertion
- **AICD**: Automatic Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator
- **CRT-D**: Cardiac Resynchronization Therapy with Defibrillator


### Indications in ICU Context

#### **Temporary Pacemaker (TPI)**

**Indications**:

- Hemodynamically significant bradycardia unresponsive to atropine/vasopressors [^1_12]
- High-grade AV block (Mobitz II, complete heart block) awaiting permanent device
- Post-cardiac surgery bradycardia (often transient)
- Drug toxicity (beta-blocker, CCB, digoxin) while awaiting reversal
- Bridge to PPI or AICD

**Contraindications**:

- Reversible cause likely to resolve within 24–48h (e.g., hypothermia, transient ischemia)
- Active infection (relative)


#### **Permanent Pacemaker (PPI)**

**Indications** (based on ACC/AHA/HRS guidelines):

- **Symptomatic bradycardia** (sinus node dysfunction) not due to reversible causes
- **High-grade AV block** (Mobitz II, 2:1 block, complete heart block) regardless of symptoms
- **Post-MI persistent AV block** (beyond 2 weeks)
- **Neurocardiogenic syncope** with documented bradycardia
- **Tachy-brady syndrome** requiring beta-blocker/CCB

**Timing in ICU**:

- Stabilize acute phase (treat ischemia, correct electrolytes, stop drugs)
- If bradycardia persists >48–72h without reversible cause → refer for PPI
- **Do not implant** during active sepsis, uncontrolled infection, or coagulopathy


#### **AICD (Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator)**

**Indications**:

- **Secondary prevention**: Survivors of cardiac arrest due to VT/VF (after reversible causes excluded) [^1_11]
- **Primary prevention**:
    - LVEF ≤35% (ischemic or non-ischemic cardiomyopathy) despite ≥3 months optimal medical therapy
    - NYHA class II–III heart failure
    - Specific genetic conditions (Long QT, Brugada, HCM with risk factors)

**ICU Decision Points**:

- **Post-cardiac arrest**: If VT/VF due to reversible cause (hypokalemia, acute ischemia, drug toxicity) → **do not implant AICD** until cause corrected and reassessed
- **Post-MI**: Wait ≥40 days post-MI, ≥3 months post-PCI/CABG before considering AICD (unless sustained VT/VF occurs after this period)
- **Bridge**: Use wearable defibrillator (LifeVest) if uncertain about recovery


#### **CRT-D (Cardiac Resynchronization Therapy with Defibrillator)**

**Indications**:

- LVEF ≤35%
- NYHA class II–IV on optimal medical therapy
- **LBBB with QRS ≥150 ms** (strongest indication)
- Non-LBBB with QRS ≥150 ms (weaker evidence)

**ICU Considerations**:

- Optimize HF therapy first (beta-blockers, ACEi/ARB/ARNI, MRA, SGLT2i)
- If acute decompensated HF with bradycardia or high-grade block → may need temporary pacing → reassess for CRT-D after stabilization
- **Do not implant** during active infection, sepsis, or uncontrolled comorbidities

***

### Decision Tree: Device Selection in ICU

| Clinical Scenario | TPI | PPI | AICD | CRT-D |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Symptomatic bradycardia, reversible cause (drugs, hypothermia, ischemia)** | ✓ (bridge) | Wait 48–72h, reassess | ✗ | ✗ |
| **Complete heart block post-anterior MI** | ✓ | ✓ (if persists >2 weeks) | ✗ (unless VT/VF) | ✗ |
| **Cardiac arrest due to VT/VF (reversible cause excluded)** | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ (secondary prevention) | If LVEF ≤35% + LBBB |
| **LVEF 25%, LBBB, QRS 160 ms, NYHA III** | If bradycardic | ✗ | ✓ (if LVEF ≤35%) | ✓ (CRT-D preferred) |
| **Post-cardiac surgery bradycardia** | ✓ (often transient) | Wait 1–2 weeks | ✗ | ✗ |
| **Digoxin/beta-blocker toxicity with complete block** | ✓ (until reversal) | ✗ (if recovers) | ✗ | ✗ |

## 6. Key Clinical Pearls \& Exam Hacks

### Diagnostic Pearls

1. **Wide complex = VT until proven otherwise** in ICU. Never give adenosine/verapamil to wide-complex tachycardia unless certain of SVT with BBB. [^1_3][^1_7]
2. **"Adenosine test"**: Use adenosine diagnostically in regular narrow-complex tachycardia → transient block reveals underlying atrial activity (AF, AFL, atrial tachycardia). [^1_3]
3. **Check electrolytes aggressively**: Target **K⁺ 4.0–4.5 mEq/L**, **Mg²⁺ >2.0 mg/dL** in all arrhythmias. [^1_3]
4. **Torsades de pointes**: Think prolonged QT + low Mg²⁺ → give **MgSO₄ 2 g IV** even if Mg normal. [^1_3]

### Management Pearls

1. **Treat the patient, not the ECG**: In sepsis, prioritize source control, fluids, vasopressors before antiarrhythmics. [^1_3][^1_5]
2. **Rate vs. rhythm in AF**: Rate control preferred in most ICU patients (especially sepsis, post-op, elderly). Rhythm control only in selected young, new-onset, no structural heart disease. [^1_3][^1_10]
3. **Target HR in AF**: 80–110 bpm (not <60) to maintain cardiac output in critically ill. [^1_3]
4. **Atropine max dose**: 3 mg (0.5 mg q3–5 min). Higher doses paradoxically worsen bradycardia. [^1_12]
5. **Temporary pacing**: Set rate to 80 bpm, increase current until capture (usually 0.5–1.5 mA). [^1_12]

### Device Therapy Pearls

1. **TPI**: Bridge for unstable bradycardia or high-grade block while awaiting PPI or recovery.
2. **PPI**: Do not implant during active sepsis, infection, or uncontrolled comorbidities.
3. **AICD**: Wait ≥40 days post-MI, ≥3 months post-PCI/CABG before considering (unless sustained VT/VF occurs after this period).
4. **CRT-D**: Indicated for LVEF ≤35%, NYHA II–IV, LBBB with QRS ≥150 ms on optimal medical therapy.

## 7. Landmark Trials \& Guidelines

| Trial/Guideline | Key Finding |
| :-- | :-- |
| **ACLS Guidelines (AHA 2020)** | Unstable tachyarrhythmia → immediate cardioversion; unstable bradycardia → atropine → pacing [^1_7][^1_12] |
| **AFFIRM Trial** | Rate control non-inferior to rhythm control in AF (mortality benefit with rate control) |
| **MADIT-II, SCD-HeFT** | AICD reduces mortality in LVEF ≤35% (ischemic and non-ischemic cardiomyopathy) |
| **COMPANION, CARE-HF** | CRT-D reduces mortality and HF hospitalizations in LVEF ≤35%, LBBB, QRS ≥150 ms |
| **ESC Guidelines on Arrhythmias (2022)** | Updated recommendations on device therapy, anticoagulation in AF, VT management |

## 8. Scoring Systems

### CHA₂DS₂-VASc (AF Stroke Risk)

- **C**HF: 1
- **H**ypertension: 1
- **A**ge ≥75: 2
- **D**iabetes: 1
- **S**troke/TIA: 2
- **V**ascular disease: 1
- **A**ge 65–74: 1
- **S**ex (female): 1

**Score ≥2 (men) or ≥3 (women)** → anticoagulation recommended.

### HAS-BLED (AF Bleeding Risk)

- **H**ypertension: 1
- **A**bnormal renal/liver function: 1 each
- **S**troke: 1
- **B**leeding history: 1
- **L**abile INR: 1
- **E**lderly (>65): 1
- **D**rugs/alcohol: 1 each

**Score ≥3** → high bleeding risk (correct modifiable factors, not necessarily withhold anticoagulation).

## 9. Complications \& Prognosis

### Complications of Arrhythmias in ICU

- Hemodynamic collapse, shock
- Myocardial ischemia/infarction
- Heart failure exacerbation
- Stroke (AF-related embolism)
- Sudden cardiac death (VT/VF)


### Prognosis

- **New-onset AF in ICU**: Associated with increased mortality, longer ICU stay, higher risk of stroke [^1_5][^1_10]
- **VT/VF**: High mortality if not promptly treated; survivors need evaluation for ICD
- **Bradyarrhythmias**: Prognosis depends on underlying cause (reversible vs. structural heart disease)
