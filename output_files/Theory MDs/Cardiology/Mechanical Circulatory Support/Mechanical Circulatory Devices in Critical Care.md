

## Mechanical Circulatory Devices in Critical Care

Mechanical circulatory support (MCS) devices are life-saving interventions used when the heart fails to maintain adequate circulation despite maximal medical therapy. They range from temporary percutaneous devices (IABP, Impella, TandemHeart, VA-ECMO) to durable implantable pumps (LVAD, RVAD, BiVAD), each with distinct indications, contraindications, and hemodynamic profiles. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3][^1_4]

***

## 1. Definition and Classification

### 1.1 Definition

Mechanical circulatory support refers to devices that partially or completely replace the pumping function of the heart, maintaining systemic or pulmonary circulation in patients with cardiogenic shock or advanced heart failure. [^1_5][^1_6]

### 1.2 Classification by Duration and Invasiveness

| Category | Devices | Duration | Insertion | Typical Use |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Temporary MCS (tMCS)** | IABP, Impella (2.5/CP/5.0/5.5), TandemHeart, VA-ECMO, CentriMag | Hours to weeks | Percutaneous or surgical | Cardiogenic shock, bridge to recovery/decision |
| **Durable MCS (dMCS)** | HeartMate II/3, HeartWare HVAD, SynCardia TAH | Months to years | Surgical implantation | Bridge to transplant, destination therapy, bridge to candidacy |
| **Extracorporeal Life Support** | VA-ECMO, VV-ECMO | Days to weeks | Large-bore cannulation | Cardiorespiratory failure, cardiac arrest (E-CPR) |

[^1_1][^1_7][^1_3][^1_8]

## 2. Indications

### 2.1 General Indications for MCS

MCS is indicated when patients demonstrate **refractory cardiogenic shock** or **advanced heart failure** despite optimal medical management (OMM), defined by:

- Cardiac index <1.8–2.2 L/min/m²
- Systolic blood pressure <90 mm Hg
- Pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) >20 mm Hg
- Evidence of hypoperfusion: oliguria, rising creatinine, elevated lactate, altered mental status, cool extremities [^1_5][^1_6]


### 2.2 Indications by Clinical Scenario

#### Temporary MCS (tMCS)

1. **Acute cardiogenic shock** (AMI, myocarditis, drug toxicity, peripartum cardiomyopathy)
2. **Post-cardiotomy shock** (failure to wean from cardiopulmonary bypass)
3. **Refractory heart failure** (acute decompensation or acute-on-chronic)
4. **High-risk PCI or structural interventions** (prophylactic support)
5. **Refractory ventricular arrhythmias** (electrical storm ablation support)
6. **Bridge to durable MCS or transplantation**
7. **ECPR** (extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation) [^1_1][^1_9][^1_10][^1_7]

#### Durable MCS (dMCS) — FDA-Approved Indications

1. **Bridge to Recovery (BTR)**: Acute cardiogenic shock with expected myocardial recovery
2. **Bridge to Transplantation (BTT)**: End-stage HF awaiting transplant
3. **Destination Therapy (DT)**: Permanent support in transplant-ineligible patients
4. **Bridge to Candidacy (BTC)**: Support to improve eligibility for transplant (e.g., organ recovery, weight loss, rehabilitation) [^1_5][^1_11][^1_12][^1_13]

### 2.3 Specific Clinical Indications for Durable MCS

Patients with **Stage D (advanced) heart failure** and any of the following:

- NYHA class IIIB–IV despite maximal guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT)
- Inotrope dependence (continuous IV milrinone/dobutamine)
- Frequent HF hospitalizations (≥2 in 12 months)
- Intolerance to neurohormonal antagonists (beta-blockers, ACEi/ARB/ARNI, MRA)
- Peak VO₂ <14–16 mL/kg/min
- End-organ dysfunction from low cardiac output (renal, hepatic)
- LVEF ≤25% with progressive remodeling
- Recurrent ventricular arrhythmias/ICD shocks
- Symptomatic hypotension (SBP ≤90 mm Hg) [^1_14][^1_11][^1_12][^1_13]

## 3. Contraindications

### 3.1 Absolute Contraindications (All MCS Devices)

- **Severe aortic regurgitation** (especially for IABP, Impella, TandemHeart)
- **Aortic dissection or aneurysm**
- **Irreversible end-organ failure** (hepatic, renal, neurological)
- **Active systemic infection or sepsis**
- **Disseminated malignancy with poor prognosis**
- **Medical nonadherence or inability to operate device**
- **Severe psychiatric disorder or lack of social support**
- **Contraindication to anticoagulation** (for most devices except IABP) [^1_1][^1_14][^1_2][^1_15][^1_9][^1_7]


### 3.2 Device-Specific Contraindications

| Device | Absolute Contraindications | Relative Contraindications |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **IABP** | Severe aortic regurgitation, aortic dissection, abdominal aortic aneurysm | Severe peripheral vascular disease (PVD), sepsis, contraindication to anticoagulation |
| **Impella** | LV thrombus, mechanical aortic valve, moderate–severe aortic stenosis/regurgitation, recent TIA/stroke, aortic abnormalities | Severe PVD, contraindication to anticoagulation |
| **TandemHeart** | Ventricular septal defect (VSD), moderate–severe aortic insufficiency, contraindication to anticoagulation | Severe tricuspid/pulmonic stenosis, mechanical right-sided valves, IVC/RA thrombi |
| **VA-ECMO** | Candidates not eligible for transplant/VAD, prolonged CPR without perfusion, unwitnessed cardiac arrest, end-stage organ dysfunction | Advanced age, obesity, contraindication to anticoagulation, mechanical ventilation >7 days |
| **Durable LVAD** | Irreversible hepatic/renal/neurological disease, severe PVD, active substance abuse, unmanaged psychiatric disorder, lack of caregiver support | Age >80 years (for DT), obesity/malnutrition, untreated malignancy, severe COPD |

[^1_1][^1_14][^1_2][^1_15][^1_9][^1_16][^1_11][^1_7]

## 4. Timing of MCS Initiation

### 4.1 General Principles

- **Early initiation** before irreversible end-organ damage improves outcomes. [^1_1][^1_6]
- In **acute MI with cardiogenic shock (AMI-CS)**: Insert MCS **as soon as possible** if initial resuscitation (fluids, inotropes, vasopressors) fails, preferably **before PCI**. [^1_1][^1_6][^1_10]
- For **prophylactic high-risk PCI**: Place device **before starting the procedure**. [^1_1][^1_10]
- **SCAI Shock Stages** guide escalation:
    - **Stage A (At risk)**: No MCS needed
    - **Stage B (Beginning)**: Consider IABP or low-flow support
    - **Stage C (Classic)**: pVAD (Impella, TandemHeart) preferred over IABP
    - **Stage D (Deteriorating)**: Escalate to high-flow pVAD or VA-ECMO; consider combination therapy
    - **Stage E (Extremis)**: VA-ECMO or ECPR as rescue; often combined with LV unloading [^1_17][^1_8][^1_18]


### 4.2 INTERMACS Profiles for Durable MCS Timing

| Profile | Clinical Status | Timing |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **1** | Critical cardiogenic shock (hours of life) | Emergency implant |
| **2** | Progressive decline on inotropes (days) | Urgent implant |
| **3** | Stable on inotropes (weeks) | Elective implant |
| **4–7** | Ambulatory, worsening symptoms | Planned implant |

[^1_15][^1_11][^1_13]

## 5. Device Selection

### 5.1 Hemodynamic Goals and Device Matching

| Device | Mechanism | Max Flow (L/min) | LV Unloading | RV Support | Oxygenation | Anticoagulation |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **IABP** | Counterpulsation (diastolic augmentation) | 0.5–1.0 | Mild (pressure) | No | No | Optional |
| **Impella 2.5/CP/5.0/5.5** | Axial flow (LV → aorta) | 2.5–5.5 | Strong (volume + pressure) | No (except Impella RP for RV) | No | Required |
| **TandemHeart** | Centrifugal (LA → femoral artery) | 4.0–5.0 | Strong (volume) | No | No | Required |
| **VA-ECMO** | Centrifugal (venous → arterial) | 4.0–10.0 | None (may increase LV afterload) | Yes | Yes | Required |
| **Durable LVAD** | Continuous/intermittent flow (LV → aorta) | 4.0–10.0 | Complete | No (unless BiVAD) | No | Required |
| **RVAD / BiVAD** | RV or biventricular support | 4.0–10.0 | LVAD: yes; RVAD: no | Yes | No | Required |

[^1_1][^1_7][^1_3][^1_8]

### 5.2 Algorithm for Device Selection

#### Step 1: Assess Shock Phenotype

- **Left ventricular failure** → IABP, Impella, TandemHeart, LVAD
- **Right ventricular failure** → Impella RP, Protek Duo, RVAD
- **Biventricular failure** → VA-ECMO, BiVAD, TAH
- **Respiratory failure ± cardiac** → VV-ECMO (respiratory only) or VA-ECMO (cardiorespiratory) [^1_1][^1_2][^1_9][^1_8]


#### Step 2: Assess Severity (SCAI Stage)

- **Stage B**: IABP or low-flow Impella 2.5/CP
- **Stage C**: Impella CP/5.0 or TandemHeart
- **Stage D–E**: VA-ECMO, high-flow Impella 5.5, TandemHeart, or combination (e.g., ECMO + Impella for LV unloading) [^1_17][^1_8][^1_18]


#### Step 3: Assess Duration and Goal

- **Bridge to recovery (<2 weeks)**: tMCS (IABP, Impella, ECMO)
- **Bridge to transplant/candidacy (weeks–months)**: Durable LVAD
- **Destination therapy (indefinite)**: Durable LVAD [^1_5][^1_19][^1_11][^1_12]


#### Step 4: Assess Contraindications and Anatomy

- **Aortic valve disease** → Avoid IABP/Impella if severe AR/AS or mechanical valve
- **PVD** → Avoid large-bore femoral devices; consider axillary/subclavian access or surgical LVAD
- **LV thrombus** → Avoid transvalvular devices (Impella, TandemHeart) [^1_1][^1_2][^1_16][^1_7]


### 5.3 Special Scenarios

| Scenario | Preferred Device(s) | Rationale |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **AMI-CS undergoing PCI** | Impella CP/5.0 or IABP (early stage) | LV unloading during PCI; Impella superior hemodynamics |
| **Post-cardiotomy shock** | VA-ECMO, CentriMag, TandemHeart | Biventricular support; rapid deployment |
| **RV failure (e.g., post-LVAD, massive PE)** | Impella RP, Protek Duo, RVAD, VA-ECMO | Direct RV support or bypass |
| **Refractory arrhythmia ablation** | Impella or TandemHeart | Hemodynamic stability during prolonged procedure |
| **ECPR** | VA-ECMO | Full cardiopulmonary support during arrest |
| **Bridge to transplant in end-stage HF** | Durable LVAD (HeartMate 3) | Long-term support, improved survival vs medical therapy |
| **Biventricular failure** | BiVAD or TAH | Complete cardiac replacement |

[^1_1][^1_9][^1_7][^1_20][^1_8]

## 6. Complications of MCS

| Device | Common Complications |
| :-- | :-- |
| **IABP** | Thrombocytopenia, limb ischemia, aortic dissection/rupture, infection, malposition, plaque embolism |
| **Impella** | Hemolysis, pump migration, aortic valve injury, LV perforation, arrhythmias, bleeding, vascular injury |
| **TandemHeart** | Tamponade (atrial perforation), thromboembolism, air embolism, interatrial shunt, bleeding |
| **VA-ECMO** | LV distension, upper body hypoxia (Harlequin syndrome), thrombosis, bleeding, stroke, limb ischemia, infection |
| **Durable LVAD** | Bleeding (GI, intracranial), stroke, pump thrombosis, infection (driveline), right heart failure, aortic insufficiency |

[^1_1][^1_7][^1_21][^1_8]

## 7. Landmark Trials and Guidelines

### 7.1 Key Trials

- **IABP-SHOCK II (2012)**: No mortality benefit of IABP in AMI-CS; remains used for hemodynamic support. [^1_21]
- **IMPRESS in Severe Shock (2017)**: Impella vs IABP in AMI-CS; no 30-day mortality difference but improved hemodynamics. [^1_21]
- **DanGer Shock (2024)**: Impella in AMI-CS showed long-term survival benefit vs conventional therapy. [^1_22]
- **ECMO vs pVAD meta-analyses**: ECMO associated with higher 30-day survival vs IABP (NNT 13), no difference vs pVAD. [^1_21]


### 7.2 Guideline Recommendations

- **2023 ISHLT Guidelines**: Durable MCS for NYHA IIIB–IV refractory to GDMT, inotrope-dependent, or on tMCS. [^1_11]
- **SCAI/ACC/HFSA/STS Consensus (2015, 2022)**: Graded escalation of pMCS based on SCAI shock stage; early insertion in AMI-CS before PCI. [^1_10][^1_17]
- **AHA Scientific Statement (2017)**: Durable MCS for advanced HF with frequent hospitalizations, low VO₂, end-organ dysfunction. [^1_12]

***

## 8. Clinical Pearls for Examinations

1. **IABP is contraindicated in severe aortic regurgitation and dissection** — it worsens AR by diastolic augmentation. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_9]
2. **Impella requires an intact aortic valve** — avoid in mechanical aortic valve, severe AS/AR, or LV thrombus. [^1_2][^1_16][^1_7]
3. **VA-ECMO does not unload the LV** — may cause LV distension; consider adding Impella ("ECMella") for LV unloading. [^1_21][^1_8]
4. **Early MCS in AMI-CS improves outcomes** — insert before PCI if shock persists after initial resuscitation. [^1_1][^1_6][^1_10]
5. **Durable MCS indications**: NYHA IIIB–IV, inotrope dependence, frequent hospitalizations, VO₂ <14, end-organ dysfunction. [^1_14][^1_11][^1_12][^1_13]
6. **Absolute contraindications for all MCS**: Irreversible organ failure, sepsis, severe AR, aortic dissection, nonadherence. [^1_14][^1_2][^1_15][^1_7]
7. **SCAI shock staging guides device escalation**: IABP (B) → Impella/TandemHeart (C) → ECMO/combination (D–E). [^1_17][^1_8][^1_18]

## 9. Summary Table: Device Selection by Clinical Need

| Clinical Need | First-Line Device | Alternatives | Avoid If |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Mild LV shock (SCAI B)** | IABP | Impella 2.5/CP | Severe AR, dissection |
| **Moderate–severe LV shock (SCAI C)** | Impella CP/5.0 | TandemHeart | LV thrombus, mechanical AV, severe AS/AR |
| **Biventricular shock / respiratory failure** | VA-ECMO | BiVAD, TAH | Non-transplant candidate, prolonged CPR |
| **RV failure** | Impella RP, Protek Duo | RVAD, VA-ECMO | Mechanical tricuspid/pulmonic valve |
| **Bridge to transplant (chronic)** | Durable LVAD (HeartMate 3) | BiVAD, TAH | Irreversible organ failure, severe PVD |
| **Destination therapy** | Durable LVAD | — | Same as above + age >80 (relative) |
