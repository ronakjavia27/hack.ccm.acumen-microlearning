
# Biomarkers of Sepsis: Old and New

Sepsis biomarkers are measurable biological indicators used for early diagnosis, prognosis, risk stratification, and therapeutic guidance in patients with dysregulated host responses to infection; no single biomarker satisfies all clinical needs, and a multi-biomarker approach integrated with clinical judgment remains the best strategy [^1_1][^1_2].

***

## 1. Definition and Ideal Properties

A sepsis biomarker is a objectively measured characteristic that reflects the presence, severity, or progression of sepsis and the host's dysregulated response to infection [^1_3].

**Ideal properties of a sepsis biomarker include:**

- High sensitivity and specificity for infection vs. non-infectious inflammation
- Rapid turnaround time (ideally <1 hour) with point-of-care availability
- Ability to differentiate bacterial, viral, and fungal sepsis
- Correlation with disease severity and organ dysfunction
- Ability to guide antibiotic initiation, de-escalation, and discontinuation
- Prognostic value for mortality and organ failure prediction
- Stability in plasma/serum with standardized assays
- Low cost and widespread availability
- Minimal influence by comorbidities (renal failure, trauma, surgery, immunosuppression) [^1_1][^1_4]

***

## 2. Classification of Sepsis Biomarkers

Sepsis biomarkers can be classified by their pathophysiological origin and clinical maturity [^1_2][^1_3]:


| **Category** | **Examples** | **Pathophysiological Basis** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Acute-phase proteins | CRP, PCT | Hepatic synthesis driven by IL-6/TNF-α |
| Cytokines/chemokines | IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α, IL-27 | Pro-/anti-inflammatory mediators |
| Cell-surface/receptor markers | Neutrophil CD64, mHLA-DR, sTREM-1 | Immune cell activation/suppression |
| Endothelial/vascular markers | MR-proADM, Angiopoietin-2, sVCAM-1 | Endothelial barrier dysfunction |
| Coagulation markers | D-dimer, platelet count, fibrinogen, TAT complex | Coagulopathy/DIC pathway |
| Metabolic markers | Lactate, LDH, CK-MB, free fatty acids, TyG index | Tissue hypoperfusion, mitochondrial dysfunction |
| Organ dysfunction markers | Cystatin C, troponin, bilirubin | End-organ injury |
| DAMPs/PAMPs | cfDNA, HMGB-1, histones | Cell damage and pathogen recognition |
| Pattern recognition molecules | CRP, Pentraxin-3, LBP, Presepsin (sCD14-ST) | Innate immune activation |
| Non-coding RNAs | miRNAs (miR-486-5p), lncRNAs (HOTTIP), circRNAs | Post-transcriptional gene regulation |
| Gene expression signatures | Sepsis endotype panels (e.g., 12-gene, 5-gene panels) | Transcriptomic immune phenotyping |
| Cell counts/derived indices | NLR, PLR, SII | Systemic inflammation quantification |

## 3. Old/Established Biomarkers

### 3.1 C-Reactive Protein (CRP)

- **Source:** Liver (hepatocytes), induced by IL-6, IL-1, and TNF-α [^1_1]
- **Mechanism:** Binds phosphocholine on microbial membranes and dead/dying cells, activating complement and promoting opsonization/phagocytosis [^1_1]
- **Kinetics:** Rises within 6–12 hours of infection; peaks at 24–48 hours; half-life ~19 hours [^1_2]
- **Diagnostic performance:** High sensitivity but low specificity; cannot reliably distinguish bacterial from viral or non-infectious inflammation [^1_2][^1_1]
- **Strengths:** Inexpensive, widely available, rapid, useful for trend monitoring [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Limitations:** Elevated in trauma, surgery, burns, pancreatitis, malignancy, autoimmune flares; poor discriminator of sepsis vs. SIRS [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Clinical use:** General inflammatory screening; serial trends more useful than single values; combined with PCT improves diagnostic performance [^1_1]


### 3.2 Procalcitonin (PCT)

- **Source:** Normally produced by thyroid C-cells; in sepsis, ubiquitously produced by extra-thyroid tissues (liver, lung, kidney, adipose) in response to endotoxin and IL-1β/TNF-α/IL-6 [^1_1]
- **Mechanism:** PCT is the 116-amino-acid precursor of calcitonin; in bacterial infection, PCT is released systemically and is not degraded to calcitonin; its production is suppressed by IFN-γ (viral infections), giving it relative bacterial specificity [^1_1]
- **Kinetics:** Rises within 2–4 hours of bacterial infection; peaks at 6–24 hours; half-life ~22–26 hours [^1_2]
- **Diagnostic performance:** More specific for bacterial infection than CRP; AUC ~0.77–0.84 for sepsis diagnosis; more valuable for ruling out bacterial sepsis than for confirming it [^1_2][^1_1]
- **Strengths:** Guides antibiotic stewardship; PCT-guided algorithms reduce antibiotic duration (PRORATA trial); correlates with infection severity; useful for de-escalation decisions [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Limitations:**
    - False positives: major surgery, severe trauma, burns, cardiogenic shock, pancreatitis, prolonged severe shock, malignancies (medullary thyroid carcinoma, small cell lung cancer)
    - False negatives: early infection (<2 hours), localized infections without bacteremia, immunosuppressed patients
    - Elevated in neonates (first 48 hours of life) and in renal failure (reduced clearance)
    - Less reliable for non-bacterial (fungal, intracellular) pathogens [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Key trials:** PRORATA trial (PCT-guided antibiotic discontinuation reduced antibiotic days without adverse outcomes); SCHOC trial; Cochrane meta-analyses [^1_1]


### 3.3 Lactate

- **Source:** Product of anaerobic glycolysis; elevated in tissue hypoperfusion, mitochondrial dysfunction, and accelerated aerobic glycolysis (β-adrenergic-driven) [^1_2]
- **Mechanism:** In sepsis, lactate elevation reflects both impaired oxygen delivery (Type A) and impaired mitochondrial oxygen utilization (Type B); β2-adrenergic stimulation by endogenous catecholamines drives aerobic glycolysis in skeletal muscle independent of hypoxia [^1_2]
- **Diagnostic/prognostic performance:**
    - Lactate ≥4 mmol/L = severe hyperlactatemia, strongly associated with circulatory failure and mortality [^1_2]
    - Adjusted HR 1.32 per 1 mmol/L increase (95% CI 1.15–1.52) for 28-day mortality [^1_2]
    - Persistent hyperlactatemia (>4 mmol/L at 6 hours post-resuscitation): OR 3.45 for hospital mortality (95% CI 2.11–5.63) [^1_2]
    - Lactate clearance <10% at 6 hours combined with SOFA ≥9 predicts 90-day mortality with 78% sensitivity (AUC 0.81) [^1_2]
    - Early lactate normalization (<2 mmol/L within 24 hours): 58% lower 28-day mortality risk (NNT = 4) [^1_2]
- **Clinical use:** Incorporated into SSC guidelines; serial measurements at 0, 3, 6 hours recommended; lactate-guided resuscitation is non-inferior to ScvO2-guided approaches [^1_2]
- **Limitations:** Elevated in non-septic conditions (seizures, malignancy, liver failure, metformin toxicity, regional ischemia); does not distinguish sepsis from other causes of tissue hypoperfusion [^1_2]


### 3.4 Interleukin-6 (IL-6)

- **Source:** Macrophages, T-cells, endothelial cells, fibroblasts in response to infection/tissue injury [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Mechanism:** Pleiotropic pro-inflammatory cytokine; key driver of acute-phase response; stimulates hepatic CRP synthesis; orchestrates cytokine cascade and B-cell differentiation [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Kinetics:** Rises within 1–2 hours (earliest cytokine marker); peaks rapidly; short half-life (~minutes to hours) [^1_2]
- **Diagnostic performance:** High sensitivity for early sepsis; moderate specificity; sustained elevation independently predicts 28-day mortality (adjusted OR 2.15, 95% CI 1.34–3.42) [^1_2]
- **Strengths:** Earliest rising biomarker; reflects cytokine storm severity; correlates with organ failure [^1_2]
- **Limitations:** Short half-life limits clinical utility; elevated in non-septic conditions (trauma, surgery, burns, pancreatitis); expensive; variable assay standardization; not widely available as POC [^1_2][^1_1]


### 3.5 Interleukin-10 (IL-10) and TNF-α

- **IL-10:** Anti-inflammatory cytokine produced by regulatory T-cells and monocytes; paradoxical role — early elevation may indicate compensatory response, but sustained levels predict immunosuppression, CARS (compensatory anti-inflammatory response syndrome), and secondary infections [^1_2]
- **TNF-α:** Prototypic pro-inflammatory cytokine produced by macrophages and NK cells; rises within 1–2 hours; drives endothelial activation, capillary leak, and cytokine cascade; transient release limits utility in late sepsis [^1_2]
- **Cytokine ratios:** IL-6/IL-10 ratio and SII (systemic immune-inflammation index = [neutrophils × platelets]/lymphocytes) serve as superior prognostic indicators compared to individual cytokines [^1_2]


### 3.6 White Blood Cell Count and Derived Indices

- **WBC count:** Part of traditional SIRS criteria; nonspecific; affected by stress, steroids, hematologic malignancy, immunosuppression
- **Neutrophil-to-Lymphocyte Ratio (NLR):** AUC ~0.62 in neonatal sepsis (cutoff 3.2: sensitivity 52.1%, specificity 50.6%); improves when combined with clotting parameters (AUC 0.76) [^1_2]
- **Platelet-to-Lymphocyte Ratio (PLR):** Poorer diagnostic performance (AUC 0.58) [^1_2]
- **Systemic Immune-Inflammation Index (SII):** Validated as independent predictor of poor outcomes; calculated as (neutrophils × platelets)/lymphocytes [^1_2]


### 3.7 Coagulation Biomarkers

- **Markers:** Platelet count, PT/INR, aPTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer, fibrin degradation products, antithrombin III, Protein C, thrombin-antithrombin (TAT) complex
- **Coagulation Index (CI):** Combines prolonged aPTT (>45s), platelet count <100×10⁹/L, and fibrinogen <1.5 g/L; CI ≥3.2 predicts DIC development (OR 5.6, 95% CI 3.2–9.8) with 84% accuracy and 48-hour earlier detection than ISTH overt-DIC criteria [^1_2]
- **Clinical use:** Coagulopathy reflects sepsis severity; thrombocytopenia and elevated D-dimer correlate with mortality; Protein C deficiency associated with worse outcomes [^1_2]


### 3.8 D-Dimer and Fibrinogen

- Nonspecific but commonly elevated in sepsis; reflect coagulation activation and fibrinolysis; D-dimer elevation correlates with DIC risk and mortality; trends are more useful than absolute values [^1_2]

## 4. New/Emerging Biomarkers

### 4.1 Presepsin (sCD14-ST)

- **Source:** Soluble fragment (13–15 kDa) of CD14, cleaved from monocyte/macrophage cell-surface CD14 upon binding to LPS-LBP complex [^1_1]
- **Mechanism:** CD14 is a co-receptor for TLR4/MD2 complex recognizing LPS of Gram-negative bacteria; cleavage releases presepsin into circulation; marker of monocyte activation and innate immune response [^1_1]
- **Kinetics:** Rises within 2–3 hours of infection (earlier than PCT); peaks at 3–6 hours [^1_2]
- **Diagnostic performance:** AUC 0.89 for sepsis diagnosis in meta-analyses; superior to PCT (AUC 0.76) in pediatric oncology sepsis; 94% negative predictive value for 28-day mortality at 6 hours in pediatric septic shock [^1_2][^1_1]
- **Strengths:** Earlier rise than PCT; correlates with severity; available as POC test; less affected by trauma/surgery than PCT [^1_2]
- **Limitations:** Limited data in non-bacterial sepsis; elevated in renal failure (renal clearance); cost; population-dependent cutoffs; assay standardization issues [^1_2][^1_1]
- **Diagnostic odds ratio:** 10.04 (95% CrI 6.71–14.24) in Bayesian network meta-analysis [^1_5]


### 4.2 Soluble Triggering Receptor Expressed on Myeloid Cells-1 (sTREM-1)

- **Source:** Soluble form of TREM-1, a receptor on neutrophils and monocytes; upregulated and shed during bacterial/fungal infection [^1_2]
- **Mechanism:** TREM-1 amplifies TLR-mediated inflammation; soluble form released into plasma/bronchoalveolar lavage; marker of neutrophil/macrophage activation in infection [^1_2]
- **Kinetics:** Elevated within 6–8 hours of infection [^1_2]
- **Diagnostic performance:** Moderate accuracy for bacterial sepsis; higher in localized infections (pneumonia, peritonitis); diagnostic odds ratio 18.73 (95% CrI 1.31–83.98) but low quality of evidence [^1_5]
- **Prognostic value:** Higher sTREM-1 associated with mortality (pooled RR 2.54, 95% CI for death); pooled sensitivity 0.75 and specificity 0.66 for mortality prediction; combined with SOFA improves prognostic accuracy [^1_6]
- **Limitations:** Unstable in plasma; assay standardization issues; elevated in non-infectious inflammation; variable performance across populations [^1_2][^1_6]


### 4.3 Neutrophil CD64 (nCD64)

- **Source:** Neutrophil surface FcγRI receptor; normally low expression on neutrophils; upregulated within 4–6 hours in response to infection/inflammation [^1_2]
- **Mechanism:** High-affinity receptor for IgG; upregulation reflects neutrophil activation by IFN-γ and G-CSF; marker of innate immune activation [^1_2]
- **Diagnostic performance:** Highest superiority index among all studied biomarkers in Bayesian network meta-analysis; diagnostic odds ratio 20.17 (95% CrI 8.39–38.61); moderate quality of evidence [^1_5]
- **Strengths:** Distinguishes bacterial from viral infections; improves accuracy when combined with neutrophil count; high specificity for bacterial infection [^1_2]
- **Limitations:** Requires flow cytometry (not universally available); limited dynamic data; cutoff standardization needed; cannot be easily repeated for serial monitoring [^1_2]


### 4.4 Soluble Urokinase-type Plasminogen Activator Receptor (suPAR)

- **Source:** Soluble form of uPAR (CD87), expressed on neutrophils, monocytes, macrophages, T-cells, endothelial cells; cleaved and released during immune activation [^1_7]
- **Mechanism:** Involved in cell adhesion, migration, and plasminogen activation; reflects immune activation and systemic inflammation [^1_6]
- **Diagnostic performance:** Low diagnostic value for distinguishing sepsis from SIRS; AUC ~0.77–0.81 for mortality prediction [^1_6]
- **Prognostic value:** Superior prognostic value for mortality in critically ill septic patients; pooled sensitivity 0.74 (95% CI 0.67–0.80) and specificity 0.70 (95% CI 0.63–0.76) for mortality prediction; AUC 0.78–0.81; high suPAR associated with 3.37-fold increased death risk (95% CI 2.6–4.38) [^1_6]
- **Strengths:** Prognostic rather than diagnostic; stable in plasma; adds incremental value when combined with other markers or severity scores [^1_6]
- **Limitations:** Elevated in non-infectious critical illness, malignancy, cardiovascular disease; low specificity for infection; limited clinical availability [^1_6]


### 4.5 Mid-Regional Pro-Adrenomedullin (MR-proADM)

- **Source:** Stable surrogate marker for adrenomedullin (ADM), a 52-amino-acid vasodilatory peptide; ADM is unstable, so MR-proADM (a fragment of the prohormone) is measured instead [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Mechanism:** ADM maintains endothelial barrier integrity and vascular tone; upregulated in sepsis in response to inflammation and oxidative stress; elevated levels reflect endothelial dysfunction, increased vascular permeability, and vasodilatory shock [^1_1]
- **Diagnostic/prognostic performance:** Strongest association with mortality and high disease severity compared to PCT, CRP, and lactate; elevated levels predict 90-day mortality, multi-organ failure, and hemodynamic support requirements; Bio-ADM trajectory during first week predicts 90-day mortality; reduction to <110 pg/mL at day 7 associated with improved survival [^1_2][^1_6]
- **Strengths:** Superior prognostic marker for mortality; reflects endothelial dysfunction dimension; may individualize hemodynamic support [^1_2]
- **Limitations:** Population-dependent cutoffs; prospective studies needed for integration into clinical pathways; cost; limited availability [^1_2]


### 4.6 Interleukin-27 (IL-27)

- **Source:** Produced by antigen-presenting cells (dendritic cells, macrophages) in response to bacterial PAMPs [^1_7]
- **Mechanism:** Heterodimeric cytokine (p28 + EBI3); induces T-bet expression and Th1 differentiation; promotes T-cell proliferation and IFN-γ production; marker of macrophage activation in bacterial infection [^1_7]
- **Diagnostic performance:** Proposed as differentiator of bacterial vs. viral infection in febrile pediatric patients; limited adult data; promising in meta-analyses but requires further validation [^1_7]


### 4.7 Monocyte HLA-DR (mHLA-DR)

- **Source:** Cell-surface MHC class II molecule on monocytes; measured by flow cytometry [^1_2]
- **Mechanism:** Downregulation of mHLA-DR reflects monocyte deactivation and immunoparalysis; hallmark of sepsis-induced immunosuppression (CARS phenotype) [^1_2]
- **Clinical use:** mHLA-DR-guided interleukin-7 therapy in adaptive platform trial (NCT04818879) reduced 28-day mortality by 19% (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.70–0.94) in immunoparalyzed subgroups (CD3+ <800 cells/μL); identifies patients for immunostimulatory therapy [^1_2]
- **Strengths:** Directly assesses adaptive immune function; guides precision immunomodulation; identifies immunoparalyzed patients for targeted therapy (IL-7, IFN-γ, PD-L1 inhibitors) [^1_2]


### 4.8 Calprotectin (S100A8/A9)

- **Source:** Heterodimer of S100A8 and S100A9; abundant in neutrophil cytoplasm; released during neutrophil activation and degranulation [^1_2]
- **Diagnostic performance:** 2.8-fold elevation in bacterial/fungal superinfections in COVID-19 sepsis; cutoff >3,800 ng/mL: 96% specificity and 60% sensitivity for VAP/VRE detection (AUC 0.82); outperforms CRP (ΔAUC = 0.14) [^1_2]
- **Strengths:** Reflects neutrophil activation; distinguishes bacterial superinfection in viral sepsis [^1_2]


### 4.9 Cell-Free DNA (cfDNA)

- **Source:** Released into circulation from necrotic/apoptotic cells and neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) [^1_7]
- **Mechanism:** Acts as a DAMP; activates TLR9 and NLRP3 inflammasome; amplifies inflammation [^1_7]
- **Diagnostic/prognostic value:** Elevated in sepsis; correlates with mortality and organ failure; differentiates sepsis from SIRS in some studies [^1_7]


### 4.10 Pentraxin-3 (PTX3)

- **Source:** Produced by endothelial cells, macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells at sites of infection/inflammation (unlike CRP, which is hepatic) [^1_2]
- **Mechanism:** Pattern recognition molecule; binds PAMPs (LPS, fungal β-glucans) and DAMPs; activates complement and opsonization [^1_2]
- **Clinical value:** Reflects local tissue-level inflammation more specifically than CRP; associated with septic shock and mortality [^1_2]


### 4.11 Angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2)

- **Source:** Stored in Weibel-Palade bodies of endothelial cells; released upon endothelial activation by inflammatory stimuli (TNF-α, VEGF) [^1_2]
- **Mechanism:** Antagonist of Tie-2 receptor; causes endothelial destabilization, vascular leak, and increased adhesion molecule expression; key mediator of sepsis-induced endothelial barrier disruption [^1_2]
- **Clinical value:** Elevated Ang-2 correlates with vascular leak, organ dysfunction, and mortality; Ang-1/Ang-2 ratio may better predict outcomes [^1_2]


### 4.12 Soluble IL-7 Receptor (sIL-7R)

- **Source:** Soluble form of IL-7 receptor α-chain (CD127); shed from T-cells [^1_2]
- **Prognostic value:** sIL-7R <5.2 ng/mL at day 5 combined with ΔCD3+ >150 cells/μL predicted 90-day survival with 92% NPV (AUROC 0.94); each 0.5 ng/mL decrease between days 3–5 associated with 23% lower mortality risk (OR 0.77, 95% CI 0.65–0.91); combined sIL-7R/SOFA model achieved NRI of 0.41 over SOFA alone [^1_2]


### 4.13 Non-Coding RNAs (ncRNAs)

- **Types:** MicroRNAs (miRNAs), long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), and circular RNAs (circRNAs) [^1_2]
- **miR-486-5p:** Abnormally expressed in sepsis; upregulation inhibits pro-inflammatory cytokine release; potential biomarker for sepsis-related AKI and antibiotic nephrotoxicity [^1_2]
- **HOTTIP (lncRNA):** Elevated in sepsis-associated ARDS; positively correlated with SOFA and APACHE II scores; promotes cardiac dysfunction via LPS-induced apoptosis in cardiomyocytes; regulates miR-574-5p [^1_2]
- **circRNAs:** >80% of ncRNAs differentially expressed in septic patients; high stability in plasma/exosomes; tissue-specific potential [^1_2]
- **Strengths:** Stable in body fluids; dynamic monitoring potential; tissue-specific signatures [^1_2]
- **Limitations:** Complex detection (RNA sequencing, RT-PCR); unvalidated in large cohorts; lack of standardized assays; preclinical stage [^1_2]


### 4.14 Gene Expression Signatures / Transcriptomic Panels

- **Mechanism:** RNA expression profiling identifies sepsis-specific molecular endotypes (hyperinflammatory vs. hypoinflammatory); these endotypes have vastly different mortality rates (48% vs. 12%) [^1_2]
- **Validated panels:**
    - 12-gene LASSO-Cox signature stratifies high-risk patients with distinct cytokine trajectories [^1_2]
    - 5-gene pediatric panel (CD177, MMP8, CYSTM1, S100A12, LCN2): AUROC 0.94 for early diagnosis [^1_2]
    - 6-gene panel validated across multiple cohorts: mean AUROC 0.938 [^1_2]
    - IL1R2/FCGR2B ratio in monocytes: high sensitivity for progressive organ dysfunction [^1_2]
- **S100A8/A9** genes identified as core mortality-correlated genes in WGCNA analysis of 2,148 sepsis transcriptomes [^1_2]
- **Limitations:** Machine learning models suffer from dataset shift, label noise, unmeasured confounding, and information leakage; most remain exploratory requiring prospective external validation [^1_2]


### 4.15 Metabolomic Biomarkers

- **Free Fatty Acids (FFAs):** C16:0 (palmitic acid) >180 μmol/L predicts progression to septic shock within 48h (90% sensitivity); palmitic acid/linoleic acid index >2.5 independently predicts 28-day mortality (adjusted HR 2.34, 95% CI 1.72–3.18); superior discrimination to SOFA score (ΔAUC +0.09) [^1_2]
- **ω-6/ω-3 ratio:** Dysregulated ratio <5:1 activates NLRP3 inflammasomes (2.8-fold increase); associated with cytokine storm [^1_2]
- **LDH:** >480 U/L during ICU days 3–5 confers 4.1-fold mortality risk (95% CI 3.2–5.3); independent prognostic value after adjusting for APACHE IV (AUC 0.77 vs. 0.69) [^1_2]
- **CK-MB:** >5 ng/mL demonstrates 82% specificity for sepsis-induced myocardial dysfunction; >10 ng/mL associated with 3.2-fold increased risk of inotropic support requirement and 42% higher 28-day mortality [^1_2]
- **Pancreatic Stone Protein (PSP):** In pediatric septic shock (PEDSEPSIS trial), serial PSP >400 ng/mL at 0h + >20% increase at 24h: AUC 0.91 (vs. CRP 0.78, PCT 0.82); 6h PSP predicts 28-day mortality with 94% NPV [^1_2]
- **TyG Index (Triglyceride-Glucose):** Elevated TyG index predicts sepsis risk in COPD exacerbations (adjusted OR 2.01, 95% CI 1.34–3.02); robust correlation with mortality in critically ill sepsis patients [^1_2]


### 4.16 Tyrosine Metabolism Index and Other Novel Markers

- **LGALS3BP and GDF-15:** Elevated levels associated with increased mortality; combined predictive efficacy surpasses individual markers [^1_2]
- **Cystatin C:** Earlier indicator of renal damage than traditional markers (creatinine) in sepsis-associated AKI [^1_2]
- **Lipopolysaccharide-Binding Protein (LBP):** Acute-phase protein that transfers LPS to CD14/TLR4 complex; moderate diagnostic accuracy; included in Bayesian network meta-analysis [^1_5]

## 6. Biomarker Panels and Machine Learning Integration

- No single biomarker meets all diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic needs; multi-biomarker panels combined with clinical scores outperform individual markers [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Machine learning algorithms** integrating biomarker kinetics with clinical data improve prognostic stratification (ΔAUC +0.15 vs. static measures for cytokine trajectory patterns) [^1_2]
- **Multi-omics integration** (transcriptomics + metabolomics + proteomics) aims to decode sepsis heterogeneity and enable precision immunometabolic therapy [^1_2]
- **Wireless monitoring algorithms** integrating continuous heart rate, respiratory rate, and perfusion index achieved 2.5-hour lead-time for septic shock prediction in pediatric patients (AUROC 0.88) [^1_2]
- **Key limitation:** Most ML models remain hypothesis-generating; suffer from dataset shift, label noise, missing data, unmeasured confounding, and information leakage; require prospective external validation before clinical implementation [^1_2]

## 7. Diagnostic Accuracy Ranking (Bayesian Network Meta-Analysis)

A 2025 systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis of 78 studies (34,234 patients) compared qSOFA, SIRS, and 7 biomarkers (PCT, CRP, IL-6, presepsin, CD64, sTREM-1, LBP) [^1_5]:


| **Rank** | **Biomarker** | **Diagnostic Odds Ratio (DOR)** | **95% CrI** | **Quality of Evidence** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | **CD64** | 20.17 | 8.39–38.61 | Moderate |
| 2 | **sTREM-1** | 18.73 | 1.31–83.98 | Low |
| 3 | **Presepsin** | 10.04 | 6.71–14.24 | Low |

All biomarkers were more effective at identifying Sepsis-3–defined sepsis than clinical scores (qSOFA, SIRS) [^1_5].

## 8. Clinical Pearls

- **Lactate clearance** is more prognostically valuable than baseline lactate; clearance <10% at 6 hours is a stronger predictor of mortality than admission APACHE II [^1_2]
- **PCT is better at ruling out bacterial sepsis than confirming it** — a low PCT does not exclude infection in immunocompromised or localized infections [^1_1]
- **Presepsin rises earlier than PCT** (2–3 h vs. 2–4 h) and is less affected by trauma/surgery — making it useful in post-surgical and trauma ICU patients [^1_2]
- **MR-proADM is the strongest single prognostic biomarker for mortality** — outperforming PCT, CRP, and lactate for mortality prediction [^1_2][^1_6]
- **mHLA-DR is the only biomarker that directly guides immunomodulatory therapy** — low mHLA-DR identifies immunoparalyzed patients who may benefit from IL-7 or IFN-γ [^1_2]
- **Sepsis endotyping** (hyperinflammatory vs. hypoinflammatory) using gene expression panels may revolutionize precision therapy — these endotypes have 4-fold difference in mortality (48% vs. 12%) [^1_2]
- **Cytokine ratios (IL-6/IL-10) outperform individual cytokines** for prognostic stratification, reflecting the balance between pro- and anti-inflammatory arms [^1_2]
- **nCD64 has the highest diagnostic accuracy** among all studied biomarkers but requires flow cytometry, limiting bedside applicability [^1_5]
- **No biomarker replaces clinical judgment** — biomarkers augment, not replace, clinical assessment, microbiological cultures, and imaging [^1_1]
- **Serial trends are more informative than single values** for all biomarkers — kinetic patterns reflect disease trajectory and therapeutic response [^1_1][^1_2]
- **suPAR is a prognostic, not diagnostic, biomarker** — it cannot differentiate sepsis from SIRS but excels at mortality prediction [^1_6]
- **The "Holy Grail" biomarker for sepsis remains elusive** — sepsis is a heterogeneous syndrome with multiple phenotypes and endotypes, making a single universal biomarker unlikely [^1_8][^1_1]

***

## 9. Future Directions

- **Point-of-care multiplex assays** combining PCT, presepsin, MR-proADM, and lactate on a single platform with rapid turnaround [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Machine learning–driven biomarker integration** with electronic health records for real-time sepsis prediction and risk stratification [^1_2]
- **Sepsis endotype–specific biomarker panels** guiding targeted immunomodulation (anakinra for hyperinflammatory; IL-7 for immunoparalyzed) [^1_2]
- **Single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics** for granular immune phenotyping at the cellular level [^1_2]
- **Multi-omics integration** (genomics + transcriptomics + proteomics + metabolomics + microbiomics) for comprehensive sepsis phenotyping [^1_2]
- **Biomarker-guided adaptive platform trials** (e.g., NCT04818879) testing precision immunotherapy in biomarker-selected subgroups [^1_2]
