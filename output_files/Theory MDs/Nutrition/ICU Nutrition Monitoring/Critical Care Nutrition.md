
## Critical Care Nutrition



Monitoring in ICU nutrition is not “once and done” — it is a **dynamic, multi‑domain process** that tracks **delivery, tolerance, metabolic response, and complications** to ensure the patient receives the right substrate, at the right time, without harm. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3]

Below is a concise, exam‑oriented framework aligned with ASPEN/ESPEN and recent critical care nutrition guidelines. [^1_2][^1_4][^1_5]

***

## 1. Goals of Nutrition Monitoring in ICU

- Ensure **adequate energy and protein delivery** matched to evolving metabolic needs. [^1_2][^1_6]
- Detect and prevent **nutrition‑related complications**:
    - Refeeding syndrome
    - Hyper/hypoglycemia
    - Electrolyte disturbances
    - Over/underfeeding
    - GI intolerance
    - Line infections (for PN) [^1_7][^1_8][^1_9]
- Guide **timely adjustments** in route, rate, and composition of nutrition. [^1_1][^1_10]

***

## 2. Domains of Monitoring (The “4T” Framework)

Think of nutrition monitoring as **4T**:

1. **Target** – Are goals appropriate and updated?
2. **Throughput** – Is the prescribed nutrition actually delivered?
3. **Tolerance** – Is the patient tolerating the feed (GI/metabolic)?
4. **Toxicity/Complications** – Any adverse effects?

Use this as a mental checklist in exams and practice.

***

## 3. Baseline Assessment (Before/During Initiation)

### 3.1. Nutritional Risk \& Baseline Status

- **Screen within 48 h** of ICU admission using:
    - NRS‑2002 or
    - mNUTRIC / NUTRIC score [^1_10][^1_11]
- Document:
    - Pre‑ICU weight, recent weight loss, BMI
    - Muscle wasting, edema, cachexia
    - Comorbidities (CKD, liver disease, alcohol use, malignancy) [^1_1][^1_12]

> **High‑risk features for refeeding syndrome**:
> BMI <16, unintentional weight loss >15% in 3–6 months, little/no intake >5–10 days, low baseline K/Mg/Phos, chronic alcoholism, prolonged starvation, severe sepsis/trauma. [^1_8][^1_13][^1_9]

### 3.2. Baseline Labs (Day 0–1)

Obtain before or at nutrition initiation:

- **Electrolytes**: Na, K, Cl, HCO₃, Ca, Mg, Phosphate
- **Renal**: Urea, Creatinine
- **Liver**: AST, ALT, ALP, Bilirubin, Albumin (trend, not diagnostic)
- **Glucose**: Random / fasting
- **Triglycerides** (especially if PN or high‑risk)
- **CRP / Procalcitonin** (optional, for context)
- **Thiamine** (if available, or give empirically in high risk) [^1_14][^1_7][^1_8][^1_15]

***

## 4. Monitoring Energy \& Protein Targets

### 4.1. Energy Requirements

- **Preferred**: Indirect calorimetry (IC) once hemodynamically stable (typically after 24–72 h). [^1_6][^1_10][^1_5]
- **If IC unavailable**:
    - Use predictive equations (e.g., 25–30 kcal/kg/day) with caution.
    - Reassess at least **weekly** or with major clinical change. [^1_4][^1_5]

> **ESPEN practical approach**:
> - Days 1–3: <70% of measured/estimated REE
> - Days 3–7: progress to 80–100%
> - Avoid overfeeding, especially early. [^1_5]

### 4.2. Protein Requirements

- Typical ICU target: **1.2–2.0 g/kg/day** (higher in burns, trauma, CRRT). [^1_1][^1_4][^1_5]
- Monitor:
    - Daily protein delivery vs target
    - Renal function and urea trend (not as sole marker of catabolism) [^1_14]

***

## 5. Monitoring Delivery (Throughput)

### 5.1. Daily Documentation

Every 24 h, document:

- **Prescribed** vs **delivered** calories and protein (EN + PN + oral).
- **Reasons for interruptions**:
    - Procedures
    - Hemodynamic instability
    - High GRV / vomiting
    - Diarrhea
    - Tube displacement [^1_1][^1_4]

> **Key exam point**: >50% of target not met by **72 h** → consider supplementary PN (especially in high nutrition risk). [^1_1][^1_5]

### 5.2. Route‑Specific Monitoring

#### Enteral Nutrition (EN)

- Check:
    - Tube position (radiographic confirmation initially; clinical checks thereafter)
    - Pump rate, residual policy, flushes
    - Stool frequency/consistency
    - Abdominal exam (distension, tenderness, bowel sounds) [^1_1][^1_14]


#### Parenteral Nutrition (PN)

- Additional focus on:
    - Catheter site (erythema, discharge)
    - Fever curve / sepsis signs
    - Fluid balance (PN adds 1–2 L/day)
    - Triglycerides (especially with lipid emulsions)
    - Liver enzymes and bilirubin (cholestasis, steatosis) [^1_7][^1_14]

***

## 6. Monitoring Tolerance

### 6.1. Gastrointestinal Tolerance (EN)

Assess **daily** (or more often if unstable):

- **Clinical**:
    - Nausea, vomiting, abdominal distension/pain
    - Bowel sounds, stool pattern (diarrhea/constipation)
- **Gastric Residual Volume (GRV)**:
    - Modern guidelines (ASPEN/ESPEN): **do not routinely monitor GRV** to guide EN. [^1_16][^1_4]
    - If your unit still uses GRV:
        - Use syringe aspiration, not suction.
        - **Do not hold EN for GRV <500 mL** in absence of other intolerance signs.
        - Consider prokinetics if persistent intolerance with other signs. [^1_1][^1_4]

> **Exam pearl**: Routine GRV monitoring does **not** reduce pneumonia and leads to unnecessary feed interruptions. [^1_16][^1_4]

- **Dynamic perfusion markers** (if on vasopressors):
    - MAP, lactate, vasopressor dose, skin mottling.
    - Escalate caution if shock worsens during early EN. [^1_10]


### 6.2. Metabolic Tolerance

Monitor for:

- **Hyperglycemia**:
    - Check blood glucose:
        - At least **every 4 h** for first 48 h after nutrition initiation or ICU admission.
        - Then adjust frequency per local protocol. [^1_2][^1_10][^1_17]
    - Treat if >180 mg/dL (10 mmol/L) per local insulin protocol. [^1_17]
- **Electrolytes**:
    - K, Mg, Phosphate: **daily for first week**, more frequently if high risk or abnormal. [^1_2][^1_17]
    - In refeeding risk: check **every 12 h for first 3 days**. [^1_8][^1_13][^1_18]
- **Triglycerides**:
    - Especially with PN, sepsis, pancreatitis, or high lipid loads. [^1_14][^1_10]
- **Acid–base**:
    - Watch for metabolic acidosis/alkalosis related to feed composition (e.g., chloride vs acetate in PN). [^1_7]

***

## 7. Refeeding Syndrome – Focused Monitoring

### 7.1. Who to Monitor Closely?

High‑risk patients (see Section 3.1) need **enhanced monitoring**:

- Baseline electrolytes and glucose **before feeding**.
- Aggressive thiamine and multivitamin prophylaxis. [^1_8][^1_13][^1_19]


### 7.2. Monitoring Schedule (High Risk)

- **Electrolytes (K, Mg, Phos, Ca)**:
    - Every **12 h for first 3 days**, then daily once stable.
    - More frequent if levels drop or symptoms develop. [^1_8][^1_13][^1_18]
- **Glucose**:
    - Every 4–6 h initially.
- **Fluid balance**:
    - Strict input–output, daily weights if feasible.


### 7.3. Diagnostic Clues (Exam‑Relevant)

Refeeding likely if within **72 h** of starting nutrition:

- Phosphate drop **>30%** or <0.6 mmol/L (<1.8 mg/dL), and/or
- Two of three: Phos, K, Mg fall below normal without other cause. [^1_19][^1_18]

Clinical features: arrhythmias, weakness, respiratory failure, seizures, delirium, hemolysis.

> **Key management principle**: Do **not stop feeding** abruptly; correct electrolytes aggressively and consider temporarily reducing caloric load if severe. [^1_15][^1_9]

***

## 8. Monitoring Body Composition \& Muscle (Advanced/Optional)

Increasingly emphasized in modern guidelines, though evidence is still evolving:

- **Ultrasound**:
    - Quadriceps/rectus femoris thickness, lumbar muscle area (if CT available). [^1_12][^1_10]
- **Bioelectrical Impedance Analysis (BIA)**:
    - Trends in lean body mass / fluid status (interpret cautiously in edema). [^1_10]
- **CT‑based muscle area** (if abdominal CT done):
    - L3 skeletal muscle index as surrogate for muscle mass. [^1_12]

Use mainly for **trend monitoring** and research; not yet mandatory in all ICUs.

***

## 9. Monitoring Complications \& Safety

### 9.1. Infection (Especially PN)

- Daily review of:
    - Fever pattern
    - WBC, CRP/procalcitonin trends
    - Catheter site
- Low threshold for:
    - Blood cultures if sepsis suspected
    - Line removal if catheter‑related bloodstream infection likely [^1_7]


### 9.2. Liver \& Biliary Issues (PN‑Associated)

- Monitor:
    - LFTs (AST, ALT, ALP, GGT, bilirubin) **2–3 times/week** in prolonged PN.
    - Triglycerides (weekly or more often if abnormal). [^1_14][^1_7]


### 9.3. Fluid Overload

- Daily weight (if feasible), fluid balance, edema, lung exam.
- Adjust diuretics and fluid components of nutrition accordingly. [^1_7]

***

## 10. Practical Monitoring Checklist (Daily ICU Round)

Use this as a **mental or written checklist**:

1. **Targets**
    - Current kcal/kg/day and g protein/kg/day targets appropriate for phase of illness?
2. **Delivery**
    - Prescribed vs delivered calories/protein in last 24 h?
    - Any prolonged interruptions? Why?
3. **Tolerance – GI**
    - Vomiting, distension, diarrhea, constipation?
    - Bowel sounds, abdominal exam normal?
    - If EN: tube position secure, GRV policy followed (if used)?
4. **Tolerance – Metabolic**
    - Glucose trend and insulin requirements?
    - Electrolytes (K, Mg, Phos, Ca) – any new deficits?
    - Triglycerides (if PN or high risk)?
5. **Complications**
    - Fever, line site issues, rising WBC/CRP?
    - LFT trend in PN?
    - Fluid balance, weight change, edema?
6. **Adjustments**
    - Need to increase/decrease rate, change formula, add PN, or modify electrolytes/vitamins?

***

## 11. Frequency Summary (Exam‑Ready Table)

| Parameter | When to Check | High‑Risk / Abnormal |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Nutritional risk score | Within 48 h of ICU admission | Repeat if clinical status changes |
| Weight / hydration status | On admission; then weekly (or as feasible) | More often if fluid shifts major |
| Energy expenditure (IC) | Once stable (24–72 h); repeat weekly | With major clinical change |
| Blood glucose | Every 4 h × 48 h after nutrition/ICU start | More frequent if unstable or on insulin |
| K, Mg, Phosphate, Ca | Daily × first week | Every 12 h × 3 days if refeeding risk |
| Triglycerides | Baseline; then 2–3×/week if on PN or high risk | More often if elevated |
| LFTs | Baseline; 2–3×/week if on PN | More frequent if abnormal |
| GI tolerance (EN) | Daily | More often if symptoms |
| Delivery (kcal \& protein) | Daily | — |
| Line site / infection signs | Daily (PN) | Immediate review if sepsis suspected |

[^1_1][^1_2][^1_10][^1_7][^1_17][^1_8][^1_5]

***

## 12. Key Exam Pearls

- **Do not routinely monitor GRV**; it increases feed interruptions without clear benefit. [^1_16][^1_4]
- **Refeeding syndrome** = think phosphate first; monitor K/Mg/Phos closely in malnourished/starved patients. [^1_8][^1_9][^1_19]
- **Indirect calorimetry** is the gold standard for energy targets; use if available once stable. [^1_6][^1_10][^1_5]
- If **>50% of target not met by 72 h**, consider supplemental PN in high‑risk patients. [^1_1][^1_5]
- **Glucose monitoring** is mandatory after starting nutrition; hyperglycemia is common and treatable. [^1_2][^1_7][^1_17]
