
## Process of Withdrawing or Withholding Life-Sustaining Treatment in Indian ICUs

The withdrawal or withholding of life-sustaining treatment (LST) in Indian ICUs is governed by a framework combining Supreme Court directives (most recently clarified in January 2023 and applied in subsequent 2025–2026 rulings), the Indian Society of Critical Care Medicine (ISCCM) consensus guidelines, and the Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act (THOA) for brainstem death. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3][^1_4] The process is legally permissible under specific conditions and follows a structured, multi-step pathway involving medical boards, surrogate consent, and judicial intimation. [^1_1][^1_2]

## Definitions and Legal Basis

### Key Definitions [^1_1]

- **Terminal illness**: An irreversible or incurable condition from which death is inevitable in the foreseeable future; includes severe devastating traumatic brain injury showing no recovery after ≥72 hours.
- **Withdrawal (WD)**: A considered decision to stop ongoing LST in a terminally ill patient when it is no longer likely to benefit and may cause suffering or loss of dignity.
- **Withholding (WH)**: A considered decision not to start a life-supporting measure in a terminally ill patient when it is unlikely to benefit and may cause harm.
- **Do-Not-Attempt-Resuscitation (DNAR)**: A decision not to perform CPR in the event of anticipated cardiac arrest if there is no realistic possibility of survival or meaningful recovery.
- **Foregoing of Life-Sustaining Treatment (FLST)**: Collective term for WD, WH, and DNAR.
- **Advance Medical Directive (AMD)**: A written declaration by a person with decision-making capacity documenting treatment preferences if they lose capacity.


### Legal Foundations

- **Supreme Court of India**: In *Common Cause v. Union of India* (2018) and subsequent orders (January 24, 2023; March 11, 2026), the Court affirmed that:
    - An adult patient with capacity may refuse LST even if it results in death.
    - LST may be withheld or withdrawn from patients without capacity based on autonomy, privacy, and dignity.
    - AMDs meeting specified requirements are legally valid.
    - A two-tier medical board system (Primary and Secondary) must validate FLST decisions for incapacitated patients.
    - Active euthanasia remains unlawful. [^1_1][^1_5][^1_4][^1_6]
- **THOA Act (1994)**: Recognizes brainstem death as equivalent to legal death, permitting withdrawal of LST in such cases. [^1_1][^1_7]


## Stepwise Process for WD/WH in Indian ICUs

The following pathway integrates ISCCM guidelines and Supreme Court directives: [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3][^1_4]

### Step 1: Initiation of Discussion

- **Trigger**: Treating physician identifies futility based on clinical criteria (e.g., advanced disease, no response to aggressive therapy, brainstem death, or surrogate refusal).
- **Family conference**: Establish irreversibility, evaluate decision-making capacity, and ensure full comprehension of consequences. [^1_2]


### Step 2: Validation of Advance Medical Directive (if applicable)

- If a valid AMD exists, verify its authenticity per Supreme Court format.
- If no AMD, proceed with surrogate decision-maker (next of kin/next friend/guardian). [^1_2][^1_4]


### Step 3: Primary Medical Board (PMB) Constitution and Evaluation

- **Composition**: Treating physician + ≥2 subject experts with ≥5 years' post-qualification experience.
- **Task**: Evaluate patient, interact with family, document prognosis, and provide opinion on WD/WH within 48 hours.
- **Consent**: Obtain written consent from surrogate (or AMD holder) after explaining pros/cons. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_4]


### Step 4: Secondary Medical Board (SMB) Constitution and Validation

- **Composition**: ≥2 subject experts (≥5 years' experience) + 1 Registered Medical Practitioner nominated by the District Chief Medical Officer (CMO).
- **Task**: Independent review of patient, family interaction, and concurrence with PMB opinion within 48 hours.
- **Note**: No PMB member can serve on SMB; all doctors may be from the same hospital. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_4]


### Step 5: Judicial Intimation (Not Approval)

- **Action**: Hospital Chief Administrator sends intimation (not seeking approval) to the Judicial Magistrate of First Class (JMFC) with:
    - PMB and SMB concurrence reports.
    - Copy of AMD or surrogate consent.
    - Cover letter explaining circumstances.
- **Timing**: After both boards concur; treatment may proceed thereafter. [^1_2][^1_3][^1_4]


### Step 6: Implementation of WD/WH

- **Documentation**: Complete FLST form signed by family and treating team.
- **Palliative transition**: Provide compassionate symptom control, liberalize visitation, allow religious rituals, and ensure non-abandonment. [^1_1][^1_2]


## Decision-Making Criteria for FLST

### Medical Indications [^1_7][^1_1]

- Brainstem death (per THOA Act).
- Advanced, irreversible disease with no realistic benefit from aggressive therapy.
- Documented informed refusal by patient/surrogate after prognostic awareness.
- Physician prediction of <10% survival probability with poor quality of life.


### Bedside Checklist for Initiating EOL Discussions [^1_7]

1. Advanced age + poor functional state due to chronic organ dysfunction.
2. Catastrophic illness unresponsive to reasonable aggressive treatment.
3. Coma (non-brain-death) from irreversible causes (e.g., massive stroke, TBI).
4. Chronic severe neurological conditions (e.g., advanced dementia, vegetative state).
5. Progressive metastatic cancer with failed/refused treatment.
6. Post-cardiac arrest poor neurological recovery after ≥3 days (≥7 days if hypothermia used).
7. Comparable situations with low predicted survival.
8. Patient/family preference to limit or refuse LST.

## Documentation and Oversight

### Required Documentation [^1_7][^1_1][^1_2]

- Family conference notes (including participants, discussion points, and decisions).
- PMB and SMB reports in prescribed formats.
- Surrogate consent/AMD copy.
- FLST order form signed by treating physician and family.
- Intimation letter to JMFC with attachments.


### Hospital Oversight Mechanisms

- **Clinical Ethics Committee**: Multi-professional body for audit, oversight, and conflict resolution (recommended composition: hospital administrator, senior EOLC expert, external EOLC expert, legal expert, social worker). [^1_1]
- **Standing CMO Panel**: Hospitals may maintain a pre-approved list of physicians for SMB nominations. [^1_1]


## Special Scenarios

### Brainstem Death

- Declared per THOA Act by qualified physicians.
- LST withdrawal is legally and ethically mandated; no further board approval required beyond THOA protocol. [^1_1][^1_7]


### Disagreement Between Boards or Family

- If PMB or SMB does not concur, or if family disputes decision:
    - Continue existing LST.
    - Family/treating physician may approach High Court under Article 226 for permission.
    - High Court constitutes Division Bench; may appoint independent medical committee. [^1_4][^1_8]


### Pediatric and Neonatal Considerations

- Similar principles apply; emphasis on "no chance," "no purpose," or "unbearable" situations per EACRCPCH guidelines. [^1_7]
- Parental consent critical; ethics committee involvement recommended.


## Evidence and Guidelines Summary

### ISCCM Consensus (2012, updated 2024) [^1_7][^1_3]

- Physician obligated to disclose prognosis honestly and initiate EOL discussions.
- Shared decision-making with patient/family; respect autonomy while ensuring beneficence.
- FLST decisions should be documented transparently; palliative care must follow.


### Supreme Court Directives (2018, 2023, 2026) [^1_1][^1_2][^1_4][^1_6]

- Legalized passive euthanasia (WD/WH) with safeguards.
- Simplified 2023 order: JMFC intimation only (no prior approval); 48-hour board response times.
- 2026 ruling (*Harish Rana* case): First application permitting WD in persistent vegetative state after 13 years, reinforcing procedural compliance. [^1_6]


### Epidemiological Context

- FLST precedes 34–91% of ICU deaths in Indian studies, with significant inter-center variability. [^1_7][^1_9]
- Barriers include legal anxieties, cultural "fight till the end" ethos, and lack of palliative infrastructure. [^1_7]


## Practical ICU Workflow (Condensed)

| Step | Action | Responsible Party | Timeline |

| 1 | Identify futility + initiate family discussion | Treating physician | Day 0 |
| 2 | Validate AMD / identify surrogate | Treating physician | Day 0 |
| 3 | Constitute PMB + evaluate + obtain consent | Hospital administrator + PMB | ≤48 hours |
| 4 | Constitute SMB + independent review | Hospital + CMO nominee | ≤48 hours |
| 5 | Intimate JMFC (no approval needed) | Chief Administrator | Post-concurrence |
| 6 | Implement WD/WH + palliative care | Treating team | Immediately after Step 5 |

## Key Clinical Pearls

- **Withholding ≠ Withdrawal**: Both are ethically and legally equivalent; withdrawal is not "killing" but allowing natural death from underlying disease. [^1_7]
- **Documentation is protection**: Faithful recording of family conferences, board opinions, and consent shields against legal liability. [^1_7][^1_1]
- **Palliative transition is mandatory**: Symptom control, psychosocial support, and cultural sensitivity must accompany FLST. [^1_1][^1_2]
- **No obligation to provide futile care**: Physicians are not bound to institute new therapies against clinical judgment, even if family demands. [^1_7]


## Recent Developments (2025–2026)

- **March 2026**: Supreme Court permitted WD in a 13-year vegetative state case (*Harish Rana*), marking first direct application of 2023 simplified procedure. [^1_6]
- **2025–2026**: High Courts and medical councils increasingly reference 2023 order for expedited board constitution and JMFC intimation. [^1_10][^1_4]
