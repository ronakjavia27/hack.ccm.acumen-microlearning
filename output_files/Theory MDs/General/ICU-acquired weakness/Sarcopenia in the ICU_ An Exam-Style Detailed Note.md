
## Sarcopenia in the ICU

### Definition

Sarcopenia is a progressive, generalized skeletal Muscle disease characterized by loss of Muscle Mass, Muscle Strength, and/or Physical Performance. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3] In the ICU context, it is increasingly recognized as **acute sarcopenia** (developing over <6 months) or **ICU-acquired sarcopenia**, often overlapping with the broader syndrome of **ICU-acquired weakness (ICU-AW)**. [^1_4][^1_5]

- **EWGSOP2 (2018/2019) definition**: Sarcopenia is a muscle disease (muscle failure) with:
    - **Primary criterion**: Low muscle strength (e.g., handgrip <27 kg men, <16 kg women).
    - **Confirmatory criterion**: Low muscle quantity/quality (e.g., appendicular lean mass index <7.0 kg/m² men, <5.5 kg/m² women by DEXA).
    - **Severity criterion**: Poor physical performance denotes severe sarcopenia. [^1_4][^1_6]
- **ICU-specific operational definition**: Because dynamic strength testing is often impractical in sedated/ventilated patients, ICU sarcopenia is frequently diagnosed using:
    - **Low muscle mass** (e.g., CT-derived cross-sectional area at L3, ultrasound muscle thickness) **plus**
    - **Assumed or measured functional impairment** (e.g., MRC sum score <48/60 when testable). [^1_7][^1_8]
- **ICD-10**: Recognized as a disease entity (M62.84). [^1_9]

***

### Classification

Sarcopenia can be classified based on etiology and time course:


| Type | Description |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Primary sarcopenia** | Age-related (typically >65 years), chronic, progressive. [^1_3][^1_6] |
| **Secondary sarcopenia** | Due to inflammation, malnutrition, inactivity, organ failure, or critical illness. [^1_10][^1_3] |
| **Acute sarcopenia** | Rapid muscle loss over <6 months; includes ICU-acquired cases. [^1_4][^1_11] |
| **Chronic sarcopenia** | Long-standing, typically in elderly or chronically ill. |

In the ICU, sarcopenia often represents an **acute-on-chronic** process: pre-existing low muscle mass (chronic sarcopenia/frailty) exacerbated by the catabolic storm of critical illness (acute sarcopenia/ICU-AW). [^1_4][^1_12]

***

### Epidemiology

- **Prevalence in ICU**: Varies widely from **25% to 100%** depending on population, definition, and timing of assessment. [^1_9]
- **High-risk groups**: Elderly, septic, post-major surgery/trauma, prolonged ventilation, multiorgan failure. [^1_13][^1_14][^1_15]
- **ICU-AW incidence**: ~40% in mixed medical–surgical ICU; up to 80% in some cohorts. [^1_16][^1_17]

***

### Risk Factors

Risk factors can be divided into **pre-ICU**, **ICU-related**, and **treatment-related**:

#### Pre-ICU / Patient-related

- **Advanced age** (>65 years) [^1_10][^1_13]
- **Low baseline muscle mass / frailty** (low BMI, low calf circumference, low ASMI) [^1_13][^1_11]
- **Chronic diseases**: CKD, COPD, heart failure, cirrhosis, malignancy [^1_15][^1_18]
- **Malnutrition / low protein intake** [^1_10][^1_11]
- **Sedentary lifestyle / pre-ICU immobility** [^1_13]
- **Multiple recent hospitalizations** [^1_13]


#### ICU-related / Disease-related

- **Sepsis / septic shock** (strongest driver) [^1_12][^1_19][^1_20]
- **Multiorgan failure / high SOFA or APACHE II scores** [^1_15][^1_21]
- **Prolonged mechanical ventilation** [^1_13][^1_15]
- **Extended ICU/hospital stay** [^1_13][^1_14][^1_11]
- **Hyperglycemia** [^1_19][^1_21]
- **Neurological disease** [^1_16]


#### Treatment-related

- **Corticosteroids** (high dose/prolonged) [^1_17][^1_21]
- **Neuromuscular blocking agents** [^1_17][^1_21][^1_20]
- **Deep/continuous sedation** → immobilization [^1_17]
- **Parenteral nutrition / lipid emulsions** (potential toxicity) [^1_17]
- **Renal replacement therapy** (associated with higher mortality in sarcopenic patients) [^1_22]

***

### Pathogenesis

Sarcopenia in the ICU is a **multifactorial muscle failure** state. The core problem is an **imbalance between muscle protein synthesis and degradation**, leading to rapid net loss of muscle mass and impaired contractility.

#### 1. Catabolic–Anabolic Hormonal Imbalance

- **Increased catabolic hormones**: Cortisol, catecholamines, glucagon.
- **Reduced anabolic hormones**: Insulin, IGF-1, testosterone, growth hormone resistance.
- Net effect: **Proteolysis > proteosynthesis**. [^1_5][^1_17]


#### 2. Systemic Inflammation

- **Sepsis/SIRS** → high TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6.
- Activation of **NF-κB** and **STAT3** pathways.
- Upregulation of **ubiquitin–proteasome system (UPS)** and **autophagy–lysosome pathway**.
- Increased expression of **atrogin-1 (FBXO32)** and **MuRF1** → myofibrillar protein degradation. [^1_5][^1_17][^1_23]


#### 3. Mechanical Unloading / Immobilization

- Bed rest and sedation → **disuse atrophy**.
- Loss of mechano-transduction signals (e.g., IGF-1/Akt/mTOR pathway downregulation).
- Reduced muscle protein synthesis. [^1_5][^1_20]


#### 4. Mitochondrial Dysfunction \& Oxidative Stress

- **Bioenergetic failure**: Reduced ATP production, mitochondrial damage.
- Increased ROS → oxidative damage to myofilaments and sarcoplasmic reticulum.
- Impaired calcium handling → reduced contractility. [^1_24][^1_19][^1_23]


#### 5. Neuromuscular Components (ICU-AW overlap)

- **Critical illness polyneuropathy (CIP)**: Axonal degeneration, increased nerve membrane permeability.
- **Critical illness myopathy (CIM)**: Myosin loss, muscle membrane inexcitability, autophagy of muscle fibers.
- **Polyneuromyopathy**: Combined features. [^1_16][^1_17][^1_21]


#### 6. Additional Contributors

- **Hyperglycemia**: Glycation, oxidative stress, direct myotoxicity.
- **Corticosteroids**: Direct myofibrillar proteolysis, inhibition of protein synthesis.
- **Neuromuscular blockers**: Prolonged blockade, denervation-like changes.
- **Malnutrition**: Inadequate protein/energy intake relative to hypermetabolic demands. [^1_17][^1_20][^1_25]

***

### Diagnosis

Diagnosis requires demonstrating **low muscle mass** plus **low strength and/or poor performance**, but ICU constraints often necessitate adapted approaches.

#### A. Clinical Assessment (when feasible)

1. **Muscle Strength**
    - **Handgrip strength**: <27 kg (men), <16 kg (women) – EWGSOP2. [^1_4]
    - **MRC sum score**: <48/60 indicates ICU-AW (6 muscle groups, 0–5 each side). [^1_7][^1_4]
2. **Physical Performance**
    - Gait speed (not feasible in most ICU patients).
    - Chair stand, SPPB – usually post-ICU.

#### B. Muscle Mass / Quantity Assessment

1. **Computed Tomography (CT)**
    - **Gold standard** in many ICU studies.
    - Measure **skeletal muscle cross-sectional area (CSA)** at **L3 vertebral level**.
    - Common cutoffs:
        - Men: <170 cm² (or <55 cm²/m² height-normalized).
        - Women: <110 cm² (or <39 cm²/m²).
        - Psoas muscle area (PMA) also used. [^1_26][^1_18][^1_22]
    - Advantages: Often already available for other indications; objective.
    - Limitations: Radiation, not always available, single time point.
2. **Ultrasound**
    - Measure **quadriceps muscle thickness (MT)** or rectus femoris thickness.
    - Cutoffs vary; e.g., RF thickness <16 mm (men), <20 mm (women) in some studies. [^1_9]
    - Low MT + low MRC score → ICU sarcopenia diagnosis. [^1_7][^1_8]
    - Advantages: Bedside, repeatable, no radiation.
    - Limitations: Operator-dependent, edema can confound.
3. **DEXA / BIA**
    - **DEXA**: Appendicular lean mass index (ALMI) <7.0 kg/m² (men), <5.5 kg/m² (women). [^1_4]
    - **BIA**: Less validated in critically ill (fluid shifts).
    - Usually not practical in acute ICU setting.
4. **Anthropometry**
    - **Calf circumference**, **mid-arm circumference**, **BMI**.
    - Lower values associated with sarcopenia risk (e.g., calf circumference strongly predictive). [^1_13]
    - Useful as screening, not diagnostic.

#### C. Biomarkers (Emerging / Research)

- **Creatinine height index**, **3-methylhistidine**, **myostatin**, inflammatory markers.
- Not yet standard for clinical diagnosis.


#### Practical ICU Diagnostic Algorithm (simplified)

1. **Screen**: Age, frailty, low BMI/calf circumference, chronic disease.
2. **If CT available**: Measure L3 muscle CSA → classify as sarcopenic/non-sarcopenic.
3. **If CT not available**: Use bedside ultrasound (quadriceps MT).
4. **When patient is testable**: Add MRC score / handgrip to confirm functional impairment.
5. **Diagnosis**: Low muscle mass + low strength/performance → sarcopenia.

***

### Consequences / Clinical Outcomes

Sarcopenia in the ICU is a **powerful independent predictor of adverse outcomes**:

#### 1. Mortality

- **In-hospital mortality**: OR ~1.99 (95% CI 1.45–2.73). [^1_27]
- **30-day mortality**: OR ~2.08 (95% CI 1.36–3.19). [^1_27]
- **1-year mortality**: OR ~3.23 (95% CI 2.08–5.00). [^1_27][^1_8]
- In sepsis: OR for in-hospital mortality ~1.54 (95% CI 1.03–2.30). [^1_12]
- Low muscle mass (CT) independently associated with 30-day and in-hospital mortality. [^1_18][^1_22]


#### 2. Prolonged ICU and Hospital Stay

- Significantly **longer ICU LOS** (e.g., 18.7 vs 6.4 days in trauma patients). [^1_14]
- **Longer hospital LOS** by ~5.37 days (weighted mean difference). [^1_12]
- Higher resource utilization and cost.


#### 3. Ventilator-Related Outcomes

- **Difficult-to-wean (DtW)**: Sarcopenia is an independent risk factor (OR significant in multivariate models). [^1_15]
- **Prolonged mechanical ventilation** trend. [^1_12]
- **Ventilator-induced diaphragmatic dysfunction** in up to 80% of ICU-AW patients. [^1_16]
- Impaired respiratory muscle strength → weaning failure, reintubation.


#### 4. Functional and Long-Term Outcomes

- **ICU-AW**: Persistent weakness at discharge; predicts 5-year mortality. [^1_16]
- **Physical disability**, reduced mobility, falls, fractures. [^1_1][^1_27]
- **Dysphagia**: ~10% of ICU survivors; associated with higher 28- and 90-day mortality. [^1_16]
- **Reduced quality of life**, prolonged rehabilitation, institutionalization.


#### 5. Complications

- Higher rates of:
    - Sepsis and resistant infections.
    - Ventilator-associated pneumonia.
    - Pressure ulcers.
    - Neuropathy. [^1_3][^1_16]

***

### Differential Diagnosis

Consider and differentiate from:

- **Pre-existing neuromuscular disease** (e.g., myasthenia gravis, ALS, polymyositis).
- **Electrolyte disturbances** (hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia, hypomagnesemia).
- **Drug-induced myopathy** (statins, colchicine, prolonged NMBAs).
- **Endocrine disorders** (thyroid dysfunction, Cushing's).
- **Severe malnutrition / cachexia** (distinct but overlapping).
- **Post-surgical or post-traumatic immobilization atrophy** (may coexist).

***

### Scoring Systems / Prognostic Tools

No single sarcopenia-specific ICU score exists, but several tools integrate muscle status:

- **MRC Sum Score**: <48/60 → ICU-AW. [^1_7][^1_4]
- **CT-based muscle indices**:
    - L3 skeletal muscle index (SMI).
    - Psoas muscle area (PMA).
- **Frailty scales** (e.g., Clinical Frailty Scale) often correlate with sarcopenia.
- **APACHE II / SOFA**: Higher scores associated with sarcopenia and worse outcomes. [^1_15][^1_21]

***

### Management

Management is **multimodal**: prevention, early recognition, nutrition, mobilization, and minimizing iatrogenic contributors. Evidence is still evolving; most recommendations are based on observational data, small RCTs, and expert consensus.

#### 1. Prevention and Early Recognition

- **Screen on ICU admission**:
    - Age, frailty, BMI, calf circumference, comorbidities.
    - Review prior CT for opportunistic muscle assessment.
- **High-risk patients**: Elderly, septic, post-major surgery, trauma, prolonged expected stay.
- **Serial monitoring**: Ultrasound muscle thickness, functional assessments when feasible.


#### 2. Nutritional Management

- **Energy**: 25–30 kcal/kg/day (adjusted for obesity, fluid status).
- **Protein**:
    - General ICU: 1.2–2.0 g/kg/day.
    - Sarcopenia/high catabolism: target **1.5–2.0 g/kg/day** (or higher in selected cases). [^1_25]
    - Prefer **high-quality protein** (rich in leucine: whey, eggs, meat).
- **Route**:
    - Early enteral nutrition (within 24–48 h) preferred.
    - Bolus or continuous feeding; ensure adequate protein delivery.
- **Supplements**:
    - **Branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs)**, especially leucine, to stimulate mTOR.
    - **Omega-3 fatty acids** (anti-inflammatory).
    - **Vitamin D** (if deficient; supports muscle function).
    - **HMB (β-hydroxy-β-methylbutyrate)**: Emerging evidence in elderly/sarcopenia; limited ICU data. [^1_25]
- **Glycemic control**:
    - Target glucose 140–180 mg/dL (avoid both hypo- and hyperglycemia).
    - Insulin therapy as needed; hyperglycemia worsens muscle catabolism. [^1_19][^1_20]


#### 3. Early Mobilization and Rehabilitation

- **Early mobilization** (within 24–72 h if hemodynamically stable):
    - Passive/active range-of-motion exercises.
    - Bed cycling, sitting on edge of bed, standing, walking as tolerated.
- **In-bed cycle ergometry**: Improves muscle mass and function in some trials.
- **Neuromuscular electrical stimulation (NMES)**:
    - Can preserve muscle mass/strength in sedated patients.
    - Shown to reduce quadriceps wasting in some studies. [^1_25]
- **Blood flow-restricted exercise**: Emerging technique; limited ICU data.
- **Multidisciplinary approach**: Physiotherapists, occupational therapists, nurses.


#### 4. Minimizing Iatrogenic Contributors

- **Sedation**:
    - Use lightest effective sedation.
    - Daily sedation interruption where appropriate.
    - Prefer agents with shorter half-lives.
- **Neuromuscular blockers**:
    - Use only when clearly indicated (e.g., severe ARDS).
    - Limit duration; monitor with neuromuscular monitoring.
- **Corticosteroids**:
    - Use lowest effective dose and shortest duration.
    - Avoid unnecessary high-dose/prolonged courses.
- **Glycemic control**: Avoid prolonged hyperglycemia. [^1_19][^1_20]


#### 5. Pharmacological / Emerging Therapies

Currently, **no drug is approved specifically for ICU sarcopenia**, but several strategies are under investigation:

- **Anabolic agents**:
    - Testosterone, growth hormone, IGF-1: theoretical benefit but safety/efficacy concerns in critically ill.
- **Myostatin inhibitors**: Experimental; may reduce muscle breakdown.
- **Anti-inflammatory/immunomodulatory agents**: Targeting NF-κB, cytokines; not yet standard.
- **Mitochondrial targeted therapies**: CoQ10, antioxidants; limited evidence.
- **Beta-blockers**: Some data suggest reduced muscle catabolism; not standard for sarcopenia.

At present, **nutrition + mobilization + minimizing catabolic insults** remain the cornerstone. [^1_25]

#### 6. Post-ICU Rehabilitation

- **Early referral** to rehabilitation services.
- **Progressive resistance training**.
- **Protein supplementation** continued post-ICU.
- **Monitoring**: Strength, gait speed, functional status at follow-up.

***

### Landmark Trials and Key Evidence

- **Systematic reviews/meta-analyses**:
    - Sarcopenia associated with **increased mortality** (OR ~2.28 overall; higher for 1-year mortality). [^1_27]
    - In sepsis: higher mortality, longer LOS, trend to prolonged ventilation. [^1_12]
- **Observational ICU cohorts**:
    - Low CT muscle area → higher 30-day and in-hospital mortality. [^1_18][^1_22]
    - Sarcopenia predicts **difficult-to-wean** and **ICU mortality** in surgical ICU patients. [^1_15]
    - Low quadriceps muscle thickness + low MRC score → higher 1-year mortality. [^1_8]
- **ICU-AW literature**:
    - Muscle strength at ICU discharge predicts **5-year mortality**. [^1_16]
    - Diaphragmatic dysfunction and dysphagia strongly linked to worse outcomes. [^1_16]

***

### Guideline Recommendations (Synthesized)

While no guideline is exclusively for "ICU sarcopenia," recommendations can be synthesized from ESPEN, SCCM, and EWGSOP:

- **ESPEN ICU nutrition guidelines**:
    - Early enteral feeding.
    - High protein intake (1.3–2.0 g/kg/day).
    - Avoid overfeeding; monitor tolerance.
- **EWGSOP2**:
    - Emphasize **strength** as primary diagnostic criterion.
    - Recommend **resistance exercise + protein** as core treatment.
- **SCCM/ACCM**:
    - Early mobilization.
    - Light sedation strategies.
    - Glycemic control.

***

### Key Clinical Pearls (Exam-Oriented)

1. **Definition**: Sarcopenia = low muscle mass + low strength/performance; in ICU, often acute and overlapping with ICU-AW.
2. **Pathogenesis**: Catabolic storm + immobilization + inflammation → UPS activation, mitochondrial dysfunction, myosin loss.
3. **Diagnosis in ICU**: CT (L3 muscle CSA) or ultrasound (quadriceps thickness) + MRC score when feasible.
4. **Risk factors**: Sepsis, age, steroids, NMBAs, hyperglycemia, prolonged ventilation, low baseline muscle mass.
5. **Outcomes**: Strongly associated with higher mortality, prolonged ventilation, difficult weaning, longer ICU/hospital stay, long-term disability.
6. **Management triad**:
    - Adequate **protein-energy nutrition** (1.5–2 g/kg/day protein).
    - **Early mobilization/rehab** (including NMES if needed).
    - **Minimize catabolic insults** (sedation, steroids, NMBAs, hyperglycemia).
7. **Prognosis**: Sarcopenia is not just a marker but a **modifiable risk factor**; early recognition and intervention can improve outcomes.
