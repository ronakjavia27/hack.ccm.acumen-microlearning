
## Advance Medical Directive (AMD) in ICU

An Advance Medical Directive (AMD), or “living will”, is a written document in which a competent adult records their wishes about future medical treatment, especially life‑sustaining measures, in case they later become unable to communicate. In India, it is legally valid under the Supreme Court’s *Common Cause* (2018) judgment and subsequent 2023 procedural modifications, and was first practically applied in the **Harish Rana (2026)** case. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3]

***

## 1. Legal Basis (Very Short)

- **Article 21 (Right to life)** includes **right to die with dignity**.
- **Common Cause v. Union of India (2018)**: SC recognised **passive euthanasia** and **AMDs** as constitutional. [^1_1]
- **2023 MoHFW guidelines**: Simplified the procedure for AMD implementation. [^1_2]
- **Harish Rana v. Union of India (2026)**: First case where SC actually **allowed withdrawal of life support** under this framework, clarifying key points like CANH as “medical treatment” and “best interest” standard. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6][^1_7][^1_3]

***

## 2. What Is an Advance Medical Directive?

An AMD is a **written, voluntary, specific** document in which a person:

- States which **life‑sustaining treatments** they do or do not want if they become:
    - Terminally ill, or
    - In an irreversible unconscious/vegetative state, or
    - In an end‑stage condition with no hope of recovery. [^1_1][^1_2]
- May appoint a **nominee/surrogate decision‑maker** to speak on their behalf. [^1_8][^1_9]

**Key features (for exam):**

- Must be **voluntary**, **clear**, and **specific**.
- Signed in presence of **two witnesses** (usually one should be a doctor).
- Preferably **registered** (with a notary/JMFC/local health authority) and copies shared with family and treating hospital. [^1_8][^1_9][^1_2]

***

## 3. When Does an AMD Apply in ICU?

An AMD comes into play only when **all** of the following are true:

1. Patient is **adult** and previously **competent** when making the AMD.
2. Now **lacks capacity** to take medical decisions (e.g., coma, PVS, severe brain injury).
3. Suffering from:
    - **Terminal illness**, or
    - **Irreversible unconscious state** (e.g., persistent vegetative state), or
    - **End‑stage condition** with no realistic chance of recovery. [^1_1][^1_2]
4. Proposed treatment is **life‑sustaining** (ventilator, dialysis, vasopressors, CPR, etc.) or **clinically assisted nutrition \& hydration (CANH)**. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]

If the patient is **conscious and competent**, their **current consent/refusal** overrides any prior AMD.

***

## 4. Step‑by‑Step Process in ICU (With AMD)

This is the core “process” question for exams. Think in **two layers**: hospital board + district/collector board + JMFC oversight. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_10]

### Step 1: AMD is brought to notice

- Family/nominee/records show an **existing AMD**.
- Treating physician **verifies authenticity** with the **Jurisdictional Judicial Magistrate First Class (JMFC)** (or relevant registering authority). [^1_1][^1_2]


### Step 2: Inform family \& explain prognosis

- Doctor explains:
    - Diagnosis, prognosis, treatment options.
    - What AMD says (e.g., “no ventilator”, “no CPR”, “no artificial feeding”).
- Family’s understanding is documented. [^1_2]


### Step 3: First (Hospital) Medical Board

- Constituted by the **hospital**:
    - Head of treating department + **at least 3 specialists** (e.g., medicine, neurology, cardiology, nephrology, etc.).
    - Each with **≥20 years’ experience** (as per original Common Cause guidelines; some later simplifications exist but for exams, quote 20 years). [^1_1][^1_2]
- Board:
    - Examines patient.
    - Reviews medical records.
    - Confirms that condition is **terminal/irreversible/end‑stage**.
    - Decides whether AMD instructions should be followed.
- Their decision = **preliminary opinion**. [^1_1][^1_2]


### Step 4: Inform District Authorities \& Second Medical Board

- Hospital informs the **District Collector / CDMO**.
- Collector forms a **second medical board**:
    - Chaired by **Chief District Medical Officer (CDMO)**.
    - Includes **3 expert doctors** not from the treating hospital. [^1_1][^1_2]
- This board:
    - Re‑examines the patient and records.
    - Confirms or disagrees with the first board’s opinion.


### Step 5: JMFC Visit \& Final Authorization

- If **both boards agree** that AMD should be implemented:
    - They inform the **JMFC**.
    - JMFC visits the patient (or reviews thoroughly) and examines all aspects.
    - JMFC **authorises implementation** of the decision to withhold/withdraw life‑sustaining treatment as per AMD. [^1_1][^1_2]


### Step 6: Implementation in ICU

- Once JMFC authorises:
    - ICU team **withholds/withdraws** specified treatments (e.g., does not start new vasopressors, does not re‑intubate, withdraws ventilator, stops dialysis, etc.).
    - **Palliative care** is ensured: pain relief, comfort measures, psychosocial support to family.
    - All steps are **documented** in detail (time, drugs stopped, vitals, family counselling). [^1_2][^1_10]


### Step 7: If Boards Disagree or Family Objects

- If **second board refuses** or there is dispute:
    - Executor/family/treating doctor can approach the **High Court** under **Article 226**. [^1_1][^1_2]
- HC can give **final direction** on whether to follow AMD.

***

## 5. Process When There Is NO AMD (But Similar Situation)

Often asked as a variant: “No living will, but patient in PVS/terminal – what to do?”

- Hospital forms **first medical board** (same composition).
- Board discusses with **family/nominee**, explains prognosis.
- If family agrees in writing to **withhold/withdraw** futile treatment:
    - Form **second (district) medical board** for confirmation.
    - Then approach **JMFC** for authorisation.
- If disagreement → **High Court**. [^1_1][^1_2]

This is essentially **non‑voluntary passive euthanasia** under judicial supervision.

***

## 6. Harish Rana (2026) – Key Outcomes for Exams

**Case:** *Harish Rana v. Union of India*, decided on **11 March 2026** by a two‑judge SC bench (Justices J.B. Pardiwala \& K.V. Viswanathan). Harish, injured in 2013, had been in a **permanent vegetative state for 13 years**; his father sought permission to withdraw life support. [^1_4][^1_11][^1_5][^1_6][^1_10][^1_7][^1_3]

### A. First Court‑Sanctioned Passive Euthanasia in Practice

- This is widely reported as the **first actual implementation** of the 2018 *Common Cause* framework: SC **allowed withdrawal of life‑sustaining treatment** for a specific patient. [^1_6][^1_10][^1_3]


### B. CANH = Medical Treatment, Not Basic Care

- **Clinically Assisted Nutrition and Hydration (CANH)** (tube feeding, IV fluids for nutrition) was held to be **“medical treatment”**, not ordinary feeding or basic care. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6][^1_7]
- Therefore, CANH can be **withheld or withdrawn** if:
    - Medical boards certify it is **not in the patient’s best interest**, and
    - Procedural safeguards are followed. [^1_4][^1_5]

Exam point: This distinguishes **spoon/oral feeding** (basic care) from **tube/IV feeding** (medical treatment).

### C. “Best Interest” Standard Emphasised

- SC applied the **“best interest”** test from *Common Cause*:
    - Is continuing treatment **benefiting** the patient, or merely **prolonging dying**?
    - In Harish’s case, condition was **irreversible**, no improvement despite years of care → continuing CANH was **not in his best interest**. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_7][^1_3]


### D. Waiver of 30‑Day Reconsideration Period

- Earlier guidelines had a **30‑day “cooling/reconsideration” period** before implementing withdrawal.
- In Harish Rana, SC **waived** this period to avoid **unnecessary prolongation of suffering**, allowing **immediate implementation** once boards approved. [^1_3]

Exam point: This shows SC can **relax procedural timelines** in clear, prolonged vegetative cases to prevent further indignity.

### E. Reinforcement of Existing Procedure, Not New Law

- The judgment **did not create new law**, but:
    - **Applied** the 2018 + 2023 framework.
    - Clarified that **CANH is withdrawable**.
    - Showed how “best interest” works in a real PVS case. [^1_6][^1_7][^1_3]

***

## 7. Practical ICU Checklist (For Implementation)

Useful for practical/clinical questions:

1. **Check for AMD** in records / ask family / check registry.
2. If AMD exists:
    - Verify with **JMFC**.
    - Form **hospital medical board** → preliminary opinion.
3. Inform **Collector/CDMO** → form **second medical board**.
4. If both boards agree → inform **JMFC** for visit and **authorisation**.
5. Document:
    - Boards’ opinions, JMFC order, family counselling, palliative plan.
6. **Withdraw/withhold** specified treatments gradually and humanely.
7. Provide **palliative care** and psychosocial support.
8. If any dispute → **High Court**. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_10][^1_3]

***

## 8. Common Exam‑Style One‑Liners

- AMD is valid in India under **Article 21** as part of **right to die with dignity** (*Common Cause*, 2018). [^1_1]
- AMD applies only in **terminal/irreversible/end‑stage** conditions when patient lacks capacity. [^1_1][^1_2]
- Implementation needs **two medical boards** (hospital + district) + **JMFC authorisation**. [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Harish Rana (2026)**: First case where SC actually **allowed withdrawal of life support**; held **CANH = medical treatment**, can be withdrawn if not in patient’s **best interest**; **30‑day period waived**. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6][^1_7][^1_3]
