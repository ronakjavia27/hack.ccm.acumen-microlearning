
## VV ECMO Troubleshooting:

VV ECMO troubleshooting follows a systematic approach: first assess the **patient** (oxygenation, ventilation, hemodynamics), then the **circuit** (flow, pressures, oxygenator function), and finally the **cannulae** (position, patency, recirculation). [^1_1][^1_2] The most common problems are hypoxemia (often from recirculation or low ECMO flow-to-cardiac output ratio), low circuit flow (from drainage issues or hypovolemia), and membrane lung dysfunction. [^1_3][^1_4]

***

## 1. Initial Assessment Framework

### The "Patient–Circuit–Cannula" Approach

When a VV ECMO patient deteriorates, systematically evaluate:

**Patient:**

- SpO₂, hemodynamics, mental status
- Ventilator settings and synchrony
- Signs of sepsis, bleeding, or new pathology [^1_2][^1_5]

**Circuit:**

- Flow (L/min), RPM, pump function
- Pressures: Pven (pre-pump), PInt (internal), PArt (return), ΔP (transmembrane)
- Oxygenator appearance (color change, clots)
- Sweep gas function and FiO₂ setting [^1_2][^1_6]

**Cannula:**

- Position on CXR/echo
- Kinks, external compression, dislodgement
- Separation distance between drainage and reinfusion ports [^1_4][^1_7]

## 2. Hypoxemia on VV ECMO (SpO₂ <80–85%)

### Differential Diagnosis

| Cause | Mechanism | Key Clues |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Recirculation** | Oxygenated blood re-enters drainage cannula without systemic circulation | High SvO₂ (>75%), SpO₂–SvO₂ gradient <10%, both cannulae blood bright red [^1_4][^1_2] |
| **Low ECMO flow / CO ratio** | ECMO flow <60% of cardiac output; too much shunted blood bypasses oxygenator | Low ECMO flow, high cardiac output (fever, sepsis, agitation), SvO₂ normal/low [^1_1][^1_5] |
| **Oxygenator dysfunction** | Thrombus, plasma leakage, membrane failure | ΔP >50–60 mmHg, post-oxygenator PaO₂ <250 mmHg on 100% FiO₂, visible clots [^1_2][^1_6] |
| **Low SvO₂** | Anemia, high O₂ consumption, shivering, fever | SvO₂ <60%, high lactate, fever, agitation [^1_2][^1_6] |
| **Ventilator/circuit disconnection** | Sweep gas off, kinked tubing, ventilator failure | Check FdO₂, sweep flow, gas line connections [^1_2][^1_8] |
| **Native lung deterioration** | Worsening shunt, new consolidation, pneumothorax | CXR changes, worsening compliance, new infiltrates [^1_2] |

### Stepwise Management of Hypoxemia [^1_1][^1_2][^1_7]

**Step 1: Basic System Check**

- Confirm sweep gas is ON and connected
- FdO₂ set to 100%
- Ventilator functioning (not in standby)
- No kinks in gas or blood lines [^1_2][^1_8]

**Step 2: Assess Oxygenator Function**

- Check ΔP (transmembrane pressure): >50–60 mmHg suggests dysfunction [^1_2][^1_6]
- Inspect oxygenator: blood should be dark entering, bright red exiting
- Check post-oxygenator PaO₂: should be >250–600 mmHg on 100% FiO₂ [^1_2]
- If ΔP rising or PaO₂ low despite high sweep → **oxygenator failure** → prepare for circuit change [^1_2][^1_8]

**Step 3: Evaluate for Recirculation**

- **Signs:**
    - SvO₂ >75% (especially if rising) [^1_2]
    - SpO₂–SvO₂ gradient <10% (or negative if SvO₂ > SpO₂) [^1_2]
    - Both drainage and return blood appear bright red [^1_4]
    - Paradoxical **improvement in SpO₂ when flow is reduced** (diagnostic trial) [^1_2]
- **Causes:**
    - Cannula tips too close (<8–10 cm apart) [^1_4][^1_7]
    - Dual-lumen cannula malpositioned (outflow jet not directed at tricuspid valve) [^1_4]
    - High pump speed increasing recirculation fraction [^1_2]
- **Management:**
    - Reduce pump speed (may improve SpO₂ by reducing recirculation) [^1_2]
    - Reposition cannula: ideal separation 12–15 cm for dual cannula [^1_4][^1_7]
    - For dual-lumen cannula: confirm under echo/fluoro that outflow jet is directed toward tricuspid valve [^1_4]
    - Add second drainage cannula if flow inadequate at lower RPM [^1_4]

**Step 4: Assess ECMO Flow / Cardiac Output Ratio**

- Target: ECMO flow >60% of cardiac output [^1_1][^1_2]
- If SvO₂ is low (<60%) and SpO₂ low → ECMO flow may be insufficient relative to CO
- **Causes of high CO:** sepsis, fever, agitation, pain, thyrotoxicosis [^1_3][^1_5]
- **Management:**
    - Increase ECMO flow (up to 6–7 L/min if tolerated) [^1_2]
    - Treat underlying cause: antipyretics, sedation, analgesia, beta-blockers (esmolol) [^1_1][^1_5]
    - Avoid hyperthermia (increases metabolic demand) [^1_1]

**Step 5: Optimize SvO₂**

- If SvO₂ <60%, systemic oxygenation will be limited regardless of ECMO flow [^1_2][^1_6]
- **Causes of low SvO₂:**
    - Anemia (Hb <7–8 g/dL)
    - High O₂ consumption (fever, shivering, agitation, seizures)
    - Low cardiac output (cardiogenic shock, RV failure)
- **Management:**
    - Transfuse PRBC if Hb <7–8 g/dL (target >8 g/dL in severe hypoxemia) [^1_2][^1_5]
    - Treat fever, shivering, agitation (sedation, paralysis if needed) [^1_1][^1_2]
    - Optimize cardiac output (inotropes if RV dysfunction) [^1_1]

**Step 6: Evaluate Native Lung and Ventilator**

- Check CXR for new infiltrates, pneumothorax, worsening edema
- Assess ventilator mechanics: rising plateau pressure, decreasing compliance
- **Optimize ventilator:**
    - PEEP ≥10 cmH₂O (prevent atelectasis, but avoid excessive PEEP impairing venous return) [^1_2]
    - FiO₂ 30–60% (avoid oxygen toxicity, but can increase to 60–70% if needed) [^1_2]
    - Plateau pressure ≤25 cmH₂O, driving pressure ≤10–15 cmH₂O [^1_2]
    - Tidal volume 4–6 mL/kg PBW (lung-protective ventilation) [^1_1][^1_2]
- Consider prone positioning if feasible (improves V/Q matching) [^1_1]

## 3. Low Circuit Flow (<2–3 L/min or >1–2 L/min below baseline)

### Differential Diagnosis

| Cause | Mechanism | Key Clues |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Hypovolemia / inadequate preload** | Insufficient venous return to drainage cannula | Low Pven (<−100 mmHg), "chattering" in venous line, patient flat on exam, low CVP [^1_3][^1_2][^1_5] |
| **Cannula malposition** | Drainage port in hepatic vein, IVC obstruction, tip against vessel wall | CXR/echo shows malposition, sudden flow drop after patient movement [^1_2][^1_5] |
| **Cannula/circuit obstruction** | Kink, external compression, thrombus in cannula or tubing | Visible kink, high Pven, chattering, clot visible in circuit [^1_2][^1_9] |
| **Excessive pump speed** | High RPM causing vessel collapse around drainage port | Pven very negative (<−100 mmHg), chattering, flow drops despite high RPM [^1_2][^1_5] |
| **Abdominal compartment syndrome / tamponade** | Increased intra-abdominal or intrathoracic pressure impairing venous return | High bladder pressure (>20 mmHg), tense abdomen, hypotension, elevated CVP [^1_2][^1_5] |
| **Oxygenator thrombosis** | Clot in membrane increasing resistance | High ΔP (>50–60 mmHg), high PInt, visible clots [^1_2][^1_6] |

### Stepwise Management of Low Flow [^1_2][^1_5]

**Step 1: Confirm Drainage Insufficiency**

- Check Pven (pre-pump pressure): should be >−100 mmHg [^1_2]
    - If Pven <−100 mmHg → **drainage insufficiency** (not return obstruction)
    - If Pven normal but PArt high → **return obstruction** (see below)
- Look for "chattering" (visible vibration in venous line) → indicates vessel collapse around drainage port [^1_2][^1_5]

**Step 2: Immediate Maneuvers**

- **Reduce pump speed** by 100–200 RPM increments until chattering stops and flow stabilizes [^1_2][^1_5]
    - Paradoxically, reducing RPM may **increase** flow by relieving suction on the vessel [^1_2]
- Place patient in **Trendelenburg** or passive leg raise → if flow improves, suggests hypovolemia [^1_2][^1_5]
- Give **fluid bolus** (250–500 mL crystalloid/colloid) if fluid responsive [^1_2][^1_5]

**Step 3: Inspect Circuit and Cannula**

- Check for kinks in venous line, external compression (patient lying on tubing)
- Examine cannula insertion site for kinking, dislodgement
- Inspect circuit for visible clots (especially in pump head, oxygenator) [^1_2][^1_9]
- If clot suspected → check ΔP, pre/post-oxygenator pressures [^1_2]

**Step 4: Evaluate Cannula Position**

- **CXR:** check if drainage cannula tip is in hepatic vein (common cause of low flow) [^1_2][^1_5]
- **Echo/POCUS:** confirm tip position in RA/IVC, rule out tamponade, pneumothorax [^1_2][^1_5]
- If malpositioned → reposition under fluoroscopic/echo guidance [^1_4]

**Step 5: Assess for Abdominal Compartment Syndrome / Tamponade**

- Check bladder pressure if abdomen tense, oliguria, hypotension [^1_2][^1_5]
    - Bladder pressure >20 mmHg → consider decompression
- Echo for tamponade (RA collapse, pericardial effusion) [^1_2]
- CXR/POCUS for tension pneumothorax [^1_2]

**Step 6: Persistent Low Flow**

- If flow remains inadequate despite optimization:
    - Add **second drainage cannula** (e.g., femoral vein) [^1_4][^1_2]
    - Consider switching to **bicaval dual-lumen cannula** (Avalon) under imaging guidance [^1_4]
    - Increase pump speed cautiously (monitor for hemolysis, chattering) [^1_2]

## 4. Return Obstruction (High PArt, High PInt, Normal ΔP)

### Causes

- Return cannula kinked or externally compressed
- Cannula malposition (e.g., in hepatic vein, aortic dissection in VA ECMO)
- Thrombus in return cannula
- Vessel too small for cannula size [^1_2][^1_6]


### Management

- Inspect return line for kinks, external pressure
- Check cannula position on CXR/echo
- If thrombus suspected → check ΔP, pre/post-oxygenator blood gases
- May need to upsize cannula or add additional return cannula [^1_2]

***

## 5. Oxygenator (Membrane Lung) Dysfunction

### Signs [^1_2][^1_6][^1_8]

- **ΔP >50–60 mmHg** (or rising trend)
- **ΔP >100 mmHg** strongly suggests failure
- Post-oxygenator PaO₂ <250 mmHg on 100% FiO₂ and adequate sweep
- <10 mmHg pCO₂ gradient across oxygenator despite high sweep
- Visible clots in oxygenator
- Blood in arterial limb not bright red
- Plasma leakage into gas exhaust line (blood in gas tubing) → **emergency** [^1_2]


### Management

- **Sigh maneuver:** increase sweep to 10–15 L/min for 10 seconds to clear moisture (won't fix clots) [^1_2]
- Increase anticoagulation if worsening thrombosis (check anti-Xa, aPTT, TEG) [^1_2]
- **Replace oxygenator/circuit** if:
    - ΔP >100 mmHg
    - Post-oxygenator PaO₂ <150–200 mmHg despite 100% FiO₂
    - Plasma leakage
    - Visible large clots impairing flow [^1_2][^1_8]

## 6. Recirculation: Detailed Approach

### Pathophysiology

Oxygenated blood from reinfusion cannula is immediately drained back into the circuit without passing through systemic circulation → effective ECMO flow decreases, SpO₂ falls despite high circuit flow. [^1_1][^1_4]

### Diagnosis [^1_4][^1_2]

- **SvO₂ >75%** (especially if rising while SpO₂ falls)
- **SpO₂–SvO₂ gradient <10%** (or negative)
- Both drainage and return blood appear bright red
- **Paradoxical improvement in SpO₂ when flow is reduced** (key diagnostic maneuver)
- CXR: cannula tips <8–10 cm apart [^1_4][^1_7]


### Quantification (if needed) [^1_1]

$$
\text{Recirculation (\%)} = \frac{\text{SpreO}_2 - \text{SvO}_2}{\text{SpostO}_2 - \text{SvO}_2} \times 100
$$

- SpreO₂ = pre-oxygenator saturation
- SpostO₂ = post-oxygenator saturation
- SvO₂ = true mixed venous saturation (from central line or PA catheter)


### Management [^1_4][^1_2]

- **Reduce pump speed** (decreases recirculation fraction, may improve SpO₂)
- **Reposition cannulae:**
    - Dual cannula: increase separation to 12–15 cm [^1_4][^1_7]
    - Dual-lumen (Avalon): confirm under echo/fluoro that outflow jet is directed at tricuspid valve, not toward IVC/SVC drainage ports [^1_4]
- **Add second drainage cannula** (e.g., femoral) to achieve same flow at lower RPM
- **Increase cardiac output** (if low): inotropes, fluid, treat RV failure [^1_1]

## 7. Sepsis in VV ECMO Patients

### Key Points

- **Fever may be absent** (ECMO circuit controls temperature) [^1_1]
- Clues: hemodynamic instability, increasing fluid/pressors, worsening oxygenation, rising lactate [^1_3][^1_1]
- Common sources: VAP, catheter-related bloodstream infection, urinary tract, line sites [^1_1]


### Workup

- CBC, lactate, procalcitonin
- Blood cultures (from central line and peripheral)
- Sputum, urine cultures
- CXR, consider CT if source unclear [^1_1]


### Management

- Early broad-spectrum antibiotics (adjust per culture)
- Source control (remove infected lines, drain abscesses)
- Optimize ECMO flow (sepsis increases CO and O₂ consumption)
- Consider sedation, paralysis, cooling to reduce metabolic demand [^1_1][^1_5]

***

## 8. Anticoagulation and Bleeding

### Monitoring [^1_1][^1_2]

- **aPTT:** target 50–70 seconds (or per institutional protocol; EOLIA used 40–55 s) [^1_1]
- **Anti-Xa:** target 0.3–0.7 IU/mL [^1_1]
- **TEG:** R-time 2–3× normal [^1_1]
- Daily: platelets, fibrinogen, PT/INR, D-dimer
- Check for hemolysis: free Hb, LDH, haptoglobin (if sepsis excluded) [^1_1]


### Bleeding Management

- Hold heparin if major bleeding; maintain flow >3.5 L/min to prevent circuit thrombosis [^1_1][^1_2]
- Transfuse:
    - Platelets >50,000/μL if bleeding
    - Fibrinogen >100–150 mg/dL
    - Hb >7–8 g/dL (restrictive strategy acceptable if perfusion adequate) [^1_1][^1_2]
- Consider epsilon-aminocaproic acid for hyperfibrinolysis (per TEG) [^1_1]
- Switch to bivalirudin/argatroban if HIT suspected [^1_1][^1_2]

## 9. Right Ventricular Dysfunction on VV ECMO

### Pathophysiology

- Hypoxemia, hypercapnia, acidosis → pulmonary vasoconstriction → RV strain
- VV ECMO corrects gas exchange, but RV may still fail if PVR remains elevated [^1_1]


### Diagnosis

- Echo: RV dilation, septal flattening, reduced TAPSE
- Hemodynamics: elevated CVP, low cardiac output, hypotension
- Clinical: rising lactate, oliguria, worsening oxygenation [^1_1]


### Management

- Optimize oxygenation, ventilation, acid-base (VV ECMO should help)
- Inhaled pulmonary vasodilators (epoprostenol, NO)
- Inotropes (dobutamine, milrinone)
- Diuresis if volume overloaded
- If severe RV failure persists → consider **VAV ECMO** (add arterial cannula) or convert to VA ECMO [^1_1]

***

## 10. Ventilator Management on VV ECMO

### Lung-Protective "Rest" Settings [^1_1][^1_2]

- **Mode:** PCV or VCV (low rate)
- **Tidal volume:** 4–6 mL/kg PBW
- **Respiratory rate:** 5–10/min (minimize mechanical power)
- **PEEP:** 10–15 cmH₂O (prevent atelectasis, avoid excessive PEEP impairing venous return)
- **Plateau pressure:** ≤25 cmH₂O
- **Driving pressure:** ≤10–15 cmH₂O
- **FiO₂:** 30–50% (increase to 60–70% if needed for oxygenation) [^1_1][^1_2]


### CO₂ Management

- **Sweep gas:** primary method for CO₂ clearance
    - Initial: 50–100% of ECMO flow (e.g., 3–5 L/min) [^1_1][^1_2]
    - Titrate to PaCO₂/pH (avoid rapid correction if chronically hypercapnic) [^1_2]
- Ventilator rate can be minimized (even 5–10/min) since sweep handles CO₂ [^1_2]


### Special Considerations

- Avoid abrupt PaCO₂ reduction in chronic hypercapnia (correct by ≤50% in first 4 hours) [^1_2]
- Increasing sweep reduces respiratory drive → may decrease sedation requirements [^1_2]

## 11. Emergency Scenarios

### Cardiac Arrest on VV ECMO [^1_2][^1_9]

- **VV ECMO does NOT support circulation** → cardiac output depends on native heart
- **Immediate actions:**
    - Start ACLS/CPR
    - Increase ventilator FiO₂ to 100%, PEEP
    - Hand-bag if ventilator failure suspected
    - Continue ECMO flow (provides oxygenation, but no perfusion without cardiac output)
    - If arrest persists → consider conversion to **VA ECMO** [^1_2][^1_9]


### Pump Failure / Circuit Flow Arrest [^1_2][^1_9][^1_8]

- **Immediate:**
    - Hand-crank pump (if available)
    - Clamp both venous and arterial lines near patient (prevent air embolism, blood loss)
    - Call perfusion/bypass team
    - Prepare for circuit exchange
- **If air in circuit:**
    - Clamp lines, tilt patient head down (air to RV apex)
    - Aspirate air via PA catheter if available [^1_9]


### Circuit Clotting / Thrombosis [^1_2][^1_9]

- Signs: rising ΔP, falling flow, visible clots
- If severe → clamp circuit, prepare for exchange
- Start CPR if VA ECMO; hand-bag if VV ECMO [^1_2][^1_9]


### Accidental Decannulation [^1_2]

- **Immediate:**
    - Clamp cannula/circuit
    - Direct pressure on insertion site
    - Call for help (surgery/perfusion)
    - Prepare for re-cannulation or emergent conversion if unstable

## 12. Weaning from VV ECMO

### Readiness Criteria [^1_1][^1_2]

- Underlying lung pathology improving
- **Oxygenation:**
    - FiO₂ ≤60%, PEEP ≤10 cmH₂O
    - PaO₂ ≥70 mmHg on minimal ECMO support
    - SpO₂ >88–90% on ECMO flow 2 L/min, FdO₂ 0.21–0.4 [^1_1]
- **Ventilation:**
    - Tidal volume <8 mL/kg PBW
    - Respiratory rate ≤28/min
    - Plateau ≤28 cmH₂O, driving pressure <15 cmH₂O
    - Acceptable pH/PaCO₂ on reduced sweep (<1 L/min) [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Gas exchange:**
    - EtCO₂ increasing, PaCO₂ decreasing, (etCO₂–PaCO₂) gap narrowing [^1_1]
    - Chest X-ray improving
    - Lung compliance improving


### Weaning Trial [^1_1][^1_2]

- Reduce sweep to <1 L/min (or off) for 4–12 hours
- Reduce ECMO flow to 2 L/min, FdO₂ to 0.21–0.4
- Monitor ABG, SpO₂, work of breathing
- If stable → proceed to decannulation
- If tracheostomy planned → can perform while still on ECMO [^1_1]

## 13. Key Clinical Pearls

- **SpO₂ <85% on VV ECMO:** think **recirculation** first (check SvO₂, cannula position), then **low ECMO flow/CO ratio**, then **oxygenator failure**. [^1_1][^1_4][^1_7]
- **Chattering in venous line + low flow:** reduce RPM first, then give fluids, then check cannula position. [^1_2][^1_5]
- **ΔP >50–60 mmHg:** suspect oxygenator thrombosis; >100 mmHg → plan for circuit change. [^1_2][^1_6]
- **SvO₂ >75% with falling SpO₂:** classic recirculation; reducing flow may paradoxically improve oxygenation. [^1_2]
- **No fever in sepsis:** ECMO circuit masks fever; look for hemodynamic changes, increasing support needs. [^1_1]
- **VV ECMO ≠ circulatory support:** in cardiac arrest, VV ECMO provides oxygenation but no perfusion; start CPR and consider VA conversion. [^1_2][^1_9]
- **Avoid rapid CO₂ correction** in chronic hypercapnia (risk of cerebral edema, seizures). [^1_2]
- **Cannula separation:** aim for 12–15 cm between drainage and reinfusion ports to minimize recirculation. [^1_4][^1_7]

```
│   • Ventilator functioning?
│   • No kinks in lines?
│
├─ Step 2: Assess oxygenator
│   • ΔP >50–60 mmHg? → oxygenator dysfunction
│   • Post-oxygenator PaO₂ <250 mmHg? → consider circuit change
│
├─ Step 3: Check for recirculation
│   • SvO₂ >75%? SpO₂–SvO₂ <10%?
│   • Both cannulae blood bright red?
│   • Trial: reduce flow 10–20% → SpO₂ improves? → recirculation
│   • Fix: reduce RPM, reposition cannula (12–15 cm apart)
│
├─ Step 4: Assess ECMO flow / CO ratio
│   • ECMO flow <60% of CO?

│
├─ Step 5: Optimize SvO₂
│   • Hb <7–8? → transfuse
│   • Fever, shivering, agitation? → treat
│
└─ Step 6: Evaluate native lung
    • CXR: new infiltrate, pneumothorax?
    • Optimize PEEP, FiO₂, consider prone
```

## 15. Monitoring Parameters on VV ECMO

| Parameter | Target | Notes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **SpO₂** | 80–90% (PaO₂ >45–50 mmHg) | Higher targets not proven beneficial; avoid hyperoxemia [^1_1][^1_2] |
| **SvO₂** | 60–80% | <60% → inadequate oxygen delivery; >75% → suspect recirculation [^1_2] |
| **ECMO flow** | 3–6 L/min (safe range) | Initially ~5 L/min; >60% of CO for adequate oxygenation [^1_2] |
| **Pven (pre-pump)** | >−100 mmHg | <−100 mmHg → drainage insufficiency (hypovolemia, malposition) [^1_2] |
| **PArt (return)** | <300 mmHg | High → return obstruction (kink, thrombus) [^1_2] |
| **ΔP (transmembrane)** | <40–50 mmHg | >60 mmHg → oxygenator dysfunction; >100 mmHg → failure [^1_2][^1_6] |
| **Post-oxygenator PaO₂** | >250–600 mmHg (on 100% FiO₂) | <150–200 mmHg → oxygenator failure [^1_2] |
| **Sweep gas** | 1–9 L/min (initially 50–100% of flow) | Titrate to PaCO₂/pH [^1_2] |
| **Hemoglobin** | >7–8 g/dL | Transfuse if <7 g/dL or SvO₂ low despite adequate flow [^1_1][^1_2] |
| **Platelets** | >50,000/μL (if bleeding) | Maintain >50k if bleeding or procedure planned [^1_1] |
| **aPTT** | 50–70 seconds (or per protocol) | EOLIA: 40–55 s; monitor anti-Xa, TEG for concordance [^1_1] |
