
## Traumatic Spinal Cord Injury: ICU Diagnosis, Scoring, Management and Complications

Traumatic spinal cord injury (tSCI) is a neurologic emergency requiring immediate ICU-level hemodynamic and respiratory support, early neurosurgical decompression when indicated, and strict prevention of secondary injury. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3] Below is a structured, clinically oriented overview tailored for critical care practice.

***

## Definition and Classification

### Definition

- **Traumatic SCI**: Acute disruption of spinal cord structure/function due to external mechanical force (e.g., motor vehicle collision, fall, sports, penetrating trauma). [^1_1][^1_4]
- Distinct from **non-traumatic SCI** (e.g., tumor, infection, ischemia, inflammatory myelopathy). [^1_4]


### Anatomical Classification by Level

- **Cervical** (50–60%): Risk of respiratory failure, neurogenic shock, autonomic dysfunction. [^1_2]
- **Thoracic** (30–32%): Trunk weakness, potential sympathetic disruption (T6 and above). [^1_2]
- **Lumbar/Sacral** (9–10%): Lower limb motor/sensory deficits, bladder/bowel dysfunction. [^1_1]


### Syndromic Classification

| Syndrome | Features | Typical Mechanism |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Central Cord** | Upper extremity > lower extremity weakness; bladder dysfunction | Hyperextension, especially in elderly with spondylosis |
| **Anterior Cord** | Motor loss + loss of pain/temperature; preserved proprioception/vibration | Flexion injury, vascular compromise |
| **Posterior Cord** | Loss of proprioception/vibration; preserved motor and pain/temp | Rare; posterior column injury |
| **Brown-Séquard (Hemicord)** | Ipsilateral motor + proprioception loss; contralateral pain/temp loss | Hemisection, penetrating trauma |
| **Complete Transection** | Total loss of motor and sensory function below injury | Severe trauma |

[^1_1][^1_2][^1_4]

***

## Epidemiology

- **Incidence**: ~39–40/million/year in North America (~17,000–18,000 cases annually). [^1_2]
- **Global burden**: ~0.9 million new cases/year; 20 million prevalent cases. [^1_2]
- **Demographics**: 80% male; bimodal age distribution (19–30 years and >70 years). [^1_2]
- **Mortality**: In-hospital 4–18%; prehospital 15–20%. [^1_2]
- **Leading causes of death**: Respiratory failure, thromboembolism, multiorgan failure. [^1_2]

***

## Pathophysiology

### Primary Injury

- Immediate mechanical disruption: fractures, dislocations, contusions, transections.
- Axonal shearing, neuronal/glial membrane permeabilization, hemorrhage. [^1_4]


### Secondary Injury (Target for ICU Management)

- **Ischemia**: Loss of autoregulation, hypotension → spinal cord hypoperfusion.
- **Inflammation**: IL-1α, IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α → edema, microvascular damage.
- **Excitotoxicity**: Glutamate release → Ca²⁺ overload → cell death.
- **Oxidative stress**: Free radicals → lipid peroxidation.
- **Apoptosis**: Delayed oligodendrocyte death → demyelination. [^1_2][^1_4]

**Clinical Implication**: Prevent/treat hypotension, hypoxia, hyperthermia, hyperglycemia to minimize secondary injury. [^1_1][^1_3]

***

## Clinical Presentation and Initial Assessment

### Red Flags in Trauma

- Midline neck/back pain or tenderness
- Focal neurologic deficit (weakness, sensory loss)
- Priapism (acute SCI marker)
- Neurogenic shock (hypotension + bradycardia)
- Respiratory distress (high cervical injury) [^1_1][^1_2]


### Primary Survey (ATLS)

- **Airway**: Secure early if C1–C5 injury, respiratory distress, or GCS <8.
- **Breathing**: SpO₂ >92%; assess FVC, NIF in cervical injuries.
- **Circulation**: Avoid hypotension; treat neurogenic shock.
- **Disability**: Rapid neuro exam (ASIA/ISNCSCI).
- **Exposure**: Prevent hypothermia. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_5]

***

## Diagnosis and Imaging

### Initial Imaging

- **CT spine (whole spine)**: First-line for bony injury; high sensitivity for fractures/dislocations. [^1_1][^1_3]
- **MRI**: Indicated if:
    - Neurologic deficit with normal CT
    - Suspected ligamentous injury, cord edema, hematoma, disc herniation
    - Preoperative planning
    - Perform within 48–72 hours if possible. [^1_1][^1_3]
- **X-rays**: Limited role; adjunct in resource-limited settings. [^1_2]


### Clearance of C-Spine

- **Awake, asymptomatic, reliable exam**: No collar needed if:
    - No midline tenderness
    - No neurologic deficit
    - No distracting injury
    - Normal CT C-spine [^1_6]
- **Obtunded patients**: Negative CT sufficient to remove collar (EAST Level III). [^1_6]
- **MRI**: Not routinely needed if CT normal and no deficit. [^1_6]

***

## Neurologic Scoring: ASIA/ISNCSCI

### International Standards for Neurological Classification of SCI (ISNCSCI)

- **Sensory exam**: 28 dermatomes (C2–S4/5) for light touch and pinprick (0–2 scale).
- **Motor exam**: 10 key muscle groups (C5–T1, L2–S1) bilaterally (0–5 MRC scale).
- **Neurological Level of Injury (NLI)**: Most caudal level with normal sensory and motor function bilaterally.
- **Sacral sparing**: Presence of S4/5 sensation or voluntary anal contraction → **incomplete injury**. [^1_1][^1_6]


### ASIA Impairment Scale (AIS)

| Grade | Definition |
| :-- | :-- |
| **A** | Complete: No motor/sensory function in S4/5 |
| **B** | Sensory incomplete: Sensory but no motor function below NLI, including S4/5 |
| **C** | Motor incomplete: Motor function below NLI, <50% key muscles have grade ≥3 |
| **D** | Motor incomplete: ≥50% key muscles below NLI have grade ≥3 |
| **E** | Normal: All sensory/motor function preserved |

[^1_1][^1_6]

**Prognostic value**: First reliable prognostic assessment at 72 hours; earlier scores affected by spinal shock. [^1_3]

***

## ICU Management: Core Principles

> **Goal**: Prevent secondary injury by optimizing perfusion, oxygenation, and metabolic milieu. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3]

### 1. Hemodynamic Management

#### MAP Augmentation

- **Target MAP**: **≥85 mmHg** for blunt and incomplete penetrating SCI.
- **Duration**: Minimum 72 hours; up to 7 days (especially first week). [^1_1][^1_3][^1_6]
- **Rationale**: Spinal cord perfusion pressure (SCPP) = MAP – intrathecal pressure; hypotension worsens ischemia. [^1_1][^1_3]


#### Vasopressor Choice

| Vasopressor | Indication | Rationale |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Norepinephrine** | First-line for cervical/high thoracic SCI | α + β activity → supports MAP and heart rate |
| **Phenylephrine** | Lower thoracic/lumbar SCI (if no bradycardia) | Pure α-agonist; may worsen bradycardia |
| **Dopamine** | Alternative (less preferred) | β + α effects; more arrhythmogenic |

[^1_2][^1_6]

#### Fluid Resuscitation

- Initial crystalloid bolus (e.g., 1–2 L NS) for hypotension.
- Avoid over-resuscitation → pulmonary edema, abdominal compartment syndrome. [^1_2][^1_6]


#### Neurogenic Shock Recognition

- **Triad**: Hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg), bradycardia (HR <50–60 bpm), warm extremities.
- **Level**: T6 and above (loss of sympathetic outflow).
- **Treatment**: Fluids + norepinephrine; atropine/chronotropes for symptomatic bradycardia. [^1_2][^1_6]

***

### 2. Respiratory Management

#### Indications for Intubation

- Injury at/above **C5** (diaphragmatic paralysis risk)
- FVC <10 mL/kg, NIF <−20 cmH₂O
- PaCO₂ >50 mmHg, SpO₂ <92% despite O₂
- Rapidly progressive deficit, bulbar dysfunction, concomitant chest trauma [^1_2][^1_5]


#### Ventilator Strategy

- **Lung-protective ventilation**: TV 6–8 mL/kg (or 10–15 mL/kg if atelectasis risk in SCI).
- **SpO₂ target**: 92–98%; avoid hyperoxia.
- **Early tracheostomy**: Consider within 4–7 days for high cervical SCI (↓ pneumonia, ICU LOS). [^1_2][^1_5][^1_6]


#### Respiratory Mechanics Monitoring

- **FVC, NIF, peak flow** every shift in non-intubated cervical SCI.
- **Cough assist devices**, incentive spirometry, chest physiotherapy.
- **Aggressive secretion clearance**: Metaneb, albuterol, suctioning, abdominal binder when upright. [^1_6]

***

### 3. Pharmacologic Considerations

#### Methylprednisolone

- **Current guideline (AANS/CNS, 2023)**: **Not recommended** (Level 2).
- **Risks**: GI bleeding, infection, hyperglycemia; no consistent neurologic benefit. [^1_2][^1_6]
- **Exception**: Some centers use 24-hour NASCIS protocol within 8 hours (controversial; discuss with neurosurgery). [^1_4]


#### Analgesia and Sedation

- **Neuropathic pain**: Gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine, amitriptyline.
- **Acute pain**: Fentanyl/morphine infusions; avoid hypotension.
- **Spasticity**: Baclofen, tizanidine, methocarbamol. [^1_6]


#### Stress Ulcer Prophylaxis

- PPI or H₂ blocker if ventilated, coagulopathic, or high-risk. [^1_6]

***

### 4. Thromboprophylaxis

| Modality | Timing | Regimen |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Mechanical** | On admission | SCDs, TED hose |
| **Chemical** | Within 24–72 hours (if no active bleeding) | UFH 5000 U SC q8h → transition to enoxaparin 40 mg SC q12h (or 0.5 mg/kg if BMI ≥30) |
| **IVC Filter** | If chemoprophylaxis contraindicated | Consider in high-risk, non-operative patients |

[^1_1][^1_3][^1_6]

- **Duration**: Minimum 8 weeks; up to 3 months in high-risk patients. [^1_1]

***

### 5. Surgical Management

#### Indications for Early Decompression (<24–72 hours)

- Progressive neurologic deficit
- Spinal instability preventing mobilization
- Compressive hematoma, disc, bone fragment
- Incomplete SCI with correctable compression [^1_1][^1_3][^1_6]


#### Timing

- **Early (<24 hours)**: Associated with improved ASIA grade conversion, shorter ICU stay. [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Delayed (>72 hours)**: May miss window for maximal neuroprotection. [^1_6]

***

### 6. Gastrointestinal and Nutritional Support

#### Bowel Management

- **Neurogenic bowel**: Reflexic (above T12) vs. areflexic (T12 and below).
- **Regimen**: Stool softeners (docusate), stimulants (senna, bisacodyl), digital stimulation, scheduled timing. [^1_1][^1_6]


#### Nutrition

- **Early enteral feeding**: Within 24–48 hours.
- **Caloric goal**: 25–30 kcal/kg/day; protein 1.5–2.0 g/kg/day.
- **Glycemic control**: Target 140–180 mg/dL. [^1_6]

***

### 7. Bladder Management

- **Indwelling catheter** initially for retention.
- **Transition to intermittent catheterization** once stable (goal ≤400 mL per cath).
- **Avoid long-term Foley**: ↑ UTI risk. [^1_1][^1_6]

***

## ICU Complications: Prevention and Management

| Complication | Pathophysiology | Prevention/Management |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Neurogenic Shock** | Loss of sympathetic tone → vasodilation, bradycardia | Fluids, norepinephrine, atropine |
| **Autonomic Dysreflexia** (T6 and above) | Noxious stimulus (bladder, bowel) → unopposed sympathetic surge | Remove trigger, nitroglycerin paste, nifedipine, sit upright |
| **Respiratory Failure** | Diaphragmatic/intercostal paralysis, impaired cough | Early intubation, tracheostomy, cough assist, chest PT |
| **Pneumonia** | Aspiration, secretion retention | VAP bundle, oral care, early mobilization |
| **DVT/PE** | Immobility, venous stasis | Early chemoprophylaxis, SCDs |
| **Pressure Ulcers** | Immobility, sensory loss | Reposition q2h, specialty mattress, sacral dressings |
| **UTI** | Catheterization, retention | Early catheter removal, intermittent cath |
| **Ileus/Constipation** | Loss of autonomic control, opioids | Bowel regimen, prokinetics, early feeding |
| **Spasticity** | Loss of inhibitory pathways | Baclofen, tizanidine, PT |
| **Heterotopic Ossification** | Ectopic bone formation in joints | Early ROM, monitor alkaline phosphatase |
| **Depression/Anxiety** | Psychosocial impact | Counseling, rehab involvement |

[^1_1][^1_2][^1_6]

***

## Prognosis and Recovery

- **Most recovery**: Within first 6–12 months; plateaus after 18–24 months.
- **Predictors of recovery**:
    - Initial AIS grade (A worst, D/E best)
    - Early decompression
    - Age <60 years
    - Incomplete injury (sacral sparing)
- **Mortality risk factors**: Age >70, high cervical injury, complete SCI, polytrauma. [^1_2][^1_6]

***

## Key Clinical Pearls

1. **“Time is spine”**: Early decompression + MAP augmentation = better outcomes. [^1_2]
2. **Avoid hypotension and hypoxia**: Even brief episodes worsen neurologic outcome. [^1_1][^1_3]
3. **No steroids**: High-dose methylprednisolone is not recommended (Level 2). [^1_6]
4. **Early tracheostomy**: Consider in high cervical SCI to reduce pulmonary complications. [^1_2][^1_6]
5. **Aggressive DVT prophylaxis**: LMWH within 72 hours unless contraindicated. [^1_1][^1_6]
6. **Autonomic dysreflexia**: Treat as emergency; remove trigger, give vasodilators. [^1_2][^1_6]

***

## Landmark Trials and Guidelines

- **NASCIS II/III**: High-dose methylprednisolone – modest motor benefit at 24h, but significant complications; not routinely recommended. [^1_2][^1_6]
- **AOSpine/AANS Guidelines (2017–2023)**: MAP ≥85 mmHg, early surgery, no steroids. [^1_3][^1_6]
- **EAST Guidelines**: CT sufficient for c-spine clearance in obtunded patients. [^1_6]

***

## Suggested ICU Protocol Summary

### Day 0–7 (Acute Phase)

- **MAP**: ≥85 mmHg (norepinephrine)
- **SpO₂**: 92–98%
- **Neuro exams**: Daily ASIA/ISNCSCI
- **DVT prophylaxis**: UFH → enoxaparin
- **Respiratory**: FVC/NIF q shift, cough assist, consider tracheostomy
- **Bowel/bladder**: Scheduled regimen, avoid retention
- **Skin**: q2h turning, pressure-redistribution mattress
- **Nutrition**: Enteral feeding within 48h
