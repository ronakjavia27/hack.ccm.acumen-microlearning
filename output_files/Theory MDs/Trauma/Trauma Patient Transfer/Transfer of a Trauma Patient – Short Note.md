
## Transfer of a Trauma Patient

Transfer of a trauma patient is the **planned movement of an injured patient from one facility to another** (or within the same facility) for definitive care when local resources are insufficient. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3] The overarching principle is **"stabilize first, then transfer"**—but do not delay transfer for investigations or interventions that will not change immediate management. [^1_4][^1_5]

***

## Indications for Transfer

Transfer is indicated when **patient needs exceed available resources**: [^1_5]

### Clinical Circumstances (ATLS Criteria)

- **CNS**: Severe TBI (GCS <8), depressed skull fracture, penetrating brain injury, spinal cord injury
- **Chest**: Flail chest, pulmonary contusion with hypoxia, massive hemothorax, cardiac tamponade
- **Abdomen/Pelvis**: Hemodynamically unstable abdominal trauma, pelvic fracture with instability
- **Extremities**: Vascular injury, compartment syndrome, amputation, open fractures
- **Multisystem**: ISS >15, multiple long-bone fractures, burns with trauma
- **Comorbid factors**: Anticoagulation, pregnancy, extreme age, significant medical comorbidities
- **Secondary deterioration**: Delayed complications requiring higher-level care [^1_5]


### Resource-Based Indications

- Lack of trauma surgeon, neurosurgeon, or orthopedic coverage
- No ICU/HDU beds or monitoring capability
- Absence of CT, angiography, or interventional radiology
- Need for damage control surgery not available locally [^1_2][^1_6]

***

## Pre-Transfer Stabilization (ABCDE)

**Do not transfer an unstable patient unless the receiving center can provide definitive intervention faster** (e.g., operating room, angio suite). [^1_3][^1_4]

### Airway

- **Definitive airway** (endotracheal intubation) before departure—intubation during transport is hazardous [^1_7][^1_4]
- Confirm tube position clinically and with **capnography**; secure firmly
- Cervical spine immobilization maintained throughout [^1_3][^1_4]


### Breathing

- **Chest decompression** for pneumothorax/hemothorax before transfer (chest drain if tension physiology) [^1_7][^1_4]
- Portable ventilator with **pressure monitoring, minute volume, disconnect alarm** [^1_7]
- Target: **SpO₂ 94–98%**, PaO₂ ≥13 kPa; avoid hyperoxia in TBI [^1_7]
- **ETCO₂ monitoring** mandatory; target PaCO₂ 4.5–5.0 kPa (35–40 mmHg), especially in TBI [^1_7]


### Circulation

- **Two large-bore IVs** (14–16G) or central access; secure all lines
- **Fluid resuscitation** with blood products per massive transfusion protocol if needed; pack blood products properly for transport [^1_8][^1_4]
- **Vasoactive drugs** immediately available (noradrenaline, ephedrine, metaraminol) [^1_7]
- Target **SBP >110 mmHg** (MAP >90 mmHg) in TBI; avoid hypotension [^1_7]
- **Invasive arterial monitoring** preferred if available; do not delay transfer for insertion [^1_7][^1_4]


### Disability (Neurological)

- **GCS documented** and monitored q15min during transfer [^1_8]
- Pupils checked and recorded
- **Sedation/analgesia** optimized to prevent agitation, coughing, or ICP spikes [^1_7][^1_4]
- Consider ICP monitor if already in place; ensure external ventricular drain (EVD) is clamped/secured appropriately for transport


### Exposure/Environment

- **Immobilize** cervical spine, pelvis (binder), and long-bone fractures (traction splints, vacuum mattress) [^1_3]
- Prevent hypothermia: warm blankets, fluid warmer, cabin heating [^1_4]
- All drains, catheters, and tubes secured; urinary catheter and NG/OG tube in place [^1_3][^1_4]

***

## Transfer Team \& Equipment

### Personnel

- **Minimum 2 clinicians** in addition to driver for critically ill trauma patients [^1_9][^1_4]
- At least **one senior clinician** (registrar/consultant level) with airway and resuscitation skills [^1_7][^1_3]
- Team trained in **ATLS and transfer medicine** [^1_7]


### Equipment Checklist

**Airway/Breathing:**

- Portable ventilator with batteries charged
- Backup bag-valve-mask (BVM) with PEEP valve
- Suction apparatus (portable)
- Spare endotracheal tubes, bougie, video laryngoscope if available
- Capnography and pulse oximetry [^1_8][^1_7][^1_4]

**Circulation:**

- Infusion pumps (minimum 2–3)
- Pressure transducers for arterial line
- Vasoactive drugs prepared and labeled
- Blood products (if indicated), properly packaged [^1_8][^1_4]

**Monitoring:**

- ECG, SpO₂, ETCO₂, NIBP (or invasive BP)
- Temperature probe
- Defibrillator with pacing capability [^1_8][^1_7][^1_4]

**Drugs:**

- Sedatives (propofol, midazolam, ketamine)
- Analgesics (fentanyl, morphine)
- Paralytics (rocuronium, succinylcholine for emergency)
- Vasopressors, antiarrhythmics, anticonvulsants
- Emergency drugs (atropine, adrenaline, calcium, bicarbonate) [^1_8][^1_4]

**Documentation:**

- Case notes, imaging (CT films/CDs), lab results
- Transfer checklist and consent form
- Blood cross-match report [^1_8][^1_4]

***

## Communication \& Handover

### Pre-Transfer Communication

- **Senior-to-senior discussion** between referring and receiving clinicians [^1_3]
- Confirm **bed availability**, operating room readiness, and specialist coverage [^1_9][^1_2]
- Agree on **mode of transport** (ground vs. air) based on distance, weather, urgency, and patient stability [^1_9][^1_2]


### Handover Framework: **ABC-SBAR**

Combine physiological assessment (ABC) with structured communication (SBAR): [^1_7]

**Situation:**

- "35-year-old male, RTA, GCS 7, intubated, suspected intracranial hemorrhage and pelvic fracture"

**Background:**

- Mechanism, time of injury, prehospital interventions, comorbidities, medications (especially anticoagulants)

**Assessment:**

- Current vitals, GCS, pupillary response, injuries identified, interventions performed (chest drains, fluids, blood products)

**Recommendation:**

- "Requires urgent CT head/pelvis, neurosurgical and orthopedic review; ETA 45 minutes" [^1_7][^1_2]

**Additional Information to Convey:**

- Allergies, blood group, tetanus status
- Time-sensitive windows (e.g., thrombolysis, surgery)
- Family contact details [^1_7][^1_2]

***

## Mode of Transport

| Factor | Ground Ambulance | Air (Helicopter/Fixed-Wing) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Distance** | <250 km | >250 km or time-critical |
| **Speed** | Slower, traffic-dependent | Faster, weather-dependent |
| **Space** | More space for interventions | Limited space, noise, vibration |
| **Physiological Effects** | Minimal | Altitude effects (gas expansion, hypoxia) |
| **Cost** | Lower | Higher |
| **Indications** | Stable patients, short distance | Unstable but transferable, remote locations, long distance |

**Decision factors:** urgency, stability, time savings, weather, medical interventions needed en route, resource availability [^1_9]

***

## Monitoring During Transfer

- **Continuous:** ECG, SpO₂, ETCO₂, BP (arterial preferred), temperature [^1_8][^1_7][^1_4]
- **Intermittent (q15min):** GCS, pupil size/reactivity, limb perfusion, urine output [^1_8]
- **Document:** All vitals, interventions, drug administration, and clinical changes [^1_8][^1_4]

***

## Complications of Transfer

### Common Adverse Events

- **Airway:** Tube displacement, obstruction, accidental extubation
- **Breathing:** Hypoxia, hypercapnia, pneumothorax expansion, ventilator malfunction
- **Circulation:** Hypotension, arrhythmias, line dislodgement, transfusion reactions
- **Neurological:** ICP elevation, seizures, agitation
- **Equipment:** Battery failure, oxygen depletion, pump malfunction
- **Environmental:** Hypothermia, pressure sores [^1_11][^1_3]


### Prevention Strategies

- **Pre-departure checklist** completion [^1_12][^1_4]
- **Adequate oxygen supply** (calculate for journey + 50% reserve for delays) [^1_7][^1_4]
- **Battery backup** for all devices (ventilator, monitors, pumps) [^1_4]
- **Secure all lines/tubes** with tape and ties; use commercial tube holders [^1_3][^1_4]
- **Experienced team** with airway and resuscitation skills [^1_7][^1_3]

***

## Special Considerations

### Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)

- **SBP >110 mmHg**, PaCO₂ 35–40 mmHg, SpO₂ >94%, head elevation 30° if no spinal injury [^1_7]
- Avoid hypotension and hypoxia—both worsen secondary brain injury
- Consider **ICP monitoring** if available; manage EVD carefully during transport [^1_6]


### Spinal Cord Injury

- **Strict immobilization** (cervical collar, vacuum mattress)
- Maintain **MAP >85 mmHg** for spinal perfusion (vasopressors if needed)
- High-dose steroids **not routinely recommended** (NASCIS criteria controversial)


### Chest Trauma

- Ensure **chest drains are on underwater seal** and secured
- Monitor for tension pneumothorax during positive pressure ventilation
- Portable ultrasound (if available) for rapid assessment


### Abdominal/Pelvic Trauma

- **Pelvic binder** applied for unstable fractures
- Avoid excessive fluid resuscitation if hemorrhage uncontrolled (permissive hypotension unless TBI)
- **Tranexamic acid** within 3 hours if massive hemorrhage suspected


### Burns with Trauma

- **Fluid resuscitation** per Parkland formula; monitor urine output
- **Wound care** during transport (clean dressings, prevent contamination)
- **Pain control** critical (opioids, ketamine)

***

## Documentation \& Legal Aspects

- **Informed consent** from patient/legally authorized representative [^1_9]
- **Transfer checklist** completed and signed [^1_12][^1_4]
- **Transfer form** with: patient details, diagnosis, interventions, vitals, drugs given, team members, ETA [^1_10][^1_4]
- **Imaging and labs** accompany patient (CD/films, printed reports) [^1_8][^1_4]
- **Incident reporting** for any adverse events during transfer [^1_11]

***

## Post-Transfer Handover

- **Face-to-face handover** to receiving team using ABCDE framework [^1_3]
- Highlight **time-critical issues** (e.g., "CT head needed within 30 min," "orthopedics for pelvic fixation")
- Transfer **responsibility formally** once receiving team accepts care [^1_3]
- **Debrief** transfer team on return; restock equipment and report faults [^1_3][^1_4]

***

## Topic Pearls

★ **Stabilize before transfer**, but do not delay for non-essential investigations (CT, MRI) if they won't change immediate management. [^1_4][^1_5]

★ **Definitive airway before departure**—intubation during transport is extremely difficult and risky. [^1_7][^1_4]

★ **Minimum 2 clinicians** plus driver for critically ill trauma transfers; senior input mandatory. [^1_9][^1_7][^1_3]

★ **ABC-SBAR handover** ensures both physiological stability and effective communication. [^1_7][^1_2]

★ **Oxygen calculation:** journey time + 50% reserve for delays; check ventilator driving gas requirements. [^1_7][^1_4]

★ **Monitor continuously:** ECG, SpO₂, ETCO₂, BP (arterial preferred), temperature; document q15min neuro checks. [^1_8][^1_7][^1_4]

★ **Complications are common:** airway displacement, hypotension, equipment failure—prevention via checklist and experienced team. [^1_11][^1_3]
