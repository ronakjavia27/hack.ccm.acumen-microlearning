
## PrAKI (Pregnancy-associated Acute Kidney Injury) — Short Note

**Definition:** PrAKI is acute kidney injury occurring during pregnancy or within 6 weeks (42 days) postpartum, characterized by a sudden decline in kidney function meeting KDIGO criteria in the obstetric context. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3]

***

## Definition \& Diagnostic Criteria

- **Time window:** Antenatal period through 42 days postpartum. [^1_2][^1_3]
- **KDIGO criteria (any one):** [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]
    - ↑ Serum creatinine (SCr) ≥0.3 mg/dL (≥26.5 µmol/L) within 48 h, **or**
    - ↑ SCr ≥1.5× baseline (presumed within 7 days), **or**
    - Urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥6 h.
- **Pregnancy-specific caveat:** No universally standardized PrAKI definition; KDIGO criteria are applied but less validated in pregnancy. [^1_1][^1_7]
- **PrAKI-ADQI 2025 suggestion:** Consider PrAKI with any SCr rise from baseline after a new insult, or prolonged oligo/anuria antenatally or postpartum (weak recommendation, grade B). [^1_8]
- **Biomarkers:** Insufficient evidence to recommend routine use of novel biomarkers (NGAL, KIM-1, TIMP-2·IGFBP7) for PrAKI diagnosis/staging. [^1_8][^1_7]

***

## Epidemiology \& Burden

- Incidence varies by region and obstetric care access; higher in low-resource settings and with delayed referral. [^1_9][^1_10]
- **Outcomes:** Maternal mortality up to ~30% and perinatal mortality ~60% in severe PrAKI requiring dialysis; worse with delayed initiation (>72 h) and advanced stage. [^1_9]
- Majority develop PrAKI in the **postpartum** period (~80% in some cohorts), underscoring need for vigilant monitoring after delivery. [^1_9]

***

## Etiology \& Risk Factors

**Most common causes:** [^1_1][^1_10][^1_3]

- Hypertensive disorders: **Pre-eclampsia/eclampsia** (leading etiology)
- **Obstetric hemorrhage** (antenatal or postpartum)
- **Sepsis** (chorioamnionitis, puerperal sepsis, septic abortion)
- Other: HELLP syndrome, TTP/HUS, acute fatty liver of pregnancy, cortical necrosis, drug-induced (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides), obstructive uropathy.

**Risk factors:** [^1_10]

- Inadequate antenatal care, delayed recognition of fever/oliguria
- Uncontrolled hypertension, proteinuria
- Nephrotoxic exposures, prolonged labor, cesarean section with hemorrhage
- Pre-existing CKD or reduced renal reserve

***

## Pathophysiology

- **Physiologic baseline:** Pregnancy causes systemic vasodilation → ↑ renal plasma flow and GFR → lower baseline SCr; thus small absolute rises may be significant. [^1_1]
- **Mechanisms:**
    - **Prerenal:** Hypovolemia (hemorrhage), sepsis-induced vasodilation, cardiogenic shock.
    - **Intrinsic:** Endothelial injury (pre-eclampsia/HELLP), microangiopathy (TTP/HUS), ischemic/nephrotoxic ATN, cortical necrosis.
    - **Postrenal:** Obstruction (rare; clots, stones, uterine compression).
- **Microvascular injury** in hypertensive disorders reduces renal perfusion despite normal/high cardiac output. [^1_1][^1_7]

***

## Clinical Features

- Oliguria/anuria, rising SCr, fluid overload, hypertension, proteinuria.
- Features of underlying cause: headache/visual changes (eclampsia), fever (sepsis), bleeding (hemorrhage), RUQ pain (HELLP/AFLP).
- Postpartum vigilance critical: many cases present after delivery with delayed oliguria or creatinine rise. [^1_9]

***

## Investigations

**Baseline \& monitoring:**

- SCr, electrolytes, acid-base, CBC, coagulation profile, LFTs, urinalysis (protein, casts), urine output charting.
- **Imaging:** Renal ultrasound to exclude obstruction and assess size/echogenicity. [^1_8]
- **Etiology-specific tests:**
    - Pre-eclampsia/HELLP: BP, protein/creatinine ratio, platelets, LDH, peripheral smear.
    - Sepsis: Cultures, lactate, procalcitonin (as available).
    - MAHA/TTP-HUS: ADAMTS13 activity (if available), smear for schistocytes, haptoglobin, reticulocytes.
- **Biopsy:** Consider when diagnosis remains uncertain and will change management (e.g., suspected glomerulonephritis, cortical necrosis). [^1_8]

***

## Severity Assessment \& Staging

- Apply **KDIGO AKI staging** (Stage 1–3) using SCr and urine output thresholds. [^1_4][^1_5]
- Higher stage correlates with incomplete recovery and mortality; Stage 3 has significantly worse outcomes. [^1_9]

***

## Differential Diagnosis

- Physiologic pregnancy changes vs true AKI (remember lower baseline SCr).
- Acute vs chronic kidney disease (history, ultrasound, prior records).
- Prerenal vs intrinsic vs postrenal causes.
- Overlap syndromes: pre-eclampsia with ATN vs cortical necrosis vs TTP/HUS.

***

## Management

### Initial/Stabilization

- **ABCDE**, secure airway if eclampsia/severe sepsis.
- **Hemodynamics:** Careful fluid resuscitation guided by clinical status, ultrasound, and (if available) dynamic indices; avoid fluid overload.
- **Control BP:** Labetalol, hydralazine, or nifedipine for severe hypertension; magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis/treatment in pre-eclampsia-eclampsia.
- **Treat underlying cause:**
    - Hemorrhage: transfusion, surgical/IR control.
    - Sepsis: early broad-spectrum antibiotics, source control.
    - HELLP/TTP-HUS: multidisciplinary management; plasma exchange for TTP; consider eculizumab in selected complement-mediated cases.


### Renal-Specific Therapy

- **Avoid nephrotoxins:** NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, contrast unless essential.
- **Dose-adjust** renally cleared drugs.
- **Diuretics:** For volume overload only; do not use to "treat" AKI per se.


### Indications for Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT) [^1_9]

- Refractory volume overload/pulmonary edema
- Severe electrolyte disturbances (hyperkalemia, severe acidosis)
- Uremic complications (encephalopathy, pericarditis)
- Oligo/anuria with worsening azotemia despite optimization

**RRT modality:** IHD, SLED, or CRRT based on hemodynamic stability and resource availability. Early initiation (within 72 h of referral) associated with better recovery. [^1_9]

### Supportive \& Obstetric Care

- **Fetal monitoring** if antenatal; timing of delivery based on maternal-fetal status.
- **Postpartum monitoring:** Hourly urine output, daily SCr in high-risk women for at least 48–72 h. [^1_10]
- **Prevention bundles:** Regular BP checks in ANC, urine culture when indicated, avoid nephrotoxins, monitor urine output during and after delivery. [^1_10]

***

## Complications

- Progression to CKD, partial renal recovery, ESRD.
- Maternal mortality (especially Stage 3, delayed RRT).
- Perinatal mortality and morbidity.
- Hypertension, proteinuria persistence postpartum. [^1_9]

***

## Prognosis

- **Stage-dependent recovery:** Stage 1–2 have high complete recovery rates (~85–100%); Stage 3 has significantly higher partial recovery, CKD progression, and death. [^1_9]
- Delayed dialysis initiation (>72 h) associated with worse renal recovery. [^1_9]

***

## Topic Pearls

- PrAKI = AKI during pregnancy or ≤42 days postpartum; apply KDIGO criteria but recognize pregnancy-specific physiology (lower baseline SCr). [^1_1][^1_2]
- Leading causes: pre-eclampsia/eclampsia, hemorrhage, sepsis; most cases present postpartum. [^1_1][^1_9]
- No validated role yet for novel biomarkers in routine PrAKI diagnosis/staging. [^1_8][^1_7]
- Outcomes are stage-dependent; Stage 3 carries high mortality and incomplete recovery, especially with delayed RRT. [^1_9]
- Prevention hinges on ANC BP monitoring, avoiding nephrotoxins, and strict urine output surveillance during/after delivery. [^1_10]
