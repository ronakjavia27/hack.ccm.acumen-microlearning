
## Monitoring in Pregnancy in ICU During Critical Illness

Monitoring the critically ill pregnant patient in the ICU requires integration of **maternal physiological monitoring**, **fetal surveillance**, and an understanding of **pregnancy-specific physiological alterations** that modify normal parameters and therapeutic targets. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3] The fundamental principle is that **maternal stabilization is the priority**, as fetal well-being is directly dependent on adequate maternal oxygenation, perfusion, and metabolic status. [^1_4][^1_5]

## Physiological Changes in Pregnancy Relevant to ICU Monitoring

### Cardiovascular System

| Parameter | Change in Pregnancy | Clinical Implication for ICU Monitoring |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Blood volume | ↑ 35–55% | Significant blood loss may occur before hypotension manifests; shock may be masked until late [^1_1][^1_2] |
| Cardiac output | ↑ 30–50% | Baseline elevated; reductions may indicate decompensation [^1_1][^1_3][^1_6] |
| Heart rate | ↑ 10–20 bpm (15–20%) | Tachycardia is physiological; must differentiate from pathological causes [^1_1][^1_3][^1_6] |
| Stroke volume | ↑ 30% | Contributes to increased cardiac output [^1_1] |
| Systemic vascular resistance | ↓ 20–30% | Lower baseline BP; vasopressor requirements may differ [^1_2][^1_3][^1_6] |
| Blood pressure | ↓ 10–15 mmHg (first two trimesters) | Mid-gestation MAP decrease; returns to baseline later [^1_2][^1_6] |
| Colloid oncotic pressure | ↓ 10–15% | Increased risk of pulmonary edema with fluid loading [^1_6] |
| Aortocaval compression | After ~20 weeks | Reduced venous return in supine position; requires left uterine displacement [^1_2][^1_7] |

### Respiratory System

| Parameter | Change in Pregnancy | Clinical Implication |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Functional residual capacity (FRC) | ↓ 20–25% | Rapid desaturation during apnea; reduced oxygen reserve [^1_2][^1_3][^1_8] |
| Minute ventilation | ↑ 40–50% | Compensatory respiratory alkalosis [^1_1][^1_3] |
| Tidal volume | ↑ 40% | Increased work of breathing [^1_1] |
| Oxygen consumption | ↑ 20–50% | Higher oxygen demand; desaturation occurs faster [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3] |
| PaCO₂ | ↓ to 28–32 mmHg (4.0–4.5 kPa) | "Normal" non-pregnant PaCO₂ indicates hypercapnia in pregnancy [^1_9][^1_3][^1_6] |
| PaO₂ | ↑ Average 100–105 mmHg | Target PaO₂ ≥70 mmHg (≥9 kPa) to ensure fetal oxygen transfer [^1_9][^1_10][^1_6] |
| HCO₃⁻ | ↓ to 18–21 mEq/L | Compensatory metabolic acidosis; base deficit interpretation differs [^1_3][^1_6] |

### Renal and Hematological Systems

| Parameter | Change | ICU Relevance |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| GFR | ↑ 50% | "Normal" creatinine may indicate AKI; baseline creatinine 0.4–0.7 mg/dL [^1_2][^1_3][^1_6] |
| Renal plasma flow | ↑ 50% | Increased drug clearance [^1_2] |
| Hemoglobin | ↓ 20% (physiological anemia) | Lower threshold for transfusion decisions [^1_1] |
| Platelets | ↓ 10% | Mild thrombocytopenia is physiological [^1_1] |
| Clotting factors | ↑ 30–250% | Hypercoagulable state; increased VTE risk [^1_1][^1_2] |

## Clinical Monitoring

### General Vital Signs and Physical Examination

**Frequency:** In critically ill pregnant patients, vital signs should be monitored **continuously or at least hourly**, with more frequent assessment during instability. [^1_11][^1_12][^1_13]

- **Heart rate:** Continuous ECG monitoring; baseline tachycardia is physiological (↑10–20 bpm) [^1_1][^1_14]
- **Blood pressure:** Invasive arterial monitoring recommended for hemodynamic instability, severe preeclampsia, or when beat-to-beat monitoring guides vasopressor therapy [^1_14][^1_15]
- **Respiratory rate:** Increased baseline (↑15%); monitor for respiratory fatigue [^1_1]
- **Oxygen saturation:** Continuous pulse oximetry; target **SpO₂ >92%** (though evidence is limited) [^1_16][^1_9]
- **Temperature:** Fever increases fetal risk linearly with duration and exponentially with degree; treat aggressively with antipyretics and external cooling [^1_16]
- **Level of consciousness:** AVPU or GCS monitoring; neurological checks for preeclampsia/eclampsia (deep tendon reflexes, pupil response) [^1_13][^1_7]
- **Capillary refill time (CRT) and skin perfusion:** Clinical markers of peripheral perfusion [^1_1]


### Urine Output Monitoring

- **Target:** ≥0.5 mL/kg/hour (pregnancy-specific thresholds may vary)
- **Method:** Indwelling urinary catheter for accurate hourly measurement
- **Significance:** Reflects renal perfusion and cardiac output; essential for magnesium sulfate clearance monitoring in preeclampsia [^1_11][^1_13]
- **Caution:** CVP values are spuriously high after 24–26 weeks; interpret with caution [^1_10]


## Hemodynamic Monitoring

### Indications for Invasive Hemodynamic Monitoring in Pregnancy

**Cardiac indications:** [^1_1]

- Severe cardiac disease (cardiomyopathy, valvular stenosis, pulmonary hypertension)
- Cardiogenic shock
- Need for inotrope/vasopressor titration

**Pulmonary indications:** [^1_1]

- Severe ARDS
- Pulmonary edema of unclear etiology
- Need for precise fluid management in respiratory failure

**Renal indications:** [^1_1]

- Severe AKI requiring CRRT
- Oliguria unresponsive to fluid challenge


### Invasive Monitoring Modalities

**Arterial Line:**

- **Indications:** Hemodynamic instability, severe preeclampsia/hypertensive emergencies, need for frequent ABGs, vasopressor/inotrope titration [^1_10][^1_14][^1_15]
- **Benefits:** Beat-to-beat BP monitoring, accurate MAP targets, frequent blood sampling
- **Targets:** MAP individualized; avoid hypotension (MAP <65 mmHg reduces placental perfusion) [^1_17][^1_2]

**Central Venous Catheter (CVC):**

- **Site preference:** Internal jugular vein (most preferred) [^1_10]
- **Indications:** Vasopressor administration, CVP monitoring (limited accuracy after 24–26 weeks), poor peripheral access [^1_10][^1_15]
- **Caveat:** CVP values are **spuriously elevated** after 24–26 weeks due to aortocaval compression; interpret cautiously for volume assessment [^1_10]

**Pulmonary Artery Catheter (PAC):**

- **Reserved for:** Cardiopulmonary decompensation, right ventricular failure requiring pulmonary vasodilator titration, high-risk volume shifts [^1_15]
- **Parameters:** Cardiac output, cardiac index, pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP), systemic vascular resistance (SVR), mixed venous oxygen saturation (SvO₂) [^1_1]
- **Pregnancy targets:** PCWP 12–18 mmHg (normal pregnancy range), SvO₂ >65–70% [^1_1][^1_3]


### Minimally Invasive/Non-Invasive Cardiac Output Monitoring

- **Echocardiography (TTE):** Preferred for volume assessment in term gravid; avoids CVP pitfalls [^1_11][^1_10]
- **Non-invasive cardiac output monitoring:** Recommended for peripartum cardiomyopathy, severe preeclampsia with organ dysfunction [^1_11]
- **IVC ultrasound:** May not be accurate in term gravid due to aortocaval compression [^1_10]


### Biochemical Monitoring

| Parameter | Target/Interpretation in Pregnancy |
| :-- | :-- |
| Lactate | Elevated lactate indicates tissue hypoperfusion; pregnancy does not alter interpretation [^1_1] |
| SvO₂ (mixed venous O₂ saturation) | >65–70%; reflects balance between oxygen delivery and consumption [^1_1] |
| Veno-arterial CO₂ difference | Elevated in low flow states; useful adjunct to lactate [^1_1] |
| Base deficit | Normal pregnancy HCO₃⁻ is 18–21 mEq/L; interpret accordingly [^1_1][^1_3] |
| Arterial blood gases | Target PaO₂ ≥70 mmHg (≥9 kPa), PaCO₂ 28–32 mmHg (4.0–4.5 kPa) [^1_9][^1_10][^1_3] |

## Respiratory Monitoring

### Oxygenation Targets

- **PaO₂:** ≥70 mmHg (≥9 kPa) to ensure adequate fetal oxygen transfer [^1_9][^1_10]
- **SpO₂:** >92% (institutional practice; evidence limited) [^1_16]
- **Rationale:** Fetal oxygenation is dependent on maternal PaO₂; placental oxygen transfer requires adequate gradient [^1_9][^1_3]


### Ventilation Targets

- **PaCO₂:** 28–32 mmHg (4.0–4.5 kPa) — **lower than non-pregnant** [^1_9][^1_3][^1_6]
- **Avoid permissive hypercapnia:** Fetal acidosis develops with maternal hypercapnia; limited respiratory reserve [^1_2][^1_3]
- **Minute ventilation:** Increased 50% above baseline; monitor for respiratory muscle fatigue [^1_1][^1_3]


### Special Considerations

- **Rapid desaturation:** Reduced FRC means pregnant patients desaturate faster during apnea or respiratory failure [^1_2][^1_3][^1_8]
- **Aspiration risk:** All critically ill pregnant patients treated as **full stomach** due to decreased lower esophageal sphincter tone and delayed gastric emptying [^1_2]
- **Airway edema:** Increased mucosal edema may complicate intubation; prepare for difficult airway [^1_8]


## Fetal Monitoring

### General Principles

- **Maternal welfare takes priority:** No imaging, treatment, or medication should be withheld if necessary for maternal well-being [^1_4][^1_5]
- **Fetal monitoring as hemodynamic parameter:** Once viable, fetal heart rate reflects placental perfusion and maternal cardiorespiratory status [^1_17][^1_18]
- **Gestational age threshold:** Fetal monitoring is **not indicated before 24 weeks** (viability threshold 20–25+6 weeks) [^1_4][^1_18]


### Frequency and Modality

| Gestational Age | Monitoring Approach |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Previable (<24 weeks)** | Fetal heart auscultation daily to weekly to confirm ongoing pregnancy; management should not deviate based on fetal status [^1_18] |
| **Viable (≥24 weeks)** | Individualized based on maternal acuity; **minimum daily monitoring** recommended [^1_19][^1_18] |
| **Maternal instability** | Continuous electronic fetal monitoring (EFM) with obstetric nurse presence [^1_16][^1_13][^1_18] |
| **Maternal stability** | Intermittent monitoring (every 4–8 hours or daily) [^1_16][^1_19] |
| **Mechanically ventilated/ECMO** | Continuous monitoring often recommended until maternal stability established [^1_18][^1_20] |

### Fetal Monitoring Modalities

**Continuous Electronic Fetal Monitoring (EFM):**

- Indicated in: Maternal hemodynamic instability, fetal distress, peripartum period, during maternal procedures [^1_16][^1_17]
- Provides: Real-time assessment of fetal heart rate patterns, variability, decelerations
- Requires: Obstetric/nursing presence for interpretation and intervention [^1_16]

**Intermittent Monitoring:**

- **Non-stress test (NST):** Daily or as clinically indicated
- **Biophysical profile (BPP):** Ultrasound-based; evaluates fetal movement, breathing, tone, amniotic fluid volume [^1_16][^1_4]
- **Umbilical artery Doppler:** Weekly for fetal growth restriction surveillance [^1_19]

**Ultrasound:**

- **Fetal growth:** Every fortnight (2 weeks) for growth monitoring [^1_19]
- **Amniotic fluid volume:** Part of BPP; reflects placental function
- **Fetal anatomy:** As indicated for congenital anomalies


### Response to Fetal Distress

When fetal monitoring abnormalities occur:

1. **Reassess maternal status:** Check MAP, oxygenation, acid-base status, aortocaval compression [^1_17]
2. **Maternal resuscitation:** Left lateral decubitus position, optimize oxygenation and perfusion [^1_17][^1_9]
3. **Intrauterine fetal resuscitation:** Maternal oxygen, IV fluids, position change, treat hypotension [^1_17]
4. **Multidisciplinary decision:** If fetal distress persists despite maternal optimization, consider delivery based on gestational age and maternal stability [^1_21][^1_13]

## Laboratory and Imaging Monitoring

### Laboratory Investigations

**Frequency:** Daily in unstable patients; frequency reduced as stability achieved [^1_13][^1_7]


| Investigation | Frequency | Rationale |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| CBC (Hb, platelets, WBC) | Daily or more frequent if bleeding | Physiological anemia, thrombocytopenia, infection monitoring [^1_1][^1_13] |
| Coagulation profile (PT, aPTT, fibrinogen) | Daily or with bleeding | Hypercoagulable state; DIC risk in sepsis, abruption, amniotic fluid embolism [^1_13] |
| Renal function (creatinine, electrolytes) | Daily | Baseline creatinine lower (0.4–0.7 mg/dL); AKI detection [^1_2][^1_3] |
| Liver function tests | Daily in preeclampsia/HELLP | Transaminases, bilirubin, LDH for HELLP syndrome [^1_13] |
| Arterial blood gases | As clinically indicated; frequent if ventilated | Target PaO₂ ≥70 mmHg, PaCO₂ 28–32 mmHg [^1_9][^1_10] |
| Lactate | Every 4–6 hours in shock | Tissue perfusion marker [^1_1] |
| Magnesium levels | Every 4–6 hours if on MgSO₄ | Therapeutic range 4–7 mEq/L; toxicity monitoring [^1_13] |

### Imaging

**General principle:** Do not withhold necessary imaging due to fetal concerns; balance risk-benefit for maternal welfare [^1_4][^1_5]

- **Chest X-ray:** As required (not routine daily); use abdominal shielding when feasible [^1_16][^1_5]
- **CT pulmonary angiography:** For suspected pulmonary embolism when benefit outweighs risk [^1_16][^1_5]
- **CT head:** For neurological deterioration (eclampsia, stroke) — fetal radiation exposure minimal [^1_5]
- **Ultrasound:** Preferred for abdominal/pelvic imaging; no ionizing radiation [^1_16]
- **MRI:** Safe in pregnancy; useful for neurological or soft tissue evaluation [^1_4]

**Radiation mitigation:**

- Limit ionizing radiation exposure when feasible
- Use shielding
- Consider alternative modalities (US, MRI) when diagnostically equivalent [^1_5]


## Scoring Systems and Risk Stratification

### Maternal Severity Scores

**Note:** Standard ICU scoring systems (APACHE II, SAPS II, SOFA, MODS) are **validated for non-obstetric patients** and may not accurately predict mortality in pregnancy due to physiological alterations. [^1_19]

- **APACHE II/SAPS II:** Based on physiological variables; pregnancy-specific norms may affect accuracy [^1_19]
- **SOFA/MODS:** Organ failure-based; useful for tracking organ dysfunction progression [^1_19]
- **MEOWS (Modified Early Obstetric Warning Score):** Pregnancy-specific; triggers early intervention [^1_7]


### Obstetric-Specific Considerations

- **Sepsis in pregnancy:** May present atypically; physiological leukocytosis and tachycardia can mask infection [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Preeclampsia severity:** Based on BP, proteinuria, end-organ dysfunction (platelets, creatinine, LFTs, neurological symptoms) [^1_13][^1_7]
- **Hemorrhage:** >500 mL blood loss is abnormal; monitor for coagulopathy and organ dysfunction [^1_13]


## Special Monitoring Scenarios

### Preeclampsia/Eclampsia

**Monitoring priorities:** [^1_11][^1_13][^1_7]

- **BP:** Continuous invasive or q5–15min non-invasive during acute phase; target SBP <160 mmHg, DBP <110 mmHg
- **Neurological:** DTRs, clonus, visual changes, headache, GCS
- **Urine output:** Hourly; target ≥0.5 mL/kg/h (assesses renal perfusion and MgSO₄ clearance)
- **Magnesium levels:** q4–6h if on MgSO₄ infusion; therapeutic 4–7 mEq/L
- **Labs:** Daily CBC, creatinine, LFTs, LDH, uric acid for HELLP surveillance


### Peripartum Cardiomyopathy

**Monitoring:** [^1_11][^1_14][^1_15]

- **Continuous ECG:** Arrhythmia detection
- **Invasive BP:** If on inotropes/vasopressors
- **Cardiac output monitoring:** Non-invasive or echocardiography
- **Daily weight and fluid balance:** Avoid fluid overload
- **Respiratory monitoring:** Early detection of pulmonary edema


### Sepsis/Septic Shock in Pregnancy

**Monitoring:** [^1_1][^1_13][^1_2]

- **Vital signs:** Continuous or q1h; recognize that tachycardia and leukocytosis may be physiological
- **Lactate:** q4–6h until clearing
- **Urine output:** Hourly; early marker of organ dysfunction
- **Coagulation:** Daily or more frequent if DIC suspected
- **Fetal monitoring:** Continuous if viable and maternal instability


### ARDS in Pregnancy

**Monitoring:** [^1_9][^1_10][^1_3]

- **ABGs:** Frequent; target PaO₂ ≥70 mmHg, avoid permissive hypercapnia
- **Plateau pressure:** Keep <30 mmHg (lower threshold than non-pregnant due to reduced chest wall compliance)
- **Fluid balance:** Conservative; pulmonary edema risk increased
- **Fetal monitoring:** Continuous if viable; maternal hypoxemia directly affects fetus


## Transport Monitoring

For critically ill pregnant women requiring inter- or intra-hospital transport: [^1_12][^1_5]

**Minimum monitoring requirements:**

- Continuous cardiac rhythm monitoring
- Continuous pulse oximetry
- Regular vital signs (BP, HR, RR, temperature)
- Invasive arterial line if hemodynamically unstable
- Portable fetal monitor if viable and feasible

**Fetal monitoring during transport:**

- Individualized based on gestational age, maternal stability, and feasibility of intervention [^1_5]
- Continuous if viable and maternal instability; intermittent if stable


## Postpartum Monitoring

**Critical period:** First 24–48 hours postpartum carries high risk of decompensation due to: [^1_2]

- Autotransfusion from uterine involution
- Mobilization of extravascular fluid
- Hemodynamic shifts

**Monitoring recommendations:** [^1_11][^1_7]

- **Vital signs:** q4h for at least 24 hours after birth (more frequent if complications)
- **Respiratory:** RR and chest auscultation q4h for pulmonary edema detection
- **Fluid balance:** Strict input-output monitoring
- **BP and HR:** q4h minimum; continuous if preeclampsia or cardiac disease
- **Oxygen saturation:** Continuous or q30min if high risk [^1_11]


## Key Clinical Pearls for DRNB

1. **Physiological norms differ:** Do not apply non-pregnant reference ranges for BP, HR, PaCO₂, creatinine, or HCO₃⁻. [^1_2][^1_3][^1_6]
2. **Shock is masked:** Up to 30–35% blood loss may occur before hypotension manifests due to increased blood volume. [^1_1][^1_2]
3. **Rapid desaturation:** Reduced FRC means pregnant patients desaturate 2–3× faster during apnea; pre-oxygenate aggressively before intubation. [^1_2][^1_3][^1_8]
4. **PaCO₂ target is lower:** 28–32 mmHg (4.0–4.5 kPa); "normal" non-pregnant PaCO₂ (40 mmHg) indicates hypercapnia in pregnancy. [^1_9][^1_3][^1_6]
5. **CVP is unreliable after 24–26 weeks:** Aortocaval compression causes spuriously elevated readings; use echocardiography for volume assessment. [^1_10]
6. **Maternal stabilization first:** Fetal well-being depends on maternal oxygenation and perfusion; prioritize maternal resuscitation. [^1_4][^1_5]
7. **Fetal monitoring starts at 24 weeks:** Before viability, confirm fetal heart daily but do not alter maternal management based on fetal status. [^1_18][^1_20]
8. **Left lateral position:** First intervention for maternal hypotension — excludes aortocaval compression. [^1_9][^1_2]
9. **Do not withhold imaging/medications:** Necessary diagnostics and therapeutics should not be withheld due to fetal concerns; balance risk-benefit for maternal welfare. [^1_4][^1_5]
10. **Postpartum is high-risk:** Autotransfusion and fluid mobilization can cause delayed pulmonary edema, hypertension, or heart failure despite apparent stability. [^1_2]
