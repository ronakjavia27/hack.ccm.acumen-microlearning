
## Microangiopathic Anemia and Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA) in Pregnancy — Exam Notes

Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA) in pregnancy is a life-threatening syndrome characterized by mechanical RBC destruction (schistocytes), thrombocytopenia, and end-organ injury. The key clinical challenge is distinguishing pregnancy-specific disorders (pre-eclampsia/HELLP, AFLP) from primary TMAs (TTP, complement-mediated aHUS) because management and urgency differ drastically. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3][^1_4]

***

## Definitions and Core Concepts

- **Microangiopathic Hemolytic Anemia (MAHA):** Hemolysis due to RBC fragmentation in small vessels; hallmark is schistocytes on peripheral smear, elevated LDH, low haptoglobin, indirect hyperbilirubinemia, and reticulocytosis. [^1_3][^1_5]
- **Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA):** Triad of MAHA + thrombocytopenia + organ damage (renal, neurologic, hepatic, placental). In pregnancy, TMA may be:
    - **Pregnancy-triggered/related:** Pre-eclampsia/HELLP (most common), AFLP (mimic).
    - **Primary hematologic/renal:** TTP (ADAMTS13 deficiency), complement-mediated aHUS (C-TMA). [^1_1][^1_6][^1_2][^1_7][^1_4]

***

## Causes of MAHA/TMA in Pregnancy

### Common pregnancy-related (secondary) TMAs

- **Pre-eclampsia with severe features** (after 20 weeks): hypertension + proteinuria/organ dysfunction; may have MAHA. [^1_8][^1_2][^1_7]
- **HELLP syndrome:** Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets; often a severe variant of pre-eclampsia. [^1_8][^1_2][^1_5]
- **Postpartum progression:** MAHA/thrombocytopenia that fails to improve 48–72 h after delivery should raise suspicion for TTP/aHUS. [^1_4][^1_9]


### Primary TMAs (must not miss)

- **Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP):** Acquired (autoimmune) or congenital ADAMTS13 deficiency; can occur any trimester/postpartum. [^1_1][^1_6][^1_7]
- **Complement-mediated HUS (aHUS/C-TMA):** Often postpartum; prominent AKI; diagnosis of exclusion after ruling out TTP and Shiga-toxin HUS. [^1_6][^1_2][^1_4]


### Other causes/mimics

- **Catastrophic antiphospholipid syndrome (CAPS)**, SLE flare. [^1_2]
- **DIC** (obstetric hemorrhage, sepsis, abruption, amniotic fluid embolism). [^1_3]
- **Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy (AFLP):** MAHA mimic with severe liver failure, coagulopathy, hypoglycemia. [^1_3][^1_10]
- **Mechanical hemolysis** (rare in pregnancy; e.g., prosthetic valve). [^1_3][^1_5]

***

## Clinical Presentation

Typical TMA features:

- **Symptoms:** Fatigue, pallor, headache, visual changes, epigastric/RUQ pain, nausea/vomiting, oliguria, edema, neurologic symptoms (confusion, seizures). [^1_1][^1_10]
- **Signs:** Hypertension (in pre-eclampsia/HELLP), jaundice, petechiae/purpura (more in TTP), oliguria/anuria, hepatic tenderness, altered sensorium. [^1_5][^1_10]

Red flags for primary TMA:

- Severe thrombocytopenia (<30–50 × 10⁹/L) with minimal hypertension/proteinuria.
- Disproportionate renal failure (aHUS) or neurologic symptoms (TTP).
- No improvement or worsening after delivery. [^1_4][^1_9]

***

## Differentiation from Pregnancy-Specific Disorders

Use timing, dominant organ involvement, coagulation profile, and specific tests.

### Differentiating table (high-yield for exams)

| Feature | Pre-eclampsia (severe) | HELLP | AFLP | TTP | Complement-mediated aHUS (C-TMA) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Typical timing** | >20 weeks (often 3rd trimester) | >20 weeks, late 3rd trimester/postpartum | 3rd trimester (30–38 weeks) | Any trimester / postpartum | Often postpartum (days–weeks) |
| **Hypertension/proteinuria** | Prominent | Common | May be absent/minimal | Often absent/mild | Often absent/mild |
| **Hemolysis (MAHA)** | Variable | Prominent | Possible (mimic) | Prominent | Prominent |
| **Platelets** | Mild–moderate ↓ | ↓ (often <100k) | ↓ (variable) | Severe ↓ (often <30k) | ↓ (variable, can be severe) |
| **Liver enzymes** | Mild–mod ↑ | ↑↑ (AST/ALT) | ↑↑↑, liver failure | Normal/mild | Normal/mild |
| **Renal involvement** | Variable | Variable | Variable | Mild–mod | Prominent AKI, oliguria |
| **Coagulation (PT/aPTT, fibrinogen)** | Usually normal | Usually normal | Prolonged PT/aPTT, low fibrinogen, hypoglycemia | Usually normal | Usually normal |
| **Glucose/ammonia** | Normal | Normal | Hypoglycemia, ↑ ammonia | Normal | Normal |
| **Key specific test** | BP, protein/creatinine ratio | Same + hemolysis markers | Hypoglycemia, coagulopathy, ammonia | ADAMTS13 activity <10% | Genetic/complement workup; diagnosis of exclusion |
| **Definitive therapy** | BP control + delivery | Delivery + supportive | Delivery + ICU/support | Plasma exchange ± steroids/caplacizumab | Eculizumab/ravulizumab + supportive |

Sources: [^1_11][^1_2][^1_3][^1_4][^1_10][^1_12]

**Practical bedside rule:** If MAHA + thrombocytopenia persist or worsen beyond 48–72 h postpartum despite delivery and supportive care, think TTP or aHUS until proven otherwise. [^1_4][^1_9]

***

## Investigations (Stepwise, Exam-Oriented)

### Immediate labs (all suspected TMA)

- CBC with platelets; peripheral blood film (schistocytes).
- Hemolysis panel: LDH (often >2× ULN), haptoglobin (low), indirect bilirubin, reticulocyte count.
- Renal function: creatinine, urea, electrolytes, urine protein/creatinine.
- Liver function: AST, ALT, bilirubin, albumin.
- Coagulation: PT, aPTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer (to exclude DIC).
- DAT (Coombs): typically negative in TMA.
- Uric acid (often elevated in pre-eclampsia/HELLP). [^1_1][^1_3][^1_5]


### Differentiation-focused tests

- **ADAMTS13 activity and inhibitor** (send urgently; do not wait to start PEX if TTP suspected). Activity <10% supports TTP. [^1_1][^1_6][^1_3][^1_9]
- **Complement studies:** C3, C4 (often normal in aHUS), genetic panel (CFH, CFI, MCP, C3, CFB, THBD) if available. [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Autoimmune screen:** ANA, dsDNA, antiphospholipid antibodies (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, anti-β2GP1) if CAPS/SLE suspected. [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Infection screen:** Shiga toxin (STEC-HUS), HIV, hepatitis as indicated. [^1_6]
- **AFLP clues:** Blood glucose (hypoglycemia), ammonia, coagulopathy profile, renal/liver failure pattern. [^1_3][^1_10]


### Imaging/monitoring

- Fetal monitoring (CTG, Dopplers), ultrasound for fetal growth and placental status.
- Renal ultrasound if AKI.
- Neuroimaging if focal neurologic deficits. [^1_9]

***

## Diagnosis: Practical Algorithm

1. **Confirm MAHA + thrombocytopenia + organ injury** (TMA phenotype). [^1_1][^1_2]
2. **Assess for pre-eclampsia/HELLP criteria** (BP, proteinuria, timing >20 weeks). If classic and improving after delivery → likely HELLP/pre-eclampsia. [^1_8][^1_4]
3. **Check coagulation and metabolic profile** to exclude DIC and AFLP (prolonged PT/aPTT, low fibrinogen, hypoglycemia, high ammonia). [^1_3][^1_10]
4. **Send ADAMTS13 urgently.** If clinical suspicion high (severe thrombocytopenia, neuro signs, any trimester), start plasma exchange while awaiting result. [^1_1][^1_6][^1_9]
5. **If ADAMTS13 >10% and renal-predominant injury (especially postpartum):** consider complement-mediated aHUS; involve nephrology/hematology; consider eculizumab if progressive. [^1_6][^1_2][^1_4]
6. **If no improvement 48–72 h postpartum:** escalate workup for TTP/aHUS even if initially labeled HELLP. [^1_4][^1_9]

***

## Management

### General supportive measures (all TMAs)

- **ICU/HDU admission** for severe cases; multidisciplinary team (obstetrics, hematology, nephrology, ICU).
- **Blood pressure control:** IV labetalol, hydralazine, or oral nifedipine as per pre-eclampsia protocols.
- **Seizure prophylaxis:** Magnesium sulfate for severe pre-eclampsia/HELLP/eclampsia.
- **Transfusion:**
    - Packed RBCs for symptomatic/severe anemia.
    - Platelets: generally avoid unless life-threatening bleeding or prior to procedures (especially in TTP, may worsen microthrombi). [^1_1][^1_9]
- **Avoid nephrotoxins**; maintain euvolemia.
- **Fetal monitoring** and timing of delivery based on maternal stability and gestational age. [^1_9]


### Condition-specific management

#### Pre-eclampsia/HELLP

- **Definitive:** Delivery when maternal/fetal indications met (non-reassuring status, worsening labs, organ failure, eclampsia). [^1_4][^1_9]
- **Supportive:** BP control, magnesium sulfate, correct coagulopathy if present, monitor labs (usually normalize within 48–72 h postpartum). [^1_9][^1_10]


#### AFLP

- **Definitive:** Urgent delivery.
- **ICU support:** Correct hypoglycemia (IV dextrose), manage coagulopathy (FFP/cryoprecipitate/platelets), treat hepatic encephalopathy, renal support if needed. [^1_3][^1_10]


#### TTP (suspected or confirmed)

- **Plasma exchange (PEX):** Start immediately; do not wait for ADAMTS13. Typical regimen: 40–60 mL/kg/day until platelets normalize and LDH trends down. [^1_1][^1_6][^1_9]
- **Adjuncts:**
    - Glucocorticoids (e.g., prednisone 1 mg/kg or pulse dexamethasone).
    - Caplacizumab (anti-vWF A1 domain) in acquired TTP where available.
    - Rituximab for refractory/relapsing cases. [^1_9]
- **Platelet transfusion:** Avoid unless catastrophic bleeding or urgent invasive procedure. [^1_1]


#### Complement-mediated aHUS (C-TMA)

- **Rule out TTP** (ADAMTS13 >10%) and STEC-HUS.
- **Complement inhibition:** Eculizumab (or ravulizumab) as soon as diagnosis is strongly suspected, especially with progressive AKI/postpartum TMA. [^1_6][^1_2][^1_4]
- **Supportive:** Dialysis if indicated, BP control, transfusion as needed.
- **Vaccination:** Meningococcal vaccination prior to eculizumab if time permits; otherwise give prophylactic antibiotics. [^1_9]


#### DIC

- Treat underlying cause (hemorrhage, sepsis, abruption).
- Replace blood products guided by labs (FFP, cryo, platelets, RBCs).

***

## Prognosis and Follow-up

- **HELLP/pre-eclampsia:** Most resolve within 72 h postpartum; recurrence risk in future pregnancies is significant; long-term cardiovascular/renal risk increased. [^1_9]
- **TTP:** High maternal/fetal mortality if untreated; with PEX, survival >80–90%. Relapse risk in subsequent pregnancies; monitor ADAMTS13 in future pregnancies. [^1_6][^1_9]
- **aHUS:** Risk of ESRD without complement blockade; eculizumab improves renal outcomes. Recurrence in future pregnancies is high; plan pre-conception counseling. [^1_6][^1_2][^1_4]

***

## Key Clinical Pearls (Exam/Bedside)

- MAHA + thrombocytopenia after 20 weeks: think pre-eclampsia/HELLP first; but **do not anchor** if severe thrombocytopenia, neuro signs, or renal failure dominate. [^1_7][^1_4]
- **LDH >2× ULN + schistocytes** is a strong TMA signal; DAT negative helps exclude autoimmune hemolysis. [^1_1][^1_5]
- **ADAMTS13 <10% = TTP** until proven otherwise; start PEX immediately. [^1_1][^1_6][^1_3]
- **Postpartum TMA that worsens or fails to improve by 72 h** → urgently reconsider TTP/aHUS. [^1_4][^1_9]
- **AFLP triad:** hypoglycemia + coagulopathy + liver failure in late 3rd trimester; delivery is life-saving. [^1_3][^1_10]
- **Platelet transfusion in TTP** can exacerbate microthrombosis; reserve for life-threatening bleeding/procedures. [^1_1]

***

## Suggested Exam-Style Answer Structure

If asked to “discuss MAHA/TMA in pregnancy”:

1. Define MAHA and TMA.
2. List causes (pregnancy-related vs primary TMAs vs mimics).
3. Present a differentiation table (HELLP vs pre-eclampsia vs AFLP vs TTP vs aHUS).
4. Outline investigations (immediate + specific tests like ADAMTS13, complement).
5. Give a diagnostic algorithm (including 48–72 h postpartum rule).
6. Detail management (supportive + condition-specific: delivery, PEX, eculizumab).
7. End with prognosis and 2–3 high-yield pearls.
