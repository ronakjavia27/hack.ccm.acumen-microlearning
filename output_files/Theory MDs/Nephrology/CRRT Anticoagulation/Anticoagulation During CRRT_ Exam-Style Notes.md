
## Anticoagulation During CRRT:

Anticoagulation during continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) aims to maintain extracorporeal circuit patency while minimizing bleeding risk, filter clotting, and metabolic complications. Regional citrate anticoagulation (RCA) is now first-line in most guidelines, with unfractionated heparin (UFH) as the main alternative when citrate is contraindicated. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3]

***

## 1. Why Anticoagulate in CRRT?

- Prevent circuit/filter clotting → prolong filter life, maintain solute/fluid clearance.
- Avoid repeated circuit changes → reduce blood loss, cost, nursing workload.
- Balance against bleeding risk in critically ill patients (coagulopathy, thrombocytopenia, recent surgery). [^1_4][^1_1][^1_5]

***

## 2. Anticoagulation Strategies Overview

| Strategy | When to Use | Key Points |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **No anticoagulation** | High bleeding risk, severe coagulopathy, recent major surgery, active bleeding | Use saline flushes, high blood flow (>200 mL/min), predilution; expect shorter filter life. [^1_4][^1_6] |
| **Systemic anticoagulation (UFH/LMWH)** | Low bleeding risk, no citrate availability/contraindications | UFH: continuous infusion, monitor aPTT/anti-Xa; LMWH: once/twice daily, monitor anti-Xa. [^1_4][^1_2] |
| **Regional Citrate Anticoagulation (RCA)** | Preferred first-line in most patients (KDIGO 2B recommendation) | Citrate infused pre-filter chelates calcium → prevents clotting; calcium replaced post-filter to prevent systemic hypocalcemia. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_7] |
| **Other options** | Specific scenarios (HIT, severe liver failure, institutional protocols) | Nafamostat (Japan), argatroban/bivalirudin (HIT), prostaglandins (adjunct). [^1_8][^1_3][^1_7] |


***

## 3. Regional Citrate Anticoagulation (RCA) – The Preferred Choice

### Mechanism

- Citrate chelates ionized calcium (iCa) in the circuit → prevents activation of calcium-dependent coagulation cascade.
- Systemic calcium is maintained by separate calcium infusion post-filter.
- Citrate is metabolized in liver, skeletal muscle, kidney → bicarbonate (risk of metabolic alkalosis). [^1_9][^1_10]


### Protocol Essentials

- **Citrate infusion**: Pre-filter (arterial limb), typically as trisodium citrate or ACD-A.
- **Target filter iCa**: 0.25–0.35 mmol/L.
- **Target systemic iCa**: 1.0–1.2 mmol/L.
- **Calcium infusion**: Post-filter (venous limb), titrated to maintain systemic iCa.
- **Monitoring**: Circuit iCa, systemic iCa, total calcium, acid-base status, sodium. [^1_9][^1_10][^1_11]


### Key Monitoring Parameter: Calcium Ratio

- **Total calcium : Ionized calcium ratio** (systemic).
- **Normal**: <2.1.
- **>2.5**: Suggests citrate accumulation/toxicity (citrate lock) → reduce/stop citrate, switch to heparin. [^1_9]


### Contraindications / Cautions

- Severe liver failure (impaired citrate metabolism).
- Severe shock with poor perfusion (reduced citrate clearance).
- Severe metabolic alkalosis or acidosis (unstable acid-base).
- Hypocalcemia refractory to replacement.
- Not recommended in SCUF mode (citrate not effectively cleared). [^1_10][^1_11]

***

## 4. Unfractionated Heparin (UFH) – The Traditional Option

### Dosing

- **Prime circuit**: 5,000–10,000 U in 1 L saline.
- **Bolus**: 1,000–5,000 U (optional, pre-filter).
- **Continuous infusion**: 5–20 U/kg/hr (typical: 8–10 U/kg/hr).
- **Target**: aPTT 1–1.4× control or anti-Xa 0.25–0.35 IU/mL. [^1_4][^1_6]


### Advantages

- Widely available, inexpensive.
- Easily reversible with protamine (100 U heparin ≈ 1 mg protamine).
- Simple monitoring (aPTT, anti-Xa). [^1_4][^1_12]


### Pitfalls

- Systemic anticoagulation → bleeding risk.
- Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT).
- Heparin resistance (antithrombin III deficiency).
- Requires frequent monitoring. [^1_4][^1_12]

***

## 5. Low Molecular Weight Heparin (LMWH)

- **Dosing**: Once/twice daily, weight-based (e.g., enoxaparin 0.5–1 mg/kg).
- **Monitoring**: Anti-Xa levels (target 0.25–0.35 IU/mL).
- **Pros**: Predictable pharmacokinetics, less monitoring.
- **Cons**: Higher bleeding risk vs UFH, not easily reversible, accumulates in renal failure (use with caution in AKI). [^1_6][^1_2][^1_7]

***

## 6. Anticoagulation in High Bleeding Risk Patients

- **Preferred**: RCA (if no contraindications) → regional effect, minimal systemic anticoagulation. [^1_1][^1_8]
- **Alternatives**:
    - No anticoagulation + saline flushes (50–200 mL every 30–60 min).
    - Nafamostat mesylate (short half-life, protease inhibitor; available mainly in Japan).
    - Prostaglandin I2 (epoprostenol) as adjunct to low-dose heparin. [^1_8][^1_7][^1_11]

***

## 7. Pitfalls \& Complications – Must Know for Exams

| Anticoagulant | Common Pitfalls | How to Avoid/Manage |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Citrate (RCA)** | Citrate accumulation (liver failure, shock), metabolic alkalosis, hypocalcemia, hypernatremia (trisodium citrate), ionized calcium monitoring errors | Monitor total:iCa ratio (<2.1), systemic iCa, acid-base; reduce citrate dose or switch to heparin if toxicity suspected. [^1_13][^1_9][^1_10] |
| **UFH** | Bleeding, HIT, heparin resistance, over-anticoagulation | Monitor aPTT/anti-Xa, platelet count; use protamine for reversal if needed. [^1_4][^1_12] |
| **LMWH** | Accumulation in AKI, bleeding, difficult reversal | Avoid in severe AKI unless monitored; use anti-Xa levels. [^1_6][^1_2] |
| **No anticoagulation** | Frequent clotting, reduced filter life, increased nursing workload | Use high blood flow, predilution, regular saline flushes. [^1_4][^1_5] |


***

## 8. Non-Anticoagulant Measures to Prolong Circuit Life

- **High blood flow rate**: >200 mL/min (reduces stasis).
- **Predilution mode**: Dilutes blood before filter → reduces hemoconcentration.
- **Avoid air bubbles**: Prime circuit thoroughly, minimize blood-air contact.
- **Use appropriate filter surface**: Biocompatible membranes reduce activation.
- **Timely saline flushes** (if no anticoagulation): 50–200 mL every 30–60 min. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_11]

***

## 9. Guideline Recommendations (KDIGO 2012, Updated Evidence)

- **5.3.2.2**: Use RCA over heparin in CRRT patients without contraindications (Grade 2B). [^1_1][^1_2]
- **5.3.2.3**: If citrate contraindicated, use UFH or LMWH over other anticoagulants (Grade 2C). [^1_2]
- RCA reduces bleeding events and prolongs filter life compared to UFH (meta-analyses). [^1_8][^1_13]

***

## 10. Quick Exam Pearls (High-Yield)

1. **First-line anticoagulation in CRRT**: Regional citrate anticoagulation (RCA). [^1_1][^1_2]
2. **Target filter iCa in RCA**: 0.25–0.35 mmol/L. [^1_9][^1_10]
3. **Citrate toxicity marker**: Total calcium : ionized calcium ratio >2.5. [^1_9]
4. **Heparin reversal agent**: Protamine sulfate (100 U heparin = 1 mg protamine). [^1_14]
5. **Avoid LMWH in severe AKI**: Risk of accumulation and bleeding. [^1_6][^1_2]
6. **RCA contraindications**: Severe liver failure, shock, severe acid-base disturbances. [^1_10][^1_11]
7. **No anticoagulation strategy**: High blood flow + predilution + saline flushes. [^1_4][^1_5]
8. **HIT suspicion in CRRT**: Switch to argatroban/bivalirudin or nafamostat (if available). [^1_3][^1_7]

***

## 11. Suggested Algorithm (Simplified)

```
Start CRRT → Assess bleeding risk
│
├─ Low bleeding risk → RCA (first-line)
│   ├─ Monitor: filter iCa, systemic iCa, total:iCa ratio, acid-base
│   └─ If citrate contraindicated → UFH (target aPTT 1–1.4×)
│
├─ High bleeding risk → RCA (preferred) OR No anticoagulation + saline flushes
│   └─ Consider nafamostat/prostaglandins if available
│
└─ HIT or heparin contraindication → Argatroban/bivalirudin or RCA
```


***

## 12. Landmark Trials \& Evidence

- **Meta-analyses (2023–2024)**: RCA vs UFH → longer filter life, fewer bleeding events. [^1_8][^1_13]
- **KDIGO Guidelines (2012, reaffirmed in later reviews)**: RCA recommended as first-line (2B). [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Single-center experience (2026)**: Despite RCA superiority, ~37% of circuits fail before 72 h; early failures (within 24 h) common → emphasizes technique, monitoring, and non-anticoagulant measures. [^1_13]

***

## 13. Practical Clinical Pearls

- **RCA is not "no anticoagulation"**: It's regional; patient remains minimally anticoagulated systemically. [^1_1][^1_15]
- **Citrate metabolism produces bicarbonate**: Watch for metabolic alkalosis, especially in liver dysfunction. [^1_9][^1_10]
- **Calcium infusion site matters**: Always post-filter; pre-filter calcium infusion negates citrate effect. [^1_10]
- **Filter clotting despite anticoagulation**: Check blood flow rate, predilution fraction, circuit air bubbles, and catheter position before escalating anticoagulation. [^1_5][^1_11]
- **Heparin resistance**: Consider antithrombin III deficiency; may need AT-III replacement or switch to RCA. [^1_12]
