
## Nutrition in Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

### Exam-Oriented, High-Yield Notes


***

## 1. Definition \& Goals of Nutritional Therapy

**Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)** is defined as abnormalities of kidney structure or function present for >3 months, with implications for health. [^1_1]

**Goals of nutrition in CKD:**

- Slow progression of kidney disease
- Prevent/treat protein-energy wasting (PEW) and malnutrition
- Control metabolic complications (hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, acidosis, fluid overload)
- Reduce cardiovascular risk
- Maintain adequate growth in children [^1_2][^1_3]

***

## 2. Energy (Calorie) Requirements

| Patient Group | Energy Requirement |
| :-- | :-- |
| **CKD G3–G5 (non-dialysis), <60 years** | 35 kcal/kg/day |
| **CKD G3–G5 (non-dialysis), ≥60 years or obese** | 30–35 kcal/kg/day |
| **Hemodialysis (HD)** | 30–35 kcal/kg/day |
| **Peritoneal Dialysis (PD)** | 30–35 kcal/kg/day (account for glucose absorption from dialysate) |
| **Diabetic CKD** | Individualize; avoid excessive calories |

**Sources:** Carbohydrates (55–60%), fats (30–35%), proteins (as per stage) [^1_3][^1_4][^1_5]

**Key point:** Inadequate energy intake → catabolism of muscle protein → worsens PEW.

***

## 3. Protein Requirements (Most Important for Exams)

| CKD Stage | Protein Intake | Comments |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **CKD G1–G2** | 0.8–1.0 g/kg/day | Normal RDA |
| **CKD G3–G5 (non-dialysis)** | **0.6–0.8 g/kg/day** (KDIGO: 0.8 g/kg/day) | Avoid >1.3 g/kg/day (risk of progression) [^1_1][^1_6][^1_7] |
| **Very low-protein diet (selected patients)** | 0.3–0.4 g/kg/day + ketoacid/amino acid supplements | Only in metabolically stable patients under supervision [^1_1][^1_8] |
| **Hemodialysis** | **1.0–1.2 g/kg/day** (some guidelines: 1.1–1.4 g/kg/day) | Higher due to dialysis losses [^1_9] |
| **Peritoneal Dialysis** | **1.2–1.3 g/kg/day** | Higher due to peritoneal protein losses [^1_9] |
| **Children with CKD** | **Do NOT restrict** (risk of growth failure) [^1_1] | Age-specific RDA |

**Protein quality matters:**

- ≥50% should be **high biological value (HBV)** proteins (egg, milk, meat, fish, soy)
- Plant-based proteins preferred over animal-based (KDIGO 2024) for cardiovascular and metabolic benefits [^1_6][^1_7][^1_8]

**Exam pearl:**
**"0.8 g/kg/day in non-dialysis CKD; 1.2 g/kg/day in dialysis"**

***

## 4. Sodium Restriction

| Recommendation | Value |
| :-- | :-- |
| **KDIGO 2024** | **<2 g sodium/day** (= <5 g NaCl/day = <90 mmol/day) [^1_1][^1_6] |
| **ADA/KDIGO (diabetes + CKD)** | 1500–2300 mg/day [^1_10][^1_11] |

**Benefits:**

- Better BP control
- Reduced albuminuria
- Decreased cardiovascular risk
- Less fluid retention

**Clinical tip:** Avoid ultra-processed foods (major source of hidden sodium) [^1_2][^1_8]

***

## 5. Potassium Management

| Scenario | Recommendation |
| :-- | :-- |
| **CKD G1–G3, normal K⁺** | Encourage potassium-rich plant foods (fruits, vegetables) [^1_2] |
| **CKD G4–G5 or hyperkalemia** | Restrict high-potassium foods; individualize based on labs [^1_2][^1_4] |
| **Dialysis patients** | Typically 2–3 g/day (adjust per pre-dialysis K⁺) |

**High-potassium foods to limit (if needed):**

- Bananas, oranges, dried fruits, potatoes, tomatoes, spinach, coconut water, salt substitutes (KCl)

**Strategies to reduce potassium in vegetables:**

- Peel, chop, soak in water, boil in large volume of water, discard water

**Exam pearl:**
**"Potassium restriction only when hyperkalemic or advanced CKD"**

***

## 6. Phosphorus Restriction

| CKD Stage | Phosphorus Intake |
| :-- | :-- |
| **CKD G3–G5** | **800–1000 mg/day** (or 10–12 mg/kg/day) [^1_4] |
| **Dialysis** | 800–1000 mg/day + phosphate binders if needed |

**Key concepts:**

- **Organic phosphorus** (plant-based): poorly absorbed (30–50%)
- **Inorganic phosphorus** (additives in processed foods): nearly 100% absorbed → **AVOID** [^1_8][^1_4]

**High-phosphorus foods to limit:**

- Dairy products, organ meats, egg yolk, nuts, seeds, colas, processed meats, fast food

**Phosphate binders:**

- Calcium-based (calcium carbonate, acetate)
- Non-calcium, non-aluminum (sevelamer, lanthanum)
- Avoid aluminum-containing binders (toxicity)

**Exam pearl:**
**"Plant phosphorus < animal phosphorus < food additive phosphorus (in absorption)"**

***

## 7. Fluid Management

| Clinical Situation | Fluid Recommendation |
| :-- | :-- |
| **CKD G1–G3, no edema** | Ad libitum (normal intake) |
| **CKD G4–G5 with oliguria/edema** | Restrict to **urine output + 500–750 mL/day** |
| **Hemodialysis** | Inter-dialytic weight gain <2–3 kg (or <4% of dry weight) |
| **Peritoneal Dialysis** | More liberal (continuous removal) |

**Clinical signs of fluid overload:** Edema, hypertension, dyspnea, elevated JVP, crackles

***

## 8. Micronutrients \& Vitamins in CKD

### Water-Soluble Vitamins (Lost in Dialysis)

| Vitamin | Status in CKD | Supplementation |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **B-complex (B1, B2, B3, B6, B12, folate)** | Often deficient (dialysis losses, poor intake) | **Supplement routinely in dialysis** (renal-specific multivitamin) [^1_12] |
| **Vitamin C** | Risk of deficiency | 60–100 mg/day (avoid >200 mg/day → oxalate accumulation) [^1_12] |
| **Vitamin B12 \& folate** | Deficiency common | Supplement if low (B2 recommendation) [^1_12] |

### Fat-Soluble Vitamins

| Vitamin | Status in CKD | Recommendation |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Vitamin A** | **Accumulates** (reduced clearance) | **Do NOT supplement routinely** (toxicity risk: bone disease, anemia) [^1_12][^1_13] |
| **Vitamin D** | Deficiency very common (reduced 1α-hydroxylation) | **Supplement cholecalciferol/ergocalciferol** to correct 25(OH)D deficiency (2C) [^1_12] |
| **Vitamin E** | No routine supplementation | Avoid unless deficiency proven [^1_12] |
| **Vitamin K** | Emerging data (vascular calcification) | Not routinely supplemented |

### Trace Elements

| Element | Status | Recommendation |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Iron** | Deficiency common (anemia of CKD) | IV or oral iron per anemia guidelines |
| **Zinc** | May be low | Supplement if deficient (taste abnormalities, poor wound healing) |
| **Selenium** | Variable | Routine supplementation not recommended |
| **Copper, Chromium** | Usually adequate | Avoid excess |

**Exam pearl:**
**"Avoid vitamin A in CKD; supplement water-soluble vitamins in dialysis"**

***

## 9. Special Considerations

### A. Plant-Based vs Animal-Based Diets

**KDIGO 2024 strongly favors plant-based diets:** [^1_6][^1_7][^1_8]


| Advantage | Mechanism |
| :-- | :-- |
| Lower acid load | Reduces metabolic acidosis |
| Lower phosphorus bioavailability | Plant phosphorus less absorbed |
| Higher fiber | Improves gut microbiome, reduces uremic toxins |
| Cardiovascular benefits | Lower saturated fat, cholesterol |
| Slower CKD progression | Reduced albuminuria, inflammation |

**Caution:** Monitor potassium in advanced CKD

### B. Protein-Energy Wasting (PEW)

**Diagnostic criteria (any 3 of 4):**

- Low BMI (<23 kg/m²)
- Low muscle mass
- Low serum albumin (<3.8 g/dL)
- Low dietary protein/energy intake

**Management:**

- Increase energy intake (oral nutritional supplements)
- Increase protein (within stage-appropriate limits)
- Treat underlying inflammation, acidosis, depression
- Consider anabolic agents (in selected cases)


### C. Diabetic CKD

- Carbohydrates: 45–60% of calories (low glycemic index, high fiber)
- Avoid refined sugars, sugar-sweetened beverages [^1_11]
- Protein: 0.8 g/kg/day (KDIGO/ADA) [^1_11]
- Sodium: <2 g/day [^1_10]


### D. Elderly \& Frail CKD Patients

- Higher protein may be needed (1.0–1.2 g/kg/day) to prevent sarcopenia [^1_8]
- Avoid overly restrictive diets
- Individualize based on functional status, comorbidities

## 11. Landmark Guidelines \& Trials

| Guideline | Year | Key Recommendation |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **KDIGO 2024 CKD Guideline** | 2024 | Protein 0.8 g/kg/day; plant-based diet; sodium <2 g/day [^1_1][^1_6] |
| **KDOQI 2020 Nutrition** | 2020 | Protein 0.55–0.8 g/kg/day (non-dialysis); energy 25–35 kcal/kg/day [^1_14][^1_11] |
| **UK Kidney Association 2019** | 2019 | Protein 0.8–1.0 g/kg/day (CKD 4–5); 1.1–1.4 g/kg/day (HD) [^1_9] |
| **MDRD Trial** | 1990s | Low-protein diet slowed CKD progression (subset analysis) |
| **Modification of Diet in Renal Disease (MDRD)** | — | Foundation for protein restriction in CKD |

## 12. Clinical Pearls (High-Yield for Exams)

- **"0.8 g/kg/day"** is the magic number for protein in non-dialysis CKD (KDIGO 2024) [^1_1][^1_6]
- **Avoid high-protein diets (>1.3 g/kg/day)** in CKD at risk of progression [^1_1][^1_7]
- **Plant-based > animal-based** proteins (cardiovascular + metabolic benefits) [^1_6][^1_8]
- **Sodium <2 g/day** (= 5 g salt) for BP and albuminuria control [^1_1][^1_6]
- **Phosphorus additives** (processed foods) are worst; avoid completely [^1_8][^1_4]
- **Vitamin A = DO NOT supplement** (accumulates → toxicity) [^1_12][^1_13]
- **Water-soluble vitamins = supplement in dialysis** (lost during treatment) [^1_12]
- **Children = do NOT restrict protein** (growth impairment risk) [^1_1]
- **Elderly/frail = may need higher protein** (1.0–1.2 g/kg/day) to prevent sarcopenia [^1_8]
- **Ultra-processed foods = avoid** (high in sodium, phosphorus additives, sugars) [^1_2][^1_8]

## 13. Sample Diet Plan (CKD G4, Non-Dialysis, 60 kg Male)

| Nutrient | Target | Example Foods |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Energy** | 35 × 60 = **2100 kcal/day** | Rice, roti, vegetables, oils |
| **Protein** | 0.8 × 60 = **48 g/day** | 1 egg white (4 g), 100 g chicken (30 g), 1 cup dal (6 g), 200 mL milk (6 g) |
| **Sodium** | <2 g/day | No added salt, avoid processed foods |
| **Potassium** | Restrict if K⁺ >5.0 mEq/L | Avoid banana, orange, coconut water; use boiled vegetables |
| **Phosphorus** | 800–1000 mg/day | Limit dairy, egg yolk, nuts; avoid colas, processed meats |
| **Fluid** | Urine output + 500 mL | If urine 800 mL → fluid ~1300 mL/day |

## 14. Mnemonics for Quick Recall

**"CKD DIET"**

- **C**alories: 30–35 kcal/kg/day
- **K**⁺: Restrict if hyperkalemic
- **D**ialysis protein: 1.2 g/kg/day
- **I**norganic phosphorus: Avoid (additives)
- **E**nergy adequate: Prevent catabolism
- **T**able salt: <5 g/day (Na <2 g)

**"Vitamin AVOID in CKD"**

- **A** = Avoid (accumulate → toxicity)
- **D** = Deficiency common → supplement D
- **B-complex** = Lost in dialysis → supplement
- **C** = Low dose only (60–100 mg/day; avoid excess)

***

## 15. Algorithm: Nutritional Approach in CKD

```
CKD Diagnosis
     ↓
Assess: Stage, GFR, Albumin, K⁺, PO₄³⁻, BMI, Comorbidities
     ↓
Energy: 30–35 kcal/kg/day (all stages)
     ↓
Protein:
  ├─ G1–G2: 0.8–1.0 g/kg/day
  ├─ G3–G5 (non-dialysis): 0.6–0.8 g/kg/day
  ├─ Hemodialysis: 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day
  └─ Peritoneal Dialysis: 1.2–1.3 g/kg/day
     ↓
Sodium: <2 g/day (all stages)
     ↓
Potassium:
  ├─ Normal K⁺: Encourage plant foods
  └─ Hyperkalemia/G4–G5: Restrict high-K⁺ foods
     ↓
Phosphorus: 800–1000 mg/day + avoid additives
     ↓
Vitamins:
  ├─ D: Supplement if deficient
  ├─ B-complex + C: Dialysis patients
  └─ A: DO NOT supplement
     ↓
Monitor: Weight, albumin, K⁺, PO₄³⁻, bicarbonate, dietary intake
```
