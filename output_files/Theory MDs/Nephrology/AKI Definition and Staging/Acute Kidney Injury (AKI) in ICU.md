
## Acute Kidney Injury (AKI) in ICU

### Pathophysiology, Biomarkers, and Prevention

*(Exam‑styled, high‑yield notes)*

***

## 1. Definition \& Diagnostic Criteria (KDIGO)

AKI is defined by any of the following [^1_1][^1_2]:

- ↑ Serum creatinine (SCr) ≥ 0.3 mg/dL (≥ 26.5 µmol/L) within 48 hours
- ↑ SCr to ≥ 1.5 × baseline, known or presumed within prior 7 days
- Urine output < 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours

AKI staging (KDIGO):

- **Stage 1**: SCr 1.5–1.9× baseline or ↑ ≥0.3 mg/dL; UO <0.5 mL/kg/h × 6–12 h
- **Stage 2**: SCr 2.0–2.9× baseline; UO <0.5 mL/kg/h × ≥12 h
- **Stage 3**: SCr ≥3× baseline or ≥4.0 mg/dL or initiation of RRT; UO <0.3 mL/kg/h × ≥24 h or anuria × 12 h

***

## 2. Epidemiology \& Clinical Impact

- Incidence in ICU: 30–60%; severe AKI requiring RRT: 5–10% [^1_3][^1_1].
- Strongly associated with:
    - ↑ ICU and hospital mortality (dose–response with AKI stage)
    - Prolonged mechanical ventilation, LOS, cost
    - Higher risk of progression to CKD and ESRD [^1_3][^1_4].

***

## 3. Classification \& Etiology (ICU Focus)

### By anatomical/functional level

- **Prerenal**: hypoperfusion without intrinsic damage (reversible if corrected early)
- **Intrinsic (renal)**:
    - Acute tubular necrosis (ATN): ischemic, septic, nephrotoxic
    - Acute interstitial nephritis (AIN)
    - Glomerulonephritis / vasculitis
    - Vascular: thrombotic microangiopathy, malignant hypertension
- **Postrenal**: obstruction (less common primary cause in ICU, but important to exclude) [^1_5][^1_1].


### Common ICU precipitants

- Sepsis and septic shock (most common)
- Major surgery (especially cardiac, vascular)
- Hypovolemia, hemorrhage, burns
- Cardiorenal syndrome, right heart failure
- Nephrotoxins: aminoglycosides, vancomycin, amphotericin B, cisplatin, contrast, NSAIDs, ACEi/ARB in unstable patients
- Rhabdomyolysis, tumor lysis, hemolysis [^1_1][^1_3].

***

## 4. Pathophysiology (Mechanism‑Based, Exam Friendly)

Think in **three overlapping domains**: hemodynamic, microvascular/inflammatory, and tubular.

### 4.1 Hemodynamic \& Macro‑circulatory Mechanisms

- **Renal hypoperfusion** → ↓ renal blood flow (RBF), ↓ GFR:
    - Hypovolemia, hemorrhage, over‑diuresis
    - Low cardiac output (cardiogenic shock, severe LV/RV failure)
    - Systemic vasodilation (sepsis, anaphylaxis, liver failure)
- **Renal venous congestion**:
    - Right heart failure, high PEEP, abdominal compartment syndrome → ↑ renal venous pressure → ↓ net filtration pressure [^1_3][^1_5].

Key concept: GFR ≈ Kf × (Pgc − Pbs − πgc); anything that ↓ Pgc (glomerular capillary pressure) or ↑ Pbs/πgc reduces GFR.

### 4.2 Microcirculatory \& Endothelial Dysfunction (Especially in Sepsis)

- Sepsis does **not** simply cause global hypoperfusion; it causes:
    - Heterogeneous microvascular flow (some areas over‑perfused, others ischemic)
    - Endothelial activation → glycocalyx shedding, capillary leak, edema
    - Microthrombi formation (coagulopathy, DIC) → patchy ischemia
    - Impaired autoregulation → afferent/efferent arteriolar dysregulation [^1_3][^1_6].

Result: **functional shunting**, periglomerular maldistribution, and regional hypoxia despite “normal” systemic BP.

### 4.3 Tubular Injury \& Cell Death

- Ischemia/toxins → ATP depletion → failure of Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase → cell swelling, loss of polarity
- Cytoskeletal disruption → loss of tight junctions → back‑leak of filtrate
- Apoptosis \& necrosis of tubular epithelial cells (especially proximal tubule \& thick ascending limb)
- Cast formation (cellular, granular, pigmented) → tubular obstruction → ↑ intratubular pressure → ↓ GFR [^1_3][^1_7].


### 4.4 Inflammatory \& Immune Mechanisms

- DAMPs (damage‑associated molecular patterns) from injured tubules activate:
    - TLRs, NLRP3 inflammasome → IL‑1β, IL‑18, TNF‑α, IL‑6
    - Neutrophil and macrophage infiltration → ROS, proteases, further injury
- Systemic inflammation (sepsis, SIRS, trauma, pancreatitis) amplifies local renal inflammation [^1_3][^1_6].


### 4.5 Oxidative Stress \& Mitochondrial Dysfunction

- Ischemia–reperfusion → burst of ROS → lipid peroxidation, DNA damage
- Mitochondrial dysfunction → impaired oxidative phosphorylation → persistent energy failure
- Tubular cells in outer medulla are especially vulnerable (high metabolic demand, low O2 tension) [^1_3].


### 4.6 Organ Cross‑Talk (ICU‑Relevant)

- **Heart–kidney**: low CO, venous congestion, neurohormonal activation (RAAS, SNS)
- **Lung–kidney**: mechanical ventilation (high PEEP → ↓ venous return, renal congestion), ARDS inflammatory mediators
- **Liver–kidney**: cirrhosis → vasodilation, effective hypovolemia, toxins
- **Gut–kidney**: barrier dysfunction → endotoxemia, systemic inflammation [^1_3][^1_4].

## 5. Biomarkers of AKI

### 5.1 Traditional Functional Biomarkers

- **Serum creatinine (SCr)**
    - Reflects GFR, not injury
    - Lag time: rises only after significant GFR loss; affected by muscle mass, catabolism, drugs, fluid balance [^1_8][^1_7].
- **Urine output**
    - Early functional marker, but nonspecific; influenced by diuretics, fluid strategy, neurohormonal status [^1_5][^1_9].


### 5.2 Conceptual Classification of Biomarkers

1. **Susceptibility / Pre‑injury risk markers**
    - Indicate vulnerable kidneys before insult (e.g., CKD, genetic, baseline stress markers).
2. **Stress / Early injury markers**
    - Rise before SCr, reflect tubular stress/damage.
3. **Functional markers**
    - Reflect GFR changes (SCr, cystatin C, urine flow).
4. **Prognostic markers**
    - Predict severity, RRT need, recovery, mortality [^1_8][^1_10][^1_11][^1_9][^1_12].

### 5.3 Key Tubular Stress \& Injury Biomarkers (High‑Yield)

#### A. Cell‑Cycle Arrest Biomarkers: [TIMP‑2]·[IGFBP7]

- **Source**: Urinary; released by stressed tubular epithelial cells (TIMP‑2 distal, IGFBP7 proximal).
- **Mechanism**: Induce G1 cell‑cycle arrest to allow repair; high levels indicate severe stress.
- **Clinical use**:
    - FDA/CE‑cleared for predicting moderate–severe AKI (stage 2–3) within 12 h in ICU [^1_8][^1_12][^1_2].
    - Cutoff: ≥ 0.3 (ng/mL)²/1000 = high risk.
- **Exam point**: Best‑validated for *prediction* of AKI in high‑risk ICU/surgical patients.


#### B. NGAL (Neutrophil Gelatinase‑Associated Lipocalin)

- **Source**: Plasma \& urine; from injured tubular cells and neutrophils.
- **Kinetics**: Rises within 2–6 h after injury, earlier than SCr.
- **Uses**:
    - Early detection of AKI post‑cardiac surgery, sepsis, contrast, ICU [^1_9][^1_13].
    - Predicts severity, RRT need, mortality.
- **Limitations**: Elevated in systemic inflammation, infection, CKD, malignancy [^1_9][^1_12].


#### C. KIM‑1 (Kidney Injury Molecule‑1)

- **Source**: Urine; proximal tubule expression after injury.
- **Role**: Adhesion molecule aiding clearance of debris; marker of proximal tubular damage.
- **Use**: Predicts AKI, progression, and long‑term outcomes; elevated before SCr [^1_7][^1_11][^1_9].


#### D. IL‑18 (Interleukin‑18)

- **Source**: Urine; produced by proximal tubule in response to ischemic/toxic injury.
- **Use**: Early AKI detection; associated with severity and mortality [^1_7][^1_9][^1_13].


#### E. L‑FABP (Liver‑type Fatty Acid–Binding Protein)

- **Source**: Urine; proximal tubule; involved in fatty acid transport \& oxidative stress response.
- **Use**: Early AKI detection; predicts severity, especially in sepsis and cardiac surgery [^1_1][^1_9].


#### F. Cystatin C

- **Source**: Serum \& urine; low‑molecular‑weight protein, freely filtered, reabsorbed \& catabolized by tubules.
- **Advantages**: Less affected by muscle mass; earlier GFR change marker than SCr.
- **Limitations**: Affected by inflammation, steroids, thyroid disease [^1_10][^1_9][^1_13].


#### G. Other Emerging Markers (Know Names + Concepts)

- **DKK3 (Dickkopf‑3)**: Tubular stress glycoprotein; predicts AKI risk and progression [^1_11][^1_12].
- **β2‑microglobulin, α1‑microglobulin**: Proximal tubular dysfunction markers [^1_10][^1_13].
- **GST‑α/π (Glutathione S‑transferases)**: Segment‑specific tubular injury (proximal vs distal) [^1_10].
- **Clusterin, Calbindin**: Tubular injury/repair markers [^1_10].
- **Endothelial/inflammatory markers**: sTNFR‑1, sTREM‑1, sICAM‑1, sVCAM‑1, sPDL‑1 – associated with severe AKI in critical illness, reflect immune/endothelial activation [^1_6].


### 5.4 How to Use Biomarkers Clinically (Exam + Practice)

- **Who to test**: High‑risk ICU patients (sepsis, major surgery, shock, nephrotoxins, contrast).
- **When**: At risk exposure or ICU admission, then serially (e.g., 0, 6, 12, 24 h).
- **Interpretation**:
    - High stress biomarkers (e.g., [TIMP‑2]·[IGFBP7], NGAL) + clinical risk → “pre‑AKI” or subclinical AKI → intensify prevention.
    - Combine with SCr \& UO for staging and monitoring.
- **Guideline stance**:
    - KDIGO/ADQI: Biomarkers can refine risk, support early diagnosis, and target preventive bundles, but SCr + UO remain core for AKI definition [^1_8][^1_5][^1_1][^1_12].

## 6. Prevention of AKI in ICU (Core Exam Topic)

### 6.1 General Principles

- **Best treatment = prevention** [^1_1].
- Two pillars:

1. **Hemodynamic \& fluid optimization**
2. **Avoidance/minimization of nephrotoxins** [^1_3][^1_5][^1_1].


### 6.2 Hemodynamic Optimization

#### A. Maintain Adequate Perfusion Pressure

- Target **MAP ≥ 65 mmHg** in most; higher in chronic HTN or suspected renal hypoperfusion.
- Use vasopressors judiciously:
    - **Norepinephrine** is first‑line in septic shock; improves renal perfusion pressure without clear nephrotoxicity.
    - Avoid prolonged hypotension before starting vasopressors [^1_3][^1_5].


#### B. Fluid Management

- **Initial resuscitation**:
    - Correct hypovolemia rapidly with balanced crystalloids (e.g., Plasma‑Lyte, Ringer’s lactate) rather than high‑chloride fluids (↓ risk of hyperchloremic acidosis and renal vasoconstriction).
- **Avoid both**:
    - Persistent hypovolemia → ischemic AKI
    - Fluid overload → venous congestion, interstitial edema, ↑ intra‑abdominal pressure → worsening renal perfusion [^1_3][^1_5][^1_1].
- **Strategy**:
    - Early adequate resuscitation → then conservative/maintenance phase; consider de‑resuscitation (diuretics, RRT) if overloaded.


#### C. Optimize Cardiac Output \& Venous Congestion

- Treat LV/RV failure, tamponade, severe valvular disease.
- Minimize excessive PEEP if causing hemodynamic compromise.
- Consider echocardiography, dynamic preload indices to guide fluids/pressors.


### 6.3 Nephrotoxin Stewardship

- **Avoid or minimize**:
    - Aminoglycosides (or use once‑daily, short course, monitor levels)
    - Vancomycin + piperacillin‑tazobactam combo when alternatives exist
    - Amphotericin B (use liposomal formulations, monitor)
    - NSAIDs, ACEi/ARB in unstable/hypotensive patients
    - High‑dose or repeated contrast studies [^1_5][^1_1].
- **Strategies**:
    - Use therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) where available (vancomycin AUC, aminoglycoside levels).
    - Dose‑adjust all renally cleared drugs as GFR falls.
    - Review drug list daily in ICU rounds for nephrotoxic burden.


### 6.4 Sepsis \& Infection Control

- Early recognition, cultures, appropriate antibiotics, source control.
- Avoid prolonged hypotension before effective resuscitation.
- Sepsis bundles (fluids, pressors, antibiotics within 1 h) reduce AKI incidence indirectly [^1_3][^1_5].


### 6.5 Glucose \& Metabolic Control

- Avoid severe hyperglycemia and wide glucose variability; target 140–180 mg/dL in most ICU patients.
- Correct severe hypercalcemia, hyperuricemia (tumor lysis), and rhabdomyolysis early [^1_3][^1_5].


### 6.6 Contrast‑Associated AKI Prevention

- **Risk stratify**: CKD, diabetes, dehydration, heart failure, high contrast volume.
- **Measures**:
    - Use lowest feasible contrast dose; iso‑/low‑osmolar agents.
    - IV isotonic saline before and after procedure (e.g., 1 mL/kg/h × 6–12 h).
    - Avoid concurrent nephrotoxins around procedure.
    - Sodium bicarbonate or N‑acetylcysteine: evidence mixed; not routinely recommended by all guidelines but sometimes used in very high risk [^1_5].


### 6.7 Biomarker‑Guided Prevention (Emerging)

- Use [TIMP‑2]·[IGFBP7], NGAL, etc. to:
    - Identify high‑risk “pre‑AKI” patients.
    - Trigger “AKI bundles”: stricter hemodynamic targets, nephrotoxin review, closer UO/SCr monitoring, early nephrology input [^1_8][^1_11][^1_12].

## 7. Diagnostic Approach in ICU (Brief, for Context)

- **History**: Baseline renal function, drugs, contrast, sepsis, surgery, hypotension episodes.
- **Examination**: Volume status, perfusion, signs of obstruction, heart failure.
- **Labs**: SCr, BUN, electrolytes, acid–base, CBC, LFTs, CK (rhabdo), urinalysis.
- **Urine**: Output trend, sediment (muddy brown casts in ATN, eosinophils in AIN – though low specificity), FeNa, FeUrea (if on diuretics).
- **Imaging**: Renal US to exclude obstruction if suspicion; consider Doppler for renal perfusion in select cases [^1_5][^1_1].

***

## 8. Scoring \& Risk Stratification (Exam Mentions)

- **KDIGO AKI stage** (as above) – main tool.
- Risk models:
    - Clinical risk factors (age, CKD, DM, HTN, sepsis, surgery type, contrast, nephrotoxins).
    - Biomarker‑enhanced risk scores (research/advanced practice).
- Use for:
    - Anticipating AKI, planning monitoring, RRT preparedness [^1_3][^1_8][^1_1].

***

## 9. Complications \& Prognosis

### Complications

- Fluid overload, pulmonary edema
- Electrolyte disturbances (hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia)
- Metabolic acidosis
- Uremic complications (encephalopathy, pericarditis, bleeding)
- Drug toxicity from accumulation


### Prognosis

- Mortality rises with AKI stage and need for RRT.
- Survivors: high risk of:
    - CKD progression, ESRD
    - Cardiovascular events
    - Recurrent AKI [^1_3][^1_4].

***

## 10. Landmark Trials \& Guidelines (High‑Yield Names)

- **KDIGO 2012 AKI Guideline** (+ updates/controversies conferences 2019–2023): Foundation for definition, staging, management recommendations [^1_8][^1_11][^1_1].
- **ADQI Consensus on Biomarkers**: Recommendations for clinical/research use of AKI biomarkers [^1_8].
- **ISCCM Guidelines (India)**: Practical ICU‑focused guidance on AKI prevention, biomarkers, RRT indications [^1_1].
- Key trials (conceptual awareness):
    - Studies showing early hemodynamic optimization and nephrotoxin avoidance reduce AKI.
    - Biomarker trials (e.g., SAPPHIRE, OPAL) demonstrating [TIMP‑2]·[IGFBP7] prediction of severe AKI [^1_8][^1_2].

***

## 11. Key Clinical Pearls (Exam \& Bedside)

- **AKI is often subclinical early**: SCr lag → use risk assessment + biomarkers in high‑risk ICU patients.
- **Sepsis‑AKI ≠ simple hypoperfusion**: microcirculatory dysfunction, inflammation, and mitochondrial injury are central.
- **Fluids are double‑edged**: both under‑ and over‑resuscitation harm kidneys.
- **Nephrotoxin stewardship** is one of the most actionable prevention strategies in ICU.
- **[TIMP‑2]·[IGFBP7] and NGAL** are the most clinically relevant early biomarkers currently.
- **Prevention bundle mindset**: hemodynamic optimization + nephrotoxin review + tight infection control + glucose control = best “renal protective strategy”.
