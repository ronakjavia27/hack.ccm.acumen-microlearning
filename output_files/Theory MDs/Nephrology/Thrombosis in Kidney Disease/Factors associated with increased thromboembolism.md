# Factors associated with increased thromboembolism and bleeding risks in patients with aki or ckd

In patients with acute kidney injury (AKI) and chronic kidney disease (CKD), both thromboembolism and bleeding risks are increased due to complex, often opposing hemostatic abnormalities. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3] The net clinical phenotype depends on the stage of kidney disease, presence of albuminuria, dialysis exposure, comorbidities, and medication use. [^1_4][^1_5]

## Core Pathophysiologic Mechanisms

### Prothrombotic Drivers in AKI/CKD

- **Endothelial dysfunction**: Reduced nitric oxide bioavailability, increased endothelin-1, and glycocalyx damage promote a proadhesive, procoagulant surface. [^1_6][^1_1][^1_3]
- **Coagulation factor abnormalities**: Elevated fibrinogen, factor VIII, and von Willebrand factor (vWF) as part of chronic inflammation; increased plasminogen activator inhibitor (PAI-1) impairs fibrinolysis. [^1_3][^1_5]
- **Platelet activation**: Despite qualitative defects, platelets can be hyperreactive due to uremic toxins (e.g., indoxyl sulfate), shear stress, and inflammation. [^1_6][^1_7][^1_8]
- **Hemoconcentration and rheology**: Volume shifts, anemia correction, and hyperviscosity in some states increase thrombotic tendency. [^1_3]
- **Dialysis-related thrombogenicity**: Hemoincompatible membranes activate coagulation; central venous catheters provide nidus for thrombosis. [^1_1][^1_9]


### Hemorrhagic Drivers in AKI/CKD

- **Platelet dysfunction**: Impaired adhesion/aggregation from uremic toxins (guanidinosuccinic acid, phenols), decreased thromboxane A2, and increased NO/prostacyclin. [^1_6][^1_3]
- **Anemia**: Reduces platelet margination and vessel wall interaction, worsening bleeding. [^1_1][^1_3]
- **Accumulation of anticoagulant/antiplatelet drugs**: Reduced renal clearance increases exposure to DOACs, LMWH, and antiplatelets. [^1_1][^1_7]
- **Invasive procedures**: Frequent vascular access, dialysis cannulation, and catheter placements increase bleeding opportunities. [^1_6][^1_1]
- **Coagulation factor deficiencies (advanced CKD)**: Reduced levels of factors II, V, VII, and VIII in some patients contribute to bleeding. [^1_10]


## Clinical and Laboratory Risk Factors

### Factors Increasing Thromboembolism Risk

- **Declining eGFR**: Risk rises as eGFR falls; moderate (eGFR 30–60) and severe (eGFR <30) CKD confer ~2.5× and ~5.5× higher VTE risk, respectively. [^1_11][^1_8]
- **Albuminuria/proteinuria**: Higher proteinuria and lower serum albumin predict VTE; nephrotic syndrome is a strong risk state. [^1_1][^1_11][^1_12]
- **AKI episode**: AKI independently increases DVT/PE risk (up to ~1.5-fold for PE within 3 years in some cohorts). [^1_13][^1_11]
- **Comorbidities**: Atrial fibrillation (OR ~1.97), congestive heart failure (OR ~1.26), coronary artery disease (OR ~1.28), systemic lupus erythematosus (OR ~3.06), malignancy (OR ~5.8), recent surgery (OR ~14.0). [^1_9][^1_8]
- **Dialysis modality/access**: Hemodialysis > peritoneal dialysis for thrombosis risk; central venous catheters increase VTE. [^1_1][^1_9]
- **Demographics/genetics**: Female sex (OR ~1.30), older age, and prothrombotic genetic variants. [^1_9][^1_8]
- **Inflammation/infection**: Sepsis, active nephritis, and high inflammatory biomarkers. [^1_6][^1_11][^1_3]


### Factors Increasing Bleeding Risk

- **Advanced CKD (eGFR <45 mL/min/1.73 m²) with albuminuria**: Up to 3.5-fold increased major bleeding risk; eGFR <45 without albuminuria shows weaker association. [^1_4]
- **Uremic platelet dysfunction**: Worsens with rising BUN/creatinine; partially reversible with dialysis, desmopressin, or cryoprecipitate in acute settings. [^1_6][^1_3]
- **Concomitant antithrombotic therapy**: Oral anticoagulants increase AKI risk (aHR ~1.90); combination anticoagulant + antiplatelet markedly increases bleeding (HR ~3.96). [^1_7][^1_8]
- **Anemia**: Synergistically impairs primary hemostasis. [^1_1][^1_3]
- **Frequent invasive procedures**: Dialysis access, catheterizations, and biopsies. [^1_6][^1_1]
- **Drug accumulation**: DOACs, LMWH, and some antiplatelets accumulate in CKD, increasing bleeding, especially at eGFR <30. [^1_14][^1_7][^1_8]


## Risk Stratification Considerations

| Domain | Thromboembolism-Predominant | Bleeding-Predominant |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Kidney function** | Mild–moderate CKD (eGFR 30–60), early CKD with proteinuria | Advanced CKD (eGFR <30–45), especially with albuminuria [^1_4][^1_5] |
| **Proteinuria** | High-grade proteinuria/nephrotic syndrome [^1_11][^1_12] | Albuminuria + low eGFR strongly predicts bleeding [^1_1][^1_4] |
| **Comorbidities** | AF, CHF, CAD, SLE, malignancy, recent surgery [^1_9][^1_8] | Prior GI bleed, peptic ulcer disease, uncontrolled hypertension [^1_6] |
| **Medications** | No antithrombotics, prothrombotic states (e.g., ESA overcorrection) | Anticoagulants ± antiplatelets, NSAIDs, SSRIs [^1_7][^1_8] |
| **Dialysis** | HD with catheters, hemoincompatible membranes [^1_1][^1_9] | HD with frequent cannulation, heparin exposure, uremic bleeding [^1_6][^1_1] |
| **Labs** | High fibrinogen, factor VIII, vWF, PAI-1; low albumin [^1_3][^1_5] | Prolonged bleeding time, low platelet function, low factors II/V/VII/VIII [^1_10][^1_3] |

## Practical ICU/Bedside Pearls

- **AKI is an independent VTE risk**: Treat AKI patients as high-risk for DVT/PE even without traditional risk factors; maintain pharmacologic prophylaxis unless active bleeding or severe coagulopathy. [^1_13][^1_11]
- **Bleeding risk escalates with eGFR <45 + albuminuria**: Reassess antithrombotic necessity and dosing; consider gastroprotection and strict BP control. [^1_4]
- **Uremic bleeding is primarily platelet dysfunction**: Correct anemia, consider short-course desmopressin or cryoprecipitate for life-threatening bleeding; optimize dialysis clearance of uremic toxins. [^1_6][^1_3]
- **Anticoagulant selection in CKD**: DOACs and LMWH require dose adjustment or avoidance at low eGFR; warfarin remains usable but requires close INR monitoring due to narrow therapeutic window and drug–drug interactions. [^1_14][^1_7][^1_8]
- **Dual therapy (anticoagulant + antiplatelet) is high-risk**: Reserve for clear indications (e.g., recent ACS/stent) and minimize duration; bleeding risk is markedly amplified in CKD. [^1_7][^1_8]
