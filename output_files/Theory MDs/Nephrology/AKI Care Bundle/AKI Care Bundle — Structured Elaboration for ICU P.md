
## AKI Care Bundle — Structured Elaboration for ICU Practice

An AKI care bundle is a short, standardized set of evidence‑based actions triggered as soon as AKI is detected, designed to reduce variability in care and improve outcomes by focusing on early recognition, hemodynamic optimization, avoidance of nephrotoxins, and timely escalation. [^1_1][^1_2]

Below is a clinically oriented, ICU‑ready elaboration aligned with major programs (e.g., Think Kidneys/NICE, KDIGO‑influenced bundles) and critical care practice. [^1_3][^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]

***

## 1. Definition \& Rationale

- **AKI definition (KDIGO):**
Any of:
    - ↑ serum creatinine ≥26.5 µmol/L (0.3 mg/dL) within 48 h, or
    - ↑ serum creatinine ≥1.5× baseline within 7 days, or
    - Urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥6 h. [^1_6]
- **Bundle concept:**
A small set of high‑impact interventions delivered together, every time, to all eligible patients, to improve reliability of care and reduce preventable harm. [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Evidence summary:**
Systematic reviews suggest AKI bundles can improve adherence to basic standards (fluids, meds review, monitoring) and may reduce AKI progression, dialysis need, and mortality, though heterogeneity is high. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_7]

***

## 2. Trigger \& Staging (for bundle activation)

Activate the AKI care bundle when:

- Lab alert: eGFR drop, creatinine rise meeting KDIGO criteria, or “AKI stage 1–3” flag.
- Clinical trigger: oliguria/anuria, rising creatinine trend, or high‑risk context (sepsis, major surgery, contrast, nephrotoxins).

**KDIGO staging (simplified for bundle use):**


| Stage | Creatinine criterion | Urine output criterion |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | ≥1.5–1.9× baseline or ≥26.5 µmol/L rise | <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6–12 h |
| 2 | ≥2.0–2.9× baseline | <0.5 mL/kg/h for ≥12 h |
| 3 | ≥3× baseline or ≥354 µmol/L or RRT started | <0.3 mL/kg/h for ≥24 h or anuria ≥12 h |

[^1_6]

In ICU, urine output often triggers the bundle before creatinine changes.

***

## 3. Core Bundle Domains (FLUIDS‑TRiaD / ABCDE style)

Many hospitals use mnemonics like **FLUIDS** or **FLUIDS‑TRiaD**; the content is remarkably consistent across programs. [^1_8][^1_5]

### 3.1. Immediate bedside assessment (first 30–60 minutes)

- **ABCDE + NEWS2 / SOFA:**
    - Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure.
    - Document vitals, perfusion, capillary refill, mental status. [^1_3][^1_4][^1_6]
- **Sepsis screen:**
    - Temperature, WBC, HR, RR, lactate, source search.
    - If sepsis suspected → **start Sepsis Six / sepsis bundle concurrently** (cultures, lactate, broad‑spectrum antibiotics, fluids, oxygen, urine output monitoring). [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]
- **Volume status assessment:**
    - Hypovolemic vs euvolemic vs hypervolemic using:
        - History (losses, intake), exam (JVP, edema, lung crackles, skin turgor), dynamic indices if available (PLR, PPV, ultrasound).
    - Start **strict fluid balance chart** immediately; aim to measure all inputs/outputs. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]
- **Bladder/obstruction check:**
    - Palpate for distended bladder.
    - **Bladder scan** if retention suspected; catheterize if critical or retention confirmed; avoid routine catheters otherwise. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]


### 3.2. Initial investigations (same day, often within hours)

- **Bloods:**
    - U\&E, creatinine, bicarbonate/chloride, FBC, CRP, LFT, bone profile, glucose, lactate.
    - ABG/VBG if stage 3, severe acidosis, or K⁺ >6.0 mmol/L. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]
- **Urine:**
    - Urinalysis (dipstick) with documentation.
    - Send **MC\&S** if infection suspected.
    - Send **urine protein:creatinine ratio (PCR)** if protein ≥1+ or intrinsic renal disease suspected. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]
- **Imaging:**
    - **Renal tract ultrasound**:
        - Immediately if urosepsis with suspected obstruction or high suspicion of post‑renal AKI.
        - Within 24 h if no clear cause or no improvement. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]
    - Consider non‑contrast CT KUB if stone disease strongly suspected and USS inconclusive. [^1_5]
- **Renal screen (selective, not routine for all):**
    - Indicated if hematuria/proteinuria, suspected glomerulonephritis, vasculitis, myeloma, or systemic disease.
    - Typical panel: ANA, ANCA, anti‑GBM, complements (C3/C4), immunoglobulins, serum free light chains, myeloma screen; CK if rhabdomyolysis suspected. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]


### 3.3. Hemodynamic optimization \& fluid management

- **If hypovolemic / hypotensive:**
    - Give **crystalloid bolus** (e.g., 500 mL balanced crystalloid or 0.9% NaCl over <15 min), then reassess.
    - Repeat guided by clinical response and dynamic indices; avoid uncontrolled fluid loading. [^1_4][^1_5]
    - In ICU, consider early vasopressors if fluid‑refractory hypotension; target MAP ≥65 mmHg (higher if chronic HTN, individualized). [^1_2][^1_9]
- **If euvolemic:**
    - Maintain perfusion; avoid both dehydration and fluid overload.
    - Use conservative fluid strategy once shock resolved.
- **If hypervolemic / pulmonary edema:**
    - Fluid restriction (often 1–1.5 L/24 h, individualized).
    - Consider loop diuretics if not anuric and no contraindication; monitor response closely. [^1_5]
- **Ongoing:**
    - Hourly urine output in unstable patients; at least 4‑hourly in others.
    - Daily weight where feasible.
    - Reassess fluid status at least daily or with any change in condition. [^1_4][^1_8]


### 3.4. Medication review \& nephrotoxin avoidance (critical in ICU)

Perform **immediate and daily** medication review:

- **Stop / hold nephrotoxic or renally harmful drugs:**
    - NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, direct renin inhibitors.
    - Hold **metformin** if eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m² or lactate elevated / unstable. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]
    - Consider holding SGLT2 inhibitors in acute illness/AKI. [^1_6]
- **Dose‑adjust all renally cleared drugs:**
    - Antibiotics (especially vancomycin, aminoglycosides, beta‑lactams), anticoagulants, DOACs, insulin, opioids, gabapentinoids, etc.
    - Involve pharmacy early in ICU. [^1_4][^1_5]
- **Avoid or minimize:**
    - Iodinated contrast (postpone non‑essential scans; use lowest dose and prophylaxis if unavoidable).
    - Aminoglycosides unless absolutely necessary; use therapeutic drug monitoring.
    - High‑dose vancomycin without AUC monitoring.
- **Hyperglycemia management:**
    - Avoid sustained glucose >180 mg/dL; target 140–180 mg/dL in most ICU patients unless specific indications for tighter control. [^1_2][^1_9]


### 3.5. Treat underlying cause \& complications

- **Sepsis / infection:**
    - Source control, appropriate antibiotics, hemodynamic support as per sepsis guidelines. [^1_4][^1_5]
- **Obstruction:**
    - Prompt decompression (catheter, nephrostomy, stent) in post‑renal AKI; involve urology/interventional radiology urgently if pyonephrosis or septic obstruction. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]
- **Rhabdomyolysis:**
    - Aggressive isotonic fluids, monitor CK, K⁺, Ca²⁺; consider urine alkalinization in select cases (evidence limited).
- **Glomerulonephritis / vasculitis / interstitial nephritis:**
    - Early nephrology input; consider immunosuppression after appropriate workup.
- **Hyperkalemia:**
    - Follow hyperkalemia protocol: membrane stabilization (IV calcium), intracellular shift (insulin‑glucose, β‑agonists, bicarbonate in acidosis), elimination (diuretics, binders, dialysis). [^1_4][^1_5]
- **Metabolic acidosis:**
    - Treat underlying cause; consider bicarbonate in severe acidemia with hemodynamic compromise, recognizing limits.
- **Volume overload refractory to diuretics:**
    - Consider early CRRT rather than prolonged diuretic trials in unstable patients.


### 3.6. Monitoring \& escalation thresholds

- **Monitoring:**
    - Daily U\&E, creatinine, bicarbonate until AKI resolves or trajectory clear; more frequent in stage 2–3 or unstable patients.
    - Continuous or frequent urine output monitoring; strict fluid balance. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]
- **Mandatory senior review:**
    - Registrar/consultant review for all AKI stage 2–3 or any AKI with red flags. [^1_8][^1_6]
- **ICU / critical care referral:**
    - Hemodynamic instability, multiorgan failure, need for vasoactive support, severe acidosis, refractory hyperkalemia, pulmonary edema, or anticipated need for RRT. [^1_8][^1_6]

## 4. Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT) Triggers in AKI

In ICU, consider urgent nephrology/critical care discussion and likely RRT when any of the following are present despite optimal medical therapy:

- **Refractory hyperkalemia** (e.g., K⁺ >6.5 mmol/L with ECG changes or recurrent).
- **Severe metabolic acidosis** (pH <7.1–7.2) contributing to hemodynamic instability.
- **Fluid overload** causing hypoxemia / pulmonary edema unresponsive to diuretics.
- **Uremic complications:** encephalopathy, pericarditis, bleeding diathesis.
- **Severe azotemia** with clinical symptoms (e.g., very high BUN with encephalopathy).
- **Anuria/oliguria** with rapid creatinine rise and no improvement.

KDIGO recommends considering RRT based on **clinical context**, not arbitrary creatinine/BUN thresholds. [^1_6][^1_7]

In practice, earlier initiation in septic shock with uncontrolled acidosis/volume may be beneficial; use local protocols and multidisciplinary discussion.

## 5. Referral Pathways \& Follow‑up

### 5.1. When to refer to nephrology (adult, non‑transplant)

Refer urgently (same day / within 12 h) for:

- **AKI stage 3** (all patients, per many bundles). [^1_6]
- AKI with:
    - Suspected glomerulonephritis/vasculitis.
    - Rapidly worsening renal function despite appropriate management.
    - Need or likely need for acute dialysis.
    - Kidney transplant recipients with any AKI.
    - Unexplained AKI with significant proteinuria/hematuria.
- Consider referral for:
    - Recurrent AKI, or AKI superimposed on advanced CKD.
    - Complex electrolyte/acid–base disorders.


### 5.2. Urology / interventional referral

- Obstruction not relieved by catheter.
- Suspected pyonephrosis, obstructed infected kidney.
- Complex stone disease requiring urgent decompression. [^1_4][^1_5][^1_6]


### 5.3. Discharge \& post‑AKI follow‑up

For patients who had AKI (especially stage 2–3 or requiring dialysis):

- Ensure discharge summary includes:
    - AKI stage, cause, peak creatinine, dialysis requirement.
    - Medications stopped/modified and plan for re‑introduction (ACEi/ARB, diuretics, metformin, SGLT2, etc.).
    - Clear plan for **repeat bloods and urine tests** (e.g., at 1–2 weeks, then 3 months) and BP monitoring. [^1_4][^1_6]
- Communicate to GP:
    - Need for long‑term CKD surveillance (AKI is a strong risk factor for incident CKD and progression).
    - Medication cautions (“sick day rules”, avoidance of nephrotoxins).

## 6. ICU‑Specific Nuances \& Pitfalls

- **Don’t wait for creatinine:** In ICU, oliguria is often the earliest sign; trigger the bundle on urine output criteria. [^1_6]
- **Avoid “fluid creep”:** After initial resuscitation, switch to a conservative strategy; cumulative positive balance is independently associated with worse outcomes. [^1_8][^1_2]
- **Vasopressors early when needed:** Prolonged hypotension while chasing fluids worsens renal perfusion; start norepinephrine promptly if fluid‑refractory. [^1_2][^1_9]
- **Antibiotic dosing:** Under‑dosing in AKI/CRRT is common; use extended/continuous infusions and therapeutic drug monitoring where available.
- **Contrast decisions:** In unstable ICU patients, often defer elective contrast CT; when essential, use lowest dose, hydration, and avoid concurrent nephrotoxins.
- **CRRT timing:** Don’t delay RRT in unstable patients with uncontrolled acidosis/volume; conversely, avoid “pre‑emptive” dialysis in stable patients without clear indications.

## 7. Example AKI Care Bundle Checklist (ICU‑adapted)

You can implement this as a paper/electronic checklist triggered by AKI alert:

**A. Immediate (within 1 hour)**

1. ABCDE + NEWS2/SOFA; document perfusion.
2. Sepsis screen; start sepsis bundle if indicated.
3. Assess volume status (hypo/eu/hyper); start strict fluid balance.
4. Bladder palpation ± scan; catheterize only if critical/retention.
5. Bloods: U\&E, creatinine, bicarb/chloride, FBC, CRP, LFT, bone profile, glucose, lactate; ABG/VBG if severe.
6. Urinalysis; send MC\&S if infection; send PCR if protein ≥1+.
7. Hold NSAIDs, ACEi/ARB, metformin (if eGFR <30 or lactate high), consider holding SGLT2.
8. Review all drug doses for renal adjustment; involve pharmacy.

**B. Early (within 6–24 hours)**
9. Renal tract USS:

- Immediately if obstructed/urosepsis suspected.
- Within 24 h if cause unclear or no improvement.

10. Targeted renal screen if hematuria/proteinuria or systemic disease suspected.
11. Optimize hemodynamics:
    - Fluid boluses if hypovolemic; vasopressors if fluid‑refractory.
    - Diuretics/fluid restriction if overloaded.
12. Treat complications: hyperkalemia, acidosis, pulmonary edema.
13. Senior review (registrar/consultant); consider ICU/nephrology/urology referral based on criteria.

**C. Ongoing (daily until resolution)**
14. Daily U\&E, creatinine, bicarbonate; more often if unstable.
15. Daily medication review; re‑check doses with changing renal function.
16. Reassess fluid status, urine output trends, and net balance.
17. Plan for discharge follow‑up: labs, medication plan, CKD surveillance.

Adapt thresholds and naming to local policy, but keep the core logic intact.

***

## 8. Key Guideline \& Evidence Anchors

- **KDIGO AKI guidelines:** Foundation for definition, staging, and many management principles (fluids, nephrotoxins, RRT indications). [^1_2][^1_7]
- **NICE AKI guidance (CG50 / related):** Emphasizes early recognition, fluid management, medication review, and structured care pathways. [^1_3][^1_5]
- **Think Kidneys (NHS) AKI care bundle:** Practical hospital bundle with ABCDE, sepsis, fluids, meds review, imaging, and referral criteria. [^1_3][^1_4][^1_6]
- **Meta‑analyses on AKI bundles:** Show improved process measures and potential outcome benefits, supporting bundle adoption in high‑risk settings (including post‑cardiac surgery and general wards/ICU). [^1_1][^1_2][^1_7]
