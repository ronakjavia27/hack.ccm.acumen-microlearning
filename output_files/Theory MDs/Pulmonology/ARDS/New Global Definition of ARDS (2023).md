
## New Global Definition of ARDS (2023)

The 2023 Global Definition of ARDS is an **extension** of the Berlin definition, designed to make ARDS diagnosis more inclusive and applicable across varying resource settings. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3] It introduces **three distinct categories**: non-intubated ARDS, intubated ARDS, and a modified definition for resource-limited settings. [^1_4][^1_5]

***

## 1. Conceptual Model

**ARDS is an acute, diffuse, inflammatory lung injury** precipitated by a predisposing risk factor (e.g., pneumonia, non-pulmonary infection, trauma, transfusion, burn, aspiration, or shock). [^1_6][^1_7]

- **Pathophysiology**: Increased pulmonary vascular and epithelial permeability → lung edema + gravity-dependent atelectasis → loss of aerated lung tissue. [^1_6]
- **Clinical hallmarks**: Arterial hypoxemia, diffuse radiographic opacities, increased shunting, increased alveolar dead space, decreased lung compliance. [^1_6]

***

## 2. Diagnostic Criteria (All Categories)

| Criterion | Global Definition (2023) | Berlin Definition (2012) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Timing** | Acute onset/worsening within **1 week** of risk factor or new/worsening respiratory symptoms | Same |
| **Risk Factor \& Edema Origin** | Precipitated by acute risk factor; pulmonary edema **not exclusively cardiogenic**; can coexist with cardiac failure/fluid overload if ARDS risk factor present | Same |
| **Chest Imaging** | Bilateral opacities on **CXR, CT, or lung ultrasound** (B-lines/consolidations not explained by effusions, atelectasis, nodules) | CXR or CT only |
| **Oxygenation** | **PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 300** OR **SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 315** (if SpO₂ ≤ 97%) | PaO₂/FiO₂ only |
| **Exclusion** | Not fully explained by cardiac failure or fluid overload; objective assessment (e.g., echo) recommended if no risk factor present | Same |

[^1_2][^1_3][^1_8][^1_7][^1_9]

***

## 3. Three ARDS Categories

### **Category 1: Non-Intubated ARDS**

- **Support**: High-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO) ≥ 30 L/min **OR** NIV/CPAP with ≥ 5 cm H₂O PEEP
- **Oxygenation**: PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 300 **OR** SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 315 (if SpO₂ ≤ 97%)
- **Severity** (same thresholds as intubated ARDS, see below)

[^1_2][^1_5][^1_10]

### **Category 2: Intubated ARDS**

- **Support**: Invasive mechanical ventilation
- **Oxygenation**: PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 300 **OR** SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 315 (if SpO₂ ≤ 97%) with PEEP/CPAP ≥ 5 cm H₂O
- **Severity**:
    - **Mild**: 200 < PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 300 **OR** 235 < SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 315
    - **Moderate**: 100 < PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 200 **OR** 148 < SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 235
    - **Severe**: PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 100 **OR** SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 148

[^1_2][^1_5][^1_7]

### **Category 3: Resource-Limited Settings (Modified Definition)**

- **No requirement** for:
    - PaO₂/FiO₂ measurement (can use SpO₂/FiO₂ alone)
    - Minimum oxygen flow rate
    - PEEP or specific respiratory support device
- **Diagnosis**: SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 315 (if SpO₂ ≤ 97%) + bilateral opacities + risk factor + timing

[^1_1][^1_2][^1_3][^1_7]

***

## 4. Critical Appraisal of the New Definition

### **Strengths**

- **Inclusivity**: Captures non-intubated patients on HFNO/NIV, expanding recognition beyond ICU. [^1_5][^1_11]
- **Resource flexibility**: Allows diagnosis in low-resource settings without ABG or PEEP requirements. [^1_1][^1_12]
- **Ultrasound integration**: Validates lung US (B-lines, consolidations) as imaging modality, useful at bedside and in resource-limited areas. [^1_3][^1_8][^1_7]
- **SpO₂/FiO₂ ratio**: Enables diagnosis without arterial blood gas when SpO₂ ≤ 97%; validated correlation with PaO₂/FiO₂. [^1_2][^1_10][^1_11]
- **Continuity**: Retains core Berlin criteria (timing, imaging, exclusion of cardiogenic edema), minimizing confusion. [^1_3][^1_8]


### **Limitations \& Controversies**

- **Not a new definition**: Technically an **extension** of Berlin, not a complete revision; may cause semantic confusion. [^1_8][^1_12]
- **SpO₂ ceiling**: SpO₂/FiO₂ only valid when SpO₂ ≤ 97%; accuracy decreases at higher saturations. [^1_2][^1_5]
- **Ultrasound operator dependency**: Requires training; inter-observer variability remains a concern. [^1_7]
- **No PEEP requirement in resource-limited settings**: May reduce specificity; risk of overdiagnosis in mild hypoxemia. [^1_1][^1_13]
- **Non-intubated ARDS category**: Lacks outcome data compared to intubated ARDS; optimal management strategies unclear. [^1_4][^1_13]
- **Validation pending**: Limited external validation in diverse populations; most data from high-income settings. [^1_12][^1_13]


### **Clinical Implications**

- **Earlier recognition**: Allows diagnosis on general wards and in emergency departments. [^1_5]
- **Trial enrollment**: May expand eligibility for ARDS trials, but introduces heterogeneity. [^1_13]
- **Resource-limited settings**: Makes ARDS diagnosis feasible where ABG and CXR are unavailable. [^1_1][^1_12]

## 5. Phenotypes of ARDS

ARDS is a **heterogeneous syndrome**. Phenotyping aims to identify subgroups with distinct pathobiology, outcomes, and treatment responses. [^1_14][^1_15]

***

### **A. Two Major Biological Phenotypes (Hyper- vs. Hypoinflammatory)**

Identified using **latent class analysis (LCA)** of clinical + biomarker data from multiple ARDS RCTs (ARMA, ALVEOLI, ALPS, SAILS, HARP-2). [^1_16][^1_17][^1_18][^1_19][^1_20]


| Feature | **Hypoinflammatory (Phenotype 1)** | **Hyperinflammatory (Phenotype 2)** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Frequency** | ~70% of ARDS patients | ~30% of ARDS patients |
| **Inflammatory biomarkers** | Low IL-6, IL-8, sTNFR-1 | **High** IL-6, IL-8, sTNFR-1 |
| **Protein C** | Normal/high | **Low** |
| **Bicarbonate** | Normal | **Low** (metabolic acidosis) |
| **Vasopressor use** | Less frequent | **More frequent** |
| **Sepsis** | Less common | **More common** (especially non-pulmonary) |
| **Organ failures** | Fewer | **More** (extra-pulmonary) |
| **Mortality** | **Lower** | **Higher** |
| **Ventilator-free days** | More | Fewer |
| **Chronic liver disease** | Less common | **More common** |
| **COPD** | **More common** | Less common |

[^1_16][^1_17][^1_21][^1_22][^1_23][^1_24][^1_15]

#### **Simplified 3-Biomarker Model** (for clinical use)

- **IL-8, sTNFR-1, bicarbonate** reliably classify phenotypes. [^1_24][^1_25]
- **4-variable model**: IL-8 + Protein C + bicarbonate + vasopressor use. [^1_25]

***

### **B. Treatment Response by Phenotype (Key Trials)**

| Trial | Intervention | **Hypoinflammatory** | **Hyperinflammatory** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **ALVEOLI** (PEEP) | Higher vs. lower PEEP | No mortality benefit | **Higher PEEP → ↓ mortality**, ↑ ventilator-free days |
| **HARP-2** (Simvastatin) | Simvastatin vs. placebo | No benefit | **Simvastatin → ↓ mortality** (signal) |
| **ARMA** (Tidal Volume) | Low vs. traditional VT | Benefit from low VT | **Greater benefit** from low VT |
| **Fluids** (FACTT) | Conservative vs. liberal | No clear difference | **Conservative fluids → better outcomes** |

[^1_21][^1_20][^1_15][^1_25]

***

### **C. Longitudinal Phenotypes (Newer Data)**

A 2022 multi-database study identified **three longitudinal phenotypes** using daily data over 4 days of ventilation: [^1_26]


| Class | Characteristics | % of Cohort | 28-day Mortality |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Class 1** | Fewer abnormalities, less organ dysfunction | 66% | 30.5% |
| **Class 2** | Pulmonary mechanical dysfunction, poorly aerated lung | 17% | 47.1% |
| **Class 3** | Extra-pulmonary dysfunction (multi-organ failure) | 17% | 56.3% |

[^1_26]

***

### **D. Radiological/Physiological Phenotypes**

| Phenotype | Features | Clinical Relevance |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Recruitable vs. Non-recruitable** | Based on CT/PEEP response; recruitable has more atelectasis, benefits from higher PEEP | Guides PEEP titration |
| **Focal vs. Diffuse** (CT) | Focal: gravity-dependent; Diffuse: homogeneous | Focal may benefit from prone positioning |
| **Direct vs. Indirect Lung Injury** | Direct (pneumonia, aspiration): higher epithelial injury (sRAGE, SP-D); Indirect (sepsis, pancreatitis): higher endothelial injury (Ang-2, vWF) | May influence biomarker profiles and targeted therapies |

[^1_27][^1_25]

***

## 6. Clinical Pearls (Exam-Oriented)

### **New Global Definition (2023)**

- **Three categories**: Non-intubated, Intubated, Resource-limited.
- **SpO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 315** (if SpO₂ ≤ 97%) can replace PaO₂/FiO₂.
- **Lung ultrasound** (B-lines, consolidations) is now an accepted imaging modality.
- **No PEEP/flow requirement** in resource-limited settings.


### **Phenotypes**

- **Two main phenotypes**: Hypoinflammatory (70%, better outcomes) vs. Hyperinflammatory (30%, worse outcomes).
- **Key biomarkers**: IL-6, IL-8, sTNFR-1 (↑ in hyperinflammatory); Protein C, bicarbonate (↓ in hyperinflammatory).
- **Hyperinflammatory**: More sepsis, shock, metabolic acidosis, organ failures, higher mortality.
- **Treatment implications**:
    - Hyperinflammatory → benefits from **higher PEEP**, **conservative fluids**, possibly **simvastatin**.
    - Hypoinflammatory → less clear benefit from targeted interventions.


### **Exam MCQ Traps**

- SpO₂/FiO₂ is **only valid when SpO₂ ≤ 97%**.
- Ultrasound diagnosis requires **bilateral B-lines/consolidations**, not unilateral.
- Hyperinflammatory phenotype = **higher mortality**, not lower.
- Simvastatin benefit seen **only in hyperinflammatory** (HARP-2 trial).

***

## 7. Landmark Trials \& References

| Trial | Year | Key Finding Related to Phenotypes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **ARMA** | 2000 | Low VT ventilation reduces mortality (greater benefit in hyperinflammatory) |
| **ALVEOLI** | 2004 | Higher PEEP benefits hyperinflammatory phenotype |
| **FACTT** | 2006 | Conservative fluid strategy; hyperinflammatory may benefit more |
| **HARP-2** | 2014 | Simvastatin showed mortality reduction signal in hyperinflammatory |
| **SAILS** | 2016 | Statin (rosuvastatin) did not show overall benefit; phenotype analysis pending |
