
## Proning on ECMO and Awake ECMO

---

## 1. PRONING ON ECMO

### Definition

Prone positioning in patients receiving extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) refers to placing the patient in a face-down position to improve oxygenation and lung recruitment while on ECMO support. [^1_1][^1_2]

### Rationale \& Physiology

- **Improves V/Q matching**: Reduces ventilation-perfusion mismatch by redistributing blood flow to better-ventilated lung zones [^1_3]
- **Homogenizes lung stress**: Creates more even distribution of gas-tissue ratios along the dependent–nondependent axis [^1_3]
- **Reduces shunt**: Particularly beneficial when posterior lung consolidation exists with anterior lung fields still open [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Facilitates pulmonary toilet**: Improves drainage of secretions from posterior lung segments [^1_2]


### Indications for Proning on ECMO [^1_2][^1_4]

1. Refractory hypoxia despite ECMO support
2. Posterior lung consolidation with anterior lung fields open
3. Failure to wean from VV-ECMO
4. To facilitate pulmonary hygiene and secretion clearance
5. Moderate-to-severe ARDS on ECMO (PaO₂/FiO₂ < 150)

### Contraindications [^1_5][^1_4]

**Absolute:**

- Unstable ECMO cannulae or recent cannulation (<24 hours)
- Hemodynamic instability
- Raised intracranial pressure
- Unstable spine or pelvic fractures
- Recent sternotomy or anterior chest surgery

**Relative:**

- Pregnancy
- Massive obesity (BMI > 40)
- Open abdomen
- Facial trauma


### Practical Technique

**Team Requirements:** [^1_1][^1_5]

- **Minimum 5 staff** for standard proning
- **6-8 staff for ECMO patients**:
    - 1 person: Head/airway management (also manages jugular ECMO cannula if present)
    - 1 person: ECMO circuit monitoring (femoral lines)
    - 4-6 persons: Patient turning and positioning

**Step-by-Step Approach:** [^1_1][^1_5][^1_4]

1. **Pre-procedure preparation**:
    - Optimize volume status
    - Check all line lengths (ECMO, ventilator, arterial, venous)
    - Secure all cannulae and lines
    - Administer adequate sedation ± neuromuscular blockade
2. **Turning technique**:
    - Use "double sheet rolling" technique (patient wrapped between two sheets)
    - Turn toward the side with **reinjection line on top** (prioritizes VV-ECMO flow)
    - Coordinate turning as a team with one leader
3. **Position maintenance**:
    - Head in neutral, centered position (avoid lateral rotation)
    - Arms parallel to trunk or in "swimming crawl" position
    - Abdomen unsupported (use chest/pelvic bolsters)
    - Pillows under ankles and chest to prevent femoral cannula compression
4. **Duration**:
    - Maintain for **12-16 hours/day** (per ARDS guidelines) [^1_6]
    - Can continue until oxygenation improves in supine position

### Monitoring During Proning [^1_5][^1_4]

- **ECMO parameters**: Blood flow, pre-/post-oxygenator pressures, delta pressure
- **Cannula position**: Check for kinking, dislodgment, or flow reduction
- **Oxygenation**: Continuous SpO₂, serial ABG
- **Hemodynamics**: Continuous BP, heart rate
- **Skin protection**: Pressure areas, face, eyes, breasts, genitals


### Complications [^1_5][^1_4]

- **Most dangerous**: ECMO cannula dislodgment, sudden flow decrease
- Airway dislodgment
- Hemodynamic instability
- Pressure injuries
- Facial/airway edema
- Line/tube dislodgment


### When to Stop Proning [^1_4][^1_3]

- PaO₂/FiO₂ ≥ 150 with PEEP ≤ 10 cm H₂O and FiO₂ ≤ 0.6 (measured 4 hours after returning to supine)
- PaO₂/FiO₂ deterioration by >20% compared to supine position
- Life-threatening complication during proning
- Two unsuccessful proning attempts


### Key Evidence

- **ELSO guidelines**: Recommend consideration of proning on ECMO for posterior consolidation with anterior lung fields open [^1_1][^1_2]
- **Safety**: Multiple observational studies and meta-analyses show proning on ECMO is safe when performed by experienced teams [^1_6][^1_7]
- **Oxygenation benefit**: Improves PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio, benefit maintained after resupination [^1_6]
- **No mortality benefit proven**: Evidence doesn't strongly favor routine proning for all ECMO patients, but may benefit specific populations [^1_7]


### Exam Pearls

- **Remember**: Proning is NOT contraindicated on ECMO [^1_1]
- **Priority**: Protect ECMO cannulae during turning—dedicate specific staff to line management
- **Direction**: Turn with reinjection line on top to prevent flow obstruction
- **Staff**: Need MORE personnel than standard proning (minimum 6-8 for ECMO)
- **Check**: Always verify ECMO flow and pressures immediately after turning

## 2. AWAKE ECMO

### Definition

Awake ECMO refers to venovenous (VV) ECMO support in spontaneously breathing, non-intubated patients who remain conscious throughout the ECMO run. [^1_8][^1_9]

### Rationale

- **Avoids ventilator-induced lung injury (VILI)**: No positive pressure ventilation
- **Preserves respiratory muscle function**: Prevents diaphragmatic atrophy
- **Enables early mobilization**: Patients can sit, stand, walk with ECMO
- **Reduces sedation complications**: No delirium, shorter ICU stay
- **Bridge strategy**: To recovery, transplant, or decision [^1_10][^1_11]


### Indications [^1_10][^1_9][^1_11]

**Respiratory:**

- Severe hypoxemic respiratory failure (PaO₂/FiO₂ < 100) despite optimal medical therapy
- Severe hypercapnic respiratory failure with pH < 7.25
- Bridge to lung transplant in end-stage lung disease
- Failed conventional mechanical ventilation with preserved respiratory drive

**Clinical scenarios:**

- Reversible conditions (e.g., acute exacerbation of ILD, pneumonia)
- Patient too unstable for transplant but needs bridge
- Contraindication to intubation (patient refusal, poor prognosis with MV)


### Patient Selection Criteria [^1_10][^1_9]

**Ideal candidate:**

- Alert, cooperative, and able to follow commands
- Hemodynamically stable (no or low-dose vasopressors)
- Adequate respiratory drive without excessive work of breathing
- Age < 65 years (typically)
- Reversible lung pathology or bridge to transplant
- Strong social support and motivation

**Contraindications:**

- Hemodynamic instability requiring high-dose vasopressors
- Altered mental status or inability to protect airway
- Excessive work of breathing (RR > 35-40, accessory muscle use)
- Severe acidosis (pH < 7.15)
- Anticipated need for procedures requiring intubation
- Poor social support or inability to cooperate


### Cannulation Strategy [^1_10][^1_12]

- **Configuration**: VV-ECMO (femoro-jugular or bi-femoral)
- **Cannula size**: Typically 21-25 Fr drainage, 19-21 Fr return
- **Ultrasound guidance**: Mandatory for all vascular access
- **Chest X-ray**: Confirm cannula position before initiating flow
- **Echocardiography**: Assess optimal positioning and flow


### Ventilatory Management [^1_9][^1_11]

- **No intubation**: Patient breathes spontaneously
- **Oxygen supplementation**: Via nasal cannula, high-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO), or non-rebreather mask
- **Targets**:
    - SpO₂: 88-92% (avoid hyperoxia)
    - PaO₂: > 70 mmHg (prevent hypoxemic brain injury)
    - PaO₂: < 300 mmHg (avoid reperfusion injury)
    - PaCO₂: Avoid rapid change in first 24 hours
    - pH: > 7.25


### ECMO Management [^1_10][^1_9]

**Initial settings:**

- **Blood flow**: Start 2-3 L/min, titrate to 3-4 L/min (60-80 mL/kg/min)
- **Sweep gas**: 1:1 to 2:1 ratio with blood flow
- **FiO₂ on ECMO**: 100% initially, titrate to target SpO₂

**Monitoring:**

- **Circuit**: Pre-membrane pressure, delta pressure (oxygenator function)
- **Anticoagulation**: ACT 180-220 seconds (or aPTT 50-70 seconds)
- **ABG**: Every 6-8 hours initially, then daily
- **CBC**: Daily (monitor for hemolysis, bleeding)
- **Coagulation**: ACT every 4-6 hours


### Mobilization Protocol [^1_8][^1_9]

- **Day 1**: Bed exercises, sitting on edge of bed
- **Day 2-3**: Standing, ambulation with ECMO cart
- **Progression**: Increase distance and frequency as tolerated
- **Monitoring during mobilization**:
    - Continuous SpO₂, heart rate, BP
    - ECMO flow and pressures
    - Work of breathing assessment
    - Cannula site inspection


### Monitoring Parameters [^1_10][^1_9]

**Clinical:**

- Work of breathing (accessory muscle use, respiratory rate)
- Mental status and cooperation
- Fluid balance (strict intake/output)

**Respiratory:**

- SpO₂: Continuous (target 88-92%)
- End-tidal CO₂: Trending for weaning guidance
- Respiratory rate: Early marker of distress
- ABG: Every 6-8 hours initially

**ECMO circuit:**

- Blood flow rate
- Pre-/post-oxygenator pressures
- Delta pressure (trending for thrombosis)
- Oxygenator function

**Daily assessments:**

- Echocardiography (cardiac function)
- Renal function (creatinine, electrolytes)
- Liver function
- Coagulation profile


### Complications [^1_8][^1_11]

- **Cannula-related**: Dislodgment, bleeding, infection
- **Circuit**: Thrombosis, hemolysis, oxygenator failure
- **Respiratory**: Worsening work of breathing, need for intubation
- **Neurological**: Hypoxic brain injury if SpO₂ drops
- **Bleeding**: Anticoagulation-related
- **Infection**: Cannula site, bloodstream


### When to Intubate [^1_10][^1_9]

- Excessive work of breathing despite maximal support
- Respiratory rate > 35-40/min persistently
- Worsening acidosis (pH < 7.20)
- Altered mental status or inability to protect airway
- Hemodynamic instability
- Need for procedures requiring intubation
- Failure to maintain SpO₂ > 88% despite adequate ECMO flow


### Weaning from Awake ECMO [^1_9][^1_11]

- **Criteria**:
    - Improvement in underlying lung pathology
    - SpO₂ > 92% on minimal oxygen supplementation
    - Respiratory rate < 25/min
    - Normal work of breathing
    - ABG: PaO₂/FiO₂ > 200, PaCO₂ < 50 mmHg
- **Process**:
    - Gradually reduce ECMO blood flow
    - Reduce sweep gas to assess CO₂ clearance
    - Trial off ECMO for 4-6 hours with close monitoring
    - ABG confirmation before decannulation


### Advantages vs. Conventional ECMO [^1_8][^1_9]

| Feature | Awake ECMO | Conventional ECMO |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Intubation | No | Yes |
| Sedation | Minimal/none | Required |
| Mobilization | Early (day 1-2) | Delayed |
| VILI risk | Absent | Present |
| Diaphragm function | Preserved | Atrophy common |
| ICU length of stay | Shorter | Longer |
| Delirium | Less common | Common |
| Patient cooperation | Essential | Not required |

### Key Evidence

- **Safety**: Awake ECMO is safe in carefully selected patients at high-volume centers [^1_9]
- **Mobilization**: Early mobilization on ECMO is feasible and safe [^1_8]
- **Outcomes**: May reduce ICU stay, delirium, and ventilator complications [^1_8][^1_11]
- **Bridge to transplant**: Successful strategy for end-stage lung disease [^1_10][^1_11]


### Exam Pearls

- **Rule of 100s**: Consider awake ECMO when PaO₂ < 100 mmHg on FiO₂ > 0.8, but only if SpO₂ > 88% without excessive work of breathing [^1_10]
- **AWAKE mnemonic** for patient selection: [^1_10]
    - **A**lert and cooperative
    - **W**ork of breathing manageable
    - **A**ge appropriate (< 65 years)
    - **K**eep hemodynamically stable
    - **E**xpect reversible disease or bridge to transplant
- **Flow-first approach**: Optimize ECMO flow before adjusting ventilator settings or sweep gas [^1_10]
- **Target SpO₂**: 88-92% (avoid both hypoxia AND hyperoxia) [^1_10][^1_9]
- **ACT target**: 180-220 seconds for anticoagulation [^1_10]

## Quick Comparison Table

| Aspect | Proning on ECMO | Awake ECMO |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Purpose** | Improve oxygenation, lung recruitment | Avoid intubation, enable mobilization |
| **Patient state** | Sedated, intubated, ventilated | Awake, spontaneously breathing |
| **Indications** | Posterior consolidation, refractory hypoxia on ECMO | Severe respiratory failure, bridge to transplant |
| **Staff needed** | 6-8 personnel | Standard ECMO team (4-6) |
| **Key risk** | Cannula dislodgment, flow reduction | Intubation requirement, work of breathing |
| **Duration** | 12-16 hours/day | Days to weeks |
| **Contraindications** | Unstable cannulae, hemodynamic instability | Altered mental status, excessive work of breathing |
