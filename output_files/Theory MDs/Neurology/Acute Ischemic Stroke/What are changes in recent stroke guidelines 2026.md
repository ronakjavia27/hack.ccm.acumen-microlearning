

The **2026 AHA/ASA Guideline for the Early Management of Patients with Acute Ischemic Stroke** (published January 26, 2026, in *Stroke*) replaces the 2018 AIS guideline and its 2019 update, introducing practice-defining changes across thrombolysis, endovascular therapy, systems of care, supportive management, and—first time—pediatric stroke [^1_1][^1_2]. Below is a domain-by-domain elaboration of what changed and why.

***

## 1. Prehospital Care and Systems of Stroke Care

### Mobile Stroke Units (MSUs)

- **2026 (COR 1, LOE A):** MSUs are now formally recommended over conventional EMS for thrombolytic-eligible patients in areas where MSU programs exist, based on evidence that they reduce door-to-needle and onset-to-treatment times [^1_3][^1_4].
- **2018:** MSUs were mentioned but not formally endorsed with a strong recommendation.


### EMS Destination Protocols

- **2026:** The default is now direct transport to the closest EVT-capable hospital. In well-coordinated systems with rapid interhospital transfer processes, bypass to a distant thrombectomy-capable stroke center (TSC) does not improve 3-month outcomes compared to transport to a local stroke center—but this applies only when systems are mature [^1_3][^1_4].
- **2018:** Previous guidelines recommended transport to the nearest thrombolytic-capable facility regardless of EVT capability.


### Field Triage Tools

- Standardized prehospital stroke severity scales (LAMS, CPSS, sNIHSS) are endorsed to guide destination selection, particularly for identifying suspected large vessel occlusion (LVO) [^1_4].

***

## 2. Intravenous Thrombolysis (IVT)

This domain saw the most fundamental pharmacologic shift in the guideline.


| Feature | 2018/2019 Guideline | 2026 Guideline |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Preferred thrombolytic** | Alteplase 0.9 mg/kg (COR 1, LOE A) was the sole standard | Tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg (max 25 mg) now COR 1, LOE A — equivalent to alteplase; single IV bolus preferred for workflow [^1_3][^1_4][^1_1] |
| **Tenecteplase 0.4 mg/kg** | Not addressed | COR 3: No Benefit — does not improve outcomes and may cause harm [^1_4][^1_1] |
| **Time window for standard IVT** | 0–4.5 hours | 0–4.5 hours (unchanged); within this window, IVT recommended regardless of NIHSS score for disabling deficits, without advanced imaging [^1_3][^1_4] |
| **Extended window IVT** | Limited guidance | COR 2a: IVT for selected patients with wake-up stroke or 4.5–9 hours from onset, using advanced imaging (DWI-FLAIR mismatch or perfusion-based mismatch) — EXTEND, ECASS-4, WAKE-UP trials [^1_3][^1_4] |
| **Non-disabling minor deficits** | IVT sometimes considered | COR 3: No Benefit — IVT not recommended for non-disabling stroke (e.g., isolated sensory syndrome); DAPT preferred instead [^1_3][^1_4] |
| **Disability-based decision-making** | NIHSS-centric | Shifted to disability assessment: a patient with disabling aphasia or hemianopia may have a low NIHSS but still warrants IVT [^1_4] |
| **Imaging delays** | Not emphasized | Explicit recommendation to avoid delays from multimodal imaging (CTA, CTP, MRP) in clearly eligible patients within 4.5 hours [^1_3][^1_4] |
| **Adjuvant antithrombotics** | Not addressed | COR 3: No Benefit — argatroban and eptifibatide as adjuncts to IVT showed no benefit and are not recommended [^1_3] |
| **Bridging IVT before EVT** | Recommended | Remains COR 1 — IVT should not be skipped to facilitate faster EVT [^1_4] |

### Contraindication Modifications

- Pre-IVT BP target remains SBP <185 mmHg / DBP <110 mmHg.
- The approach to thrombolysis contraindications has been modified, with a broader, more individualized approach to eligibility determination rather than rigid absolute exclusions [^1_2][^1_5].

***

## 3. Endovascular Thrombectomy (EVT) — Adults

EVT eligibility was substantially broadened, driven by multiple new RCTs.

### Anterior Circulation

| Indication | 2018 Guideline | 2026 Guideline |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **ICA/M1 occlusion, 0–6h, ASPECTS 6–10** | COR 1, LOE A (standard) | COR 1, LOE A — unchanged (ASPECTS now 3–10) [^1_4] |
| **ICA/M1 occlusion, 0–6h, ASPECTS 3–5** | Not addressed / often excluded | COR 1, LOE A — large-core infarct now eligible (SELECT2, TESLA, TENSION trials) [^1_6][^1_4] |
| **ICA/M1 occlusion, 0–6h, ASPECTS 0–2** | Excluded | COR 2a, LOE B-R — reasonable in select patients (age <80, NIHSS ≥6, mRS 0–1, no mass effect) [^1_4] |
| **ICA/M1 occlusion, 6–24h, ASPECTS ≥6** | COR 1 (DAWN, DEFUSE-3) | COR 1, LOE A — unchanged [^1_4] |
| **ICA/M1 occlusion, 6–24h, ASPECTS 3–5** | Excluded | COR 1, LOE A — now eligible (SELECT2, TESLA, TENSION) [^1_4] |
| **Prestroke mRS 2** | Excluded | COR 2a, LOE B-NR — EVT reasonable in select patients with moderate preexisting disability [^1_6][^1_4] |
| **Posterior circulation (basilar)** | No strong recommendation | COR 1, LOE A within 24h if NIHSS ≥10 and PC-ASPECTS ≥6 (ATTENTION, BAOCHE) [^1_3][^1_4] |
| **Medium/small vessel occlusion** | Not addressed | COR 3: No Benefit for routine use; may be considered in clinical trial only [^1_6] |

### Key Mechanism

The expansion into large-core territory reflects data showing that while absolute functional independence rates are lower than in small-core populations, the treatment effect is still present—selected patients benefit in both functional outcome and mortality [^1_4].

***

## 4. Pediatric Acute Ischemic Stroke

This is the **first time** the AHA/ASA AIS guideline includes dedicated recommendations for pediatric stroke [^1_6][^1_5].


| Intervention | Recommendation |
| :-- | :-- |
| **IV alteplase** (age 28 days–18 years, disabling deficits, within 4.5h) | May be considered [^1_6] |
| **Mechanical thrombectomy** (age ≥6 years, within 6h, large-vessel occlusion) | COR 2a — may be effective [^1_6] |
| **Mechanical thrombectomy** (age ≥6 years, 6–24h, salvageable tissue on imaging) | COR 2a — may be reasonable [^1_6] |
| **EVT** (age 28 days–6 years, within 24h) | COR 2b — may be considered; limited evidence base [^1_4] |

### Caveat

Pediatric recommendations are largely extrapolated from adult RCTs; dedicated pediatric stroke trials remain limited [^1_4].

***

## 5. Blood Pressure Management

This domain saw two critical "harm" designations.


| Scenario | 2018 Guideline | 2026 Guideline |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Pre-IVT** | Lower to <185/110 mmHg | Unchanged [^1_4] |
| **Post-IVT (24h)** | Maintain <180/105 mmHg | Unchanged; targeting SBP <140 mmHg is now COR 3: No Benefit (ENCHANTED trial — no outcome improvement with intensive lowering) [^1_3][^1_4] |
| **Post-EVT (successful reperfusion, mTICI 2b/3)** | <180/105 mmHg | **COR 3: HARM** — intensive SBP lowering to <140 mmHg within 72h of successful EVT increases death and disability (ENCHANTED2/MT trial). Optimal target: 140–180 mmHg [^1_3][^1_4][^1_1] |
| **No reperfusion after EVT** | Not specified | Individualized; avoid SBP >180 mmHg [^1_4] |
| **Permissive hypertension (no reperfusion)** | SBP up to 220 mmHg acceptable | Unchanged [^1_4] |

### Mechanistic Rationale

After successful reperfusion, aggressive BP reduction may compromise penumbral perfusion in regions with impaired autoregulation, converting salvageable tissue into infarction—a paradigm shift away from reflexive BP lowering after good angiographic results [^1_1].

***

## 6. Glycemic Management

| Parameter | 2018 Guideline | 2026 Guideline |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Target glucose** | 140–180 mg/dL suggested | Confirmed: treat >180 mg/dL with insulin infusion (target 140–180 mg/dL), COR 1 [^1_4] |
| **Intensive glucose control** | Not specifically addressed | **COR 3: No Benefit** — intensive IV insulin targeting 80–130 mg/dL showed no outcome improvement and increased severe hypoglycemia (SHINE trial, n=1,151) [^1_3][^1_4] |
| **Hypoglycemia** | Recognized as mimic | Confirmed: glucose <60 mg/dL is a stroke mimic and absolute contraindication to IVT [^1_4] |


***

## 7. Antiplatelet Therapy

| Indication | 2018 Guideline | 2026 Guideline |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **DAPT for minor AIS/high-risk TIA** | COR 1, LOE A within 24h; NIHSS ≤3 | COR 1, LOE A — now expanded to NIHSS ≤5; window extended to 72 hours (not just 24h); aspirin 325 mg + clopidogrel 300–600 mg loading → 21-day DAPT → monotherapy [^1_7][^1_4] |
| **Ticagrelor + aspirin** | COR 2b | COR 2a — for high-risk minor AIS/TIA with large artery atherosclerosis (LAA) etiology (THALES trial, HR 0.83) [^1_4] |
| **Aspirin within 24–48h (no IVT)** | COR 1, LOE A | Unchanged (IST, CAST) [^1_4] |
| **Aspirin within 24h of IVT** | Not recommended | Confirmed — risk of hemorrhagic transformation [^1_4] |


***

## 8. Early Anticoagulation for AF-Related Stroke

| Parameter | 2018 Guideline | 2026 Guideline |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **OAC timing** | Delay 3–14 days based on severity; expert opinion–driven | COR 2a: Early OAC (within 4–14 days) reasonable — ELAN trial (n=2,013) showed non-inferiority of early initiation (within 48h for minor, 3–7d for moderate, 6–7d for severe) vs. delayed (≥14d), with no increase in symptomatic ICH [^1_4] |
| **Heparin bridging** | Sometimes used | **COR 3: Harm** — not recommended while awaiting OAC initiation; no benefit, increased hemorrhage [^1_4] |
| **Severe strokes (NIHSS >15, large infarcts)** | Delay ≥14 days | Confirmed — individualized; delay ≥14 days for severe strokes [^1_4] |


***

## 9. DVT Prophylaxis

| Modality | 2018 Guideline | 2026 Guideline |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Intermittent pneumatic compression (IPC)** | COR 1 | COR 1, LOE A — confirmed (CLOTS 3 trial: proximal DVT 4.6% vs. 7.7%) [^1_4] |
| **Elastic compression stockings** | Sometimes used | **COR 3: Harm** — CLOTS 1 trial showed no benefit and increased skin breakdown; contraindicated for DVT prevention in AIS [^1_4] |
| **Subcutaneous anticoagulants** | Used after 24h | Not recommended within first 24h; may be used after 24h if no reperfusion given and no hemorrhagic transformation [^1_4] |


***

## 10. Dysphagia and Nutrition

| Recommendation | 2018 Guideline | 2026 Guideline |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Swallow screen before oral feeding** | COR 1 | Unchanged — all patients [^1_4] |
| **Pharyngeal electrical stimulation (PES)** | Not addressed | **COR 2a** — NEW recommendation; PHAST trial showed modest improvement in dysphagia; delivered via nasogastric tube electrode [^1_4] |
| **Early enteral nutrition** | Nasogastric preferred | Confirmed: NGT preferred over PEG in first 2–3 weeks (no survival difference; FOOD trial) [^1_4] |
| **Nutritional screening** | Not emphasized | New emphasis on early nutritional screening as a stroke-unit quality measure [^1_8] |


***

## 11. Rehabilitation

| Recommendation | 2018 Guideline | 2026 Guideline |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Early mobilization (24–72h, low-moderate intensity)** | Encouraged | COR 1 — short, gentle sitting/standing [^1_4] |
| **Very early high-intensity mobilization (<24h)** | Not addressed | **COR 3: Harm** — AVERT trial: early high-intensity mobilization <24h associated with worse 3-month outcomes (OR 0.59 for favorable outcome) [^1_4] |
| **SSRIs for motor recovery** | Investigational | **COR 3: No Benefit** — FOCUS, AFFINITY, EFFECTS trials (combined n >3,000): fluoxetine 20 mg daily × 6 months does not improve functional outcome and increases fracture risk [^1_4] |


***

## 12. Brain Swelling and Herniation

| Intervention | 2018 Guideline | 2026 Guideline |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Decompressive hemicraniectomy ≤60 years** | COR 1 | COR 1, LOE A — confirmed (DESTINY, DECIMAL, HAMLET); NNT 2 for survival; mortality reduced from 78% → 29%; 43% achieve mRS ≤3 [^1_4] |
| **Decompressive hemicraniectomy >60 years** | COR 2a | COR 2b — DESTINY II: reduces mortality (33% → 70% at 6 months) but majority survive with severe disability (mRS 4–5); requires individualized goals-of-care discussion [^1_4] |
| **IV glibenclamide for cerebral edema** | Investigational | **COR 3: No Benefit** — CHARM trial (n=94, phase 2b) failed primary outcome of reduced brain swelling on MRI [^1_4] |
| **Osmotic therapy (mannitol/hypertonic saline)** | Used empirically | Reasonable as bridge to surgery for acute herniation; no RCT outcome data [^1_4] |


***

## 13. Stroke Unit Organization

- **2026:** Dedicated stroke units are emphasized as a quality measure, with recommendations for tracking door-to-needle time, door-to-puncture time, successful reperfusion rates, and long-term outcomes [^1_8][^1_1].
- Neurointerventionalists should be credentialed according to established training and certification standards — quality is now defined by measurable system performance, not just technical ability [^1_1].

***

## Summary of Major Paradigm Shifts

1. **End of the alteplase-only era** — tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg is now co-equal (COR 1A), favored for its single-bolus administration and workflow integration [^1_1].
2. **Large-core infarction no longer a blanket exclusion** for EVT — ASPECTS 3–5 now COR 1A (extended window) and even ASPECTS 0–2 COR 2a (early window) [^1_4].
3. **Basilar artery occlusion** upgraded from uncertain to COR 1A for EVT within 24h with appropriate clinical/imaging criteria [^1_4].
4. **Two major "harm" designations for BP management** — intensive SBP <140 mmHg post-IVT is no benefit, and post-EVT is explicitly harmful [^1_3][^1_4].
5. **Disability-based rather than NIHSS-based** decision-making for IVT in mild stroke — the focus is on functional impact, not score [^1_4].
6. **First pediatric AIS recommendations** — IVT and EVT now have formal guidance for children [^1_6].
7. **Anti-rehabilitation evidence** — very early high-intensity mobilization and routine SSRI use for motor recovery are now COR 3 (harm or no benefit) [^1_4].
8. **Elimination of harmful practices** — elastic compression stockings, heparin bridging for AF, IV glibenclamide, adjuvant argatroban/eptifibatide all now COR 3 [^1_4].

# Elaborate on post IVT EVT care and medications

Post-IVT and post-EVT care in the 2026 AHA/ASA guideline is defined by a paradigm shift: multiple previously routine interventions are now explicitly classified as harmful or non-beneficial, while several new time-window-based medication protocols have been introduced [^2_1][^2_2][^2_3].

***

## Neurological Monitoring Protocol

All post-reperfusion patients require ICU or step-down monitoring for the first 24 hours [^2_3].

- Neurologic checks: q15 min × 2h, q30 min × 6h, q1h until 24h
- BP monitoring: continuous arterial line preferred, minimum q15 min during IVT infusion
- Emergent non-contrast CT for any neurological deterioration (new deficit, decreased GCS, pupillary changes, or sudden BP elevation)
- Repeat CBC, PT/INR, aPTT, fibrinogen if deterioration occurs

The OPTIMIST pilot suggested low-intensity monitoring may be safe in selected mild-deficit patients, but the guideline notes the optimal monitoring intensity remains uncertain [^2_3].

***

## Blood Pressure Management

This domain carries the two most consequential changes in post-reperfusion care.

### Post-IVT Blood Pressure

| Parameter | 2026 Recommendation | Evidence |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Target SBP | <180/105 mmHg | Standard of care [^2_3] |
| Intensive lowering (SBP <140) | **COR 3: No Benefit** | ENCHANTED (n=2,822): no difference in favorable outcome (mRS 0–2); trend toward reduced ICH offset by potential harm [^2_3] |

### Post-EVT Blood Pressure (Successfully Recanalized — mTICI 2b/3)

| Parameter | 2026 Recommendation | Evidence |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Target SBP | 140–180 mmHg | Standard post-EVT target [^2_3] |
| Intensive lowering (SBP <140 for 72h) | **COR 3: Harm** | ENCHANTED2/MT (stopped early for harm signal); BEST-II (no benefit) [^2_3] |

### Mechanistic Rationale

After successful reperfusion, regions of the penumbra have impaired autoregulation due to ischemic endothelial injury. Aggressive BP reduction below 140 mmHg compromises perfusion pressure to this vulnerable tissue, converting salvageable penumbra into infarction — the mechanism behind the mortality and disability signal seen in ENCHANTED2/MT [^2_3].

### When No Reperfusion Achieved

- Permissive hypertension: SBP up to 220 mmHg acceptable to maintain collateral perfusion [^2_2]
- Initiate antihypertensive therapy only if SBP >220 or DBP >120 mmHg [^2_2]
- If treatment necessary: modest reduction (10–15% in first 24 hours) [^2_2]

***

## Glycemic Management

| Strategy | 2026 Recommendation | Evidence |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Hyperglycemia treatment | COR 1: Treat >180 mg/dL with insulin infusion; target 140–180 mg/dL | Standard [^2_2] |
| Intensive IV insulin (target 80–130 mg/dL) | **COR 3: No Benefit** | SHINE trial (n=1,151): no outcome improvement; increased severe hypoglycemia [^2_2] |
| Hypoglycemia (<60 mg/dL) | Absolute contraindication to IVT; treat immediately | Stroke mimic [^2_2] |

Hyperglycemia worsens outcomes through anaerobic metabolism, lactic acidosis, free radical generation, and blood-brain barrier disruption. However, tight control is counterproductive due to neuroglycopenic injury risk in ischemic tissue with impaired glucose transport [^2_2][^2_3].

***

## Antithrombotic Medication Timing

### Antiplatelet Therapy

| Scenario | Timing | COR/LOE | Rationale |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Aspirin post-IVT | Hold ≥24h; start after repeat CT confirms no hemorrhage | COR 2b | Risk of hemorrhagic transformation in first 24h uncertain [^2_3] |
| DAPT for minor non-disabling AIS (NIHSS ≤5) | Within 72h of onset (not IVT); aspirin 300–600mg + clopidogrel 300–600mg loading → 21-day DAPT → monotherapy | COR 1, LOE A | Extended window from prior 24h; prevents early recurrence in LAA etiology [^2_4][^2_2] |
| Ticagrelor + aspirin (LAA etiology) | Within 24h for high-risk minor stroke/TIA | COR 2a | THALES trial (HR 0.83 for stroke/death) [^2_2] |
| Adjuvant antithrombotics with IVT (argatroban, eptifibatide) | **NOT recommended** | COR 3: No Benefit | CLEAR trial: safe but no added benefit [^2_2][^2_3] |

### Anticoagulation for Atrial Fibrillation

This is a major shift from the previous conservative "delay 3–14 days" approach, now stratified by severity.


| Stroke Severity | DOAC Initiation | COR/LOE | Key Trial |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Minor (NIHSS <5) | Within 24–48 hours | COR 2a | ELAN (n=2,013), OPTIMAS [^2_3] |
| Moderate (NIHSS 5–15) | 3–6 days | COR 2a | ELAN, OPTIMAS [^2_3] |
| Severe (NIHSS >15 or large infarct) | 6–14 days; individualize | COR 2a | ELAN, OPTIMAS [^2_3] |
| Heparin bridging while awaiting OAC | **NOT recommended** | COR 3: Harm | No benefit; increased hemorrhage [^2_2] |

ELAN and OPTIMAS demonstrated non-inferiority of early DOAC initiation versus delayed start (≥7–14 days), with no increase in symptomatic ICH [^2_3].

***

## DVT/VTE Prophylaxis

| Modality | Timing | COR/LOE | Evidence |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Intermittent pneumatic compression (IPC) | Immediate for all non-ambulatory patients | COR 1, LOE A | CLOTS-3: proximal DVT 4.6% vs. 7.7% [^2_2] |
| Elastic compression stockings | **Contraindicated** | COR 3: Harm | CLOTS-1: no benefit; increased skin breakdown, infection, amputation [^2_2] |
| Pharmacologic prophylaxis (LMWH) | Delay ≥24h post-IVT; reassess bleed risk | After 24h if no hemorrhagic transformation | [^2_3] |


***

## Temperature Management

| Strategy | Recommendation | COR |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Treat fever >38.5°C | Antipyretics, cooling blankets | COR 1 [^2_2] |
| Induced hypothermia | **Not recommended** for neurological outcome | COR 3: No Benefit [^2_2] |
| Maintain normothermia and normoxia | Standard supportive care | COR 1 [^2_2] |


***

## Mobilization and Rehabilitation

| Strategy | Recommendation | COR | Evidence |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Early mobilization (24–72h, low-moderate intensity) | Short, gentle sitting/standing | COR 1 [^2_2] |  |
| Very early high-intensity mobilization (<24h) | **COR 3: Harm** | AVERT (n=2,104): OR 0.59 for favorable outcome [^2_2] |  |
| SSRIs (fluoxetine 20mg) for motor recovery | **COR 3: No Benefit** | FOCUS, AFFINITY, EFFECTS (n>3,000): no functional improvement; increased fracture risk [^2_2] |  |


***

## Acute Complications Management

### Symptomatic Intracranial Hemorrhage (sICH)

| Step | Action |
| :-- | :-- |
| 1 | **Stop thrombolytic infusion immediately** |
| 2 | Emergent labs: CBC, PT/INR, aPTT, fibrinogen, type and crossmatch |
| 3 | Emergent non-contrast head CT |
| 4 | **Cryoprecipitate** 10 U IV (target fibrinogen ≥150 mg/dL) |
| 5 | **Tranexamic acid** 1000 mg IV over 10 min (or ε-aminocaproic acid 4–5g) |
| 6 | Neurosurgery + hematology consultation |
| 7 | ICP management: head of bed 30°, head midline, osmotic therapy if needed [^2_2][^2_3] |

### Orolingual Angioedema

| Step | Action |
| :-- | :-- |
| 1 | Maintain airway; prepare for intubation if larynx/oropharynx involved |
| 2 | Discontinue thrombolytic; hold ACE inhibitors |
| 3 | Methylprednisolone 125mg IV + Diphenhydramine 50mg IV + Famotidine 20mg IV |
| 4 | If progressing: Epinephrine 0.3 mL of 0.1% SC or nebulized |
| 5 | Refractory: **Icatibant 30mg SC** or C1 esterase inhibitor 20 IU/kg [^2_3] |


***

## Other "Do Not Do" Recommendations

| Intervention | COR | Rationale |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| IV Glibenclamide for cerebral edema | COR 3: No Benefit | CHARM trial (n=94): failed primary endpoint of reduced brain swelling on MRI [^2_2] |
| Prophylactic antiepileptic drugs (phenytoin) | COR 3: No Benefit | No evidence of seizure prevention; potential adverse effects [^2_2] |
| Prehospital BP lowering or GTN | Not recommended | No benefit; potential harm especially in hemorrhagic stroke [^2_5] |


***

## Clinical Pearls for ICU Practice

1. **BP is the most critical parameter post-EVT.** The reflex to lower BP after successful reperfusion is now explicitly harmful. Maintain SBP 140–180 mmHg — this is a hard target, not a suggestion [^2_3].
2. **Tenecteplase does not change post-IVT monitoring protocols.** The 24-hour BP and neuro-check schedule remains identical to alteplase [^2_2].
3. **DOAC timing is now severity-stratified, not time-fixed.** Use NIHSS as the primary triage tool: minor strokes can start DOACs within 48h, eliminating the blanket 14-day delay that left many patients unprotected [^2_3].
4. **DAPT has replaced IVT for minor non-disabling stroke.** If the deficit is non-disabling (e.g., isolated sensory change), DAPT within 72h is preferred — IVT provides no benefit in this population [^2_6][^2_2].
5. **Compression stockings must be removed** from stroke order sets. They are now a Class 3: Harm intervention [^2_2].
6. **Fluoxetine should not be prescribed for motor recovery.** Despite prior enthusiasm, three concurrent RCTs (n>3,000) showed no benefit and increased fracture risk [^2_2].
