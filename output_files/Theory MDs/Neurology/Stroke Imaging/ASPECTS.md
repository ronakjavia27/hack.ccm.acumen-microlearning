
# ASPECTS

**ASPECTS** means **Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score**. It is a 10-point semiquantitative score used on baseline non-contrast CT to estimate the extent of early ischemic change in **acute anterior-circulation, usually MCA-territory, ischemic stroke**. A normal scan scores 10; one point is subtracted for each predefined region showing early ischemic change. [^1_2][^1_4]

## Calculation

Start with:

$$
\text{ASPECTS}=10-\text{number of involved regions}
$$

Early ischemic changes include:

- Focal hypoattenuation.
- Loss of gray–white matter differentiation.
- Cortical swelling or sulcal effacement.

The 10 regions are:


| Region | Abbreviation |
| :-- | :-- |
| Caudate nucleus | C |
| Lentiform nucleus | L |
| Internal capsule | IC |
| Insular ribbon | I |
| Anterior MCA cortex | M1 |
| Lateral MCA cortex at insular level | M2 |
| Posterior MCA cortex | M3 |
| Anterior MCA cortex above basal ganglia | M4 |
| Lateral MCA cortex above basal ganglia | M5 |
| Posterior MCA cortex above basal ganglia | M6 |

**Example:** If early ischemic change is present in the insula, lentiform nucleus, M2, and M5:

$$
\text{ASPECTS}=10-4=6
$$

## Interpretation

| ASPECTS | General meaning |
| :-- | :-- |
| 10 | No visible early ischemic change |
| 8–9 | Small infarct burden |
| 6–7 | Moderate early ischemic involvement |
| 0–5 | Extensive ischemic change and large infarct burden |

A lower score generally indicates a larger established infarct core, poorer functional prognosis, and greater risk of hemorrhagic transformation. However, ASPECTS is not an absolute treatment cut-off; interpretation should be integrated with time from onset, NIHSS, vessel occlusion, collateral status, CT perfusion or MRI, premorbid function, and treatment eligibility. [^1_5][^1_8]

# PC-ASPECTS

**PC-ASPECTS** means **Posterior Circulation Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score**. It is a 10-point score designed for early ischemic changes in **posterior-circulation stroke**, particularly basilar artery occlusion and vertebrobasilar ischemia.

The score starts at 10, with points deducted for ischemic involvement of the following structures: [^1_1][^1_3][^1_6]


| Structure | Points deducted |
| :-- | --: |
| Right thalamus | 1 |
| Left thalamus | 1 |
| Right occipital lobe | 1 |
| Left occipital lobe | 1 |
| Right cerebellar hemisphere | 1 |
| Left cerebellar hemisphere | 1 |
| Pons | 2 |
| Midbrain | 2 |
| **Maximum deduction** | **10** |

Thus:

$$
\text{PC-ASPECTS}=10-\text{weighted points for ischemic regions}
$$

The pons and midbrain receive **2 points each**, because involvement of these structures is particularly important for consciousness, respiratory drive, ocular motor function, corticospinal pathways, and risk of coma or locked-in syndrome. [^1_1]

## Example

CT shows:

- Right cerebellar ischemia: −1.
- Left thalamic ischemia: −1.
- Pontine ischemia: −2.

$$
\text{PC-ASPECTS}=10-(1+1+2)=6
$$

# ASPECTS Versus PC-ASPECTS

| Feature | ASPECTS | PC-ASPECTS |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Primary vascular territory | Anterior circulation | Posterior circulation |
| Common clinical setting | MCA large-vessel occlusion | Basilar or vertebrobasilar occlusion |
| Imaging modality | Usually baseline non-contrast CT | Non-contrast CT; CTA-source images may provide additional information |
| Starting score | 10 | 10 |
| Scored regions | 10 MCA regions | Bilateral thalami, occipital lobes, cerebellum, pons, and midbrain |
| Important weighting | Each region usually 1 point | Pons and midbrain count 2 points each |
| Main purpose | Estimate anterior-circulation infarct core and treatment suitability | Estimate posterior-circulation ischemic burden and prognosis |

# Utilisation in the ICU

## 1. Initial stroke triage

In a patient with acute focal neurological deficit, reduced consciousness, aphasia, hemiparesis, gaze deviation, or suspected large-vessel occlusion:

1. Perform non-contrast CT to exclude hemorrhage.
2. Calculate ASPECTS for suspected anterior-circulation stroke.
3. Perform CT angiography to identify ICA, M1, M2, or basilar occlusion.
4. Consider CT perfusion or MRI when the time of onset is unknown, presentation is late, or the CT score is uncertain.

For posterior-circulation presentations—vertigo, diplopia, dysarthria, ataxia, quadriparesis, abnormal pupils, coma, or unexplained respiratory failure—calculate PC-ASPECTS and obtain CTA early, because a normal or near-normal initial CT does not exclude clinically important brainstem ischemia.

## 2. Reperfusion decision-making

ASPECTS is used as one component of eligibility assessment for intravenous thrombolysis and mechanical thrombectomy. Historically, a score of **≥6** was commonly used in thrombectomy selection, while low ASPECTS patients were often excluded from trials; more recent evidence has expanded thrombectomy consideration to selected patients with larger ischemic cores. Therefore, a low ASPECTS should prompt individualized review rather than automatic therapeutic nihilism.

Important modifiers include:

- Site of arterial occlusion.
- NIHSS and clinical–radiological mismatch.
- Age and premorbid functional status.
- Time last known well.
- Collateral circulation.
- Core volume on CT perfusion or diffusion MRI.
- Presence of mass effect or hemorrhagic transformation.
- Risk of futile recanalization.

For posterior-circulation stroke, PC-ASPECTS may support prognosis and selection for endovascular therapy, but it should not replace clinical assessment or CTA evidence of basilar occlusion. A low PC-ASPECTS, particularly with extensive bilateral brainstem involvement, suggests advanced infarction and lower probability of meaningful recovery; nevertheless, treatment decisions remain individualized. [^1_1][^1_6]

## 3. ICU prognostication

Serial neurological examination remains more important than a single imaging score. ASPECTS and PC-ASPECTS can help communicate infarct burden and anticipate:

- Cerebral edema.
- Malignant MCA infarction.
- Brainstem swelling.
- Need for airway protection and mechanical ventilation.
- Risk of aspiration and dysphagia.
- Need for neurosurgical consultation.
- Expected rehabilitation complexity.
- Potential for herniation or neurological deterioration.

A low ASPECTS does **not** independently determine death or neurological outcome. Prognostication should be delayed until confounders such as sedation, intubation, seizures, hypoxemia, hypotension, hypoglycemia, fever, and metabolic encephalopathy have been addressed.

## 4. Neurocritical-care monitoring

Patients with extensive infarction or low scores require close monitoring of:

- Level of consciousness and pupillary responses.
- New cranial-nerve deficits.
- Respiratory pattern and airway reflexes.
- Blood pressure and cerebral perfusion.
- Temperature, glucose, oxygenation, and carbon dioxide.
- Development of cerebral edema or hydrocephalus.
- Hemorrhagic transformation after reperfusion therapy.

In large MCA infarction, a declining ASPECTS or extensive early hypodensity should trigger early discussion with neurosurgery regarding decompressive hemicraniectomy in appropriate patients. In cerebellar or brainstem infarction, deterioration may require urgent evaluation for posterior-fossa decompression or treatment of obstructive hydrocephalus.

# ICU Practical Pitfalls

- **Do not equate ASPECTS with NIHSS.** NIHSS may underestimate posterior-circulation stroke and brainstem dysfunction.
- **Do not score chronic abnormalities as acute ischemia.** Old infarcts, leukoaraiosis, atrophy, and motion artifact can reduce reliability.
- **Do not use a single low score as an absolute contraindication to thrombectomy.** Modern trials include selected patients with larger infarct cores.
- **Do not regard a normal early posterior-circulation CT as reassuring.** The posterior fossa is affected by beam-hardening artifact, and early ischemia may be subtle.
- **Confirm uncertain findings with CTA, CT perfusion, MRI-DWI, or expert neuroradiology review.**
- **Use the score as a decision-support tool, not as a substitute for clinical judgment.**
- **Always document the side, regions involved, imaging modality, and time of imaging**, for example: “ASPECTS 6 on baseline NCCT, involving left insula, caudate, M2, and M5.”


### Clinical pearl

**ASPECTS estimates anterior-circulation infarct burden; PC-ASPECTS does the same for posterior circulation, with the pons and midbrain weighted more heavily. In the ICU, both are most useful for rapid communication, reperfusion triage, monitoring intensity, neurocritical-care planning, and structured—but not isolated—prognostication.**
