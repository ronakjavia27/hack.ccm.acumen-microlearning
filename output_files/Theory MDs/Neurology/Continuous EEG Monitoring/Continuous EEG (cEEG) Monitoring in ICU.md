

## Continuous EEG (cEEG) Monitoring in ICU

**Exam-Oriented, High-Yield Notes**

***

## 1. Definition

- **Continuous EEG (cEEG)**: ≥16-channel EEG recording (≥8 in neonates), typically with video, for **>12 hours** in ICU to detect and monitor **electrographic seizures**, encephalopathy trends, ischemia, and sedation depth. [^1_1][^1_2]

***

## 2. Why cEEG in ICU? (Key Rationale)

- **Majority of ICU seizures are nonconvulsive** → missed on clinical exam. [^1_2]
- **Nonconvulsive status epilepticus (NCSE)** occurs in **8–37%** of ICU patients, especially in coma, post–cardiac arrest, TBI, sepsis, and metabolic encephalopathy. [^1_3][^1_2]
- cEEG provides **real-time, high temporal resolution** monitoring of cortical function in obtunded/comatose patients. [^1_4]

***

## 3. Indications for cEEG in ICU

### A. Strong / Core Indications (Must Know for Exams)

1. **Suspected or confirmed nonconvulsive seizures / NCSE**
    - Unexplained or fluctuating altered mental status
    - Persistent coma or impaired consciousness **>10–60 min after convulsive seizure** or sedation hold
    - History of recent convulsive status epilepticus [^1_5][^1_1][^1_6][^1_7]
2. **Refractory status epilepticus (RSE)**
    - To titrate IV anesthetic/antiseizure infusions (midazolam, propofol, pentobarbital) to target:
        - Seizure suppression
        - Burst suppression
        - Complete EEG suppression [^1_5][^1_6][^1_8]
3. **Comatose ICU patients without clear primary brain injury**
    - Unexplained persistent altered consciousness, especially with:
        - Sepsis
        - Renal/hepatic failure
        - Metabolic derangements [^1_1][^1_6][^1_7]
4. **High-risk acute brain injury with seizure risk**
    - Traumatic brain injury (TBI), especially severe or with contusions
    - Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH)
    - Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH)
    - Ischemic stroke with unexplained deterioration
    - CNS infections (encephalitis, meningitis)
    - Brain tumors, recent neurosurgery [^1_1][^1_3][^1_9][^1_10]
5. **Post–cardiac arrest with persistent coma**
    - To detect NCSE
    - To aid neuroprognostication (background reactivity, status epilepticus, suppressed patterns) [^1_11][^1_1][^1_6][^1_12]
6. **During therapeutic hypothermia and 24 h after rewarming**
    - High risk of electrographic seizures despite no clinical signs [^1_11][^1_1]
7. **Patients under neuromuscular blockade or deep sedation**
    - When clinical seizure detection is impossible (e.g., ECMO, ARDS paralysis, TTM protocols) [^1_5][^1_7][^1_9]

***

### B. Additional / Evolving Indications

8. **Characterization of paroxysmal events**
    - Abnormal movements (myoclonus, posturing, eye deviation)
    - Paroxysmal autonomic spells (tachycardia, BP surges, sweating) [^1_11][^1_3][^1_10]
9. **Monitoring sedation / anesthetic depth**
    - Titration of sedatives in RSE, intracranial hypertension
    - Avoiding over-sedation and burst suppression when not intended [^1_11][^1_8][^1_12][^1_13]
10. **Detection of cerebral ischemia**
    - aSAH: early detection of delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI) via EEG slowing/spreading depression
    - During/after endovascular procedures, carotid clamping, hemodynamic instability [^1_11][^1_1][^1_6][^1_12]
11. **Prognostication**
    - Post–cardiac arrest encephalopathy
    - Severe TBI, severe encephalitis, hypoxic–ischemic injury
    - Patterns: suppressed background, absent reactivity, status epilepticus → poor prognosis [^1_11][^1_1][^1_6][^1_13]
12. **Brain death evaluation (adjunct)**
    - Electrocerebral silence as supportive evidence where legally/locally accepted [^1_11]

***

## 4. Feasibility in ICU

### A. Technical Feasibility

- **Digital cEEG systems** allow:
    - 16–32 channel recording
    - Continuous storage and trend analysis
    - Remote review (tele-EEG) [^1_2][^1_4]
- **Reduced setup time** with quick-apply caps and dry electrodes in some systems. [^1_9]
- **Quantitative EEG (qEEG)** tools (amplitude-integrated EEG, spectrograms, seizure detectors) assist non-neurophysiologists. [^1_4][^1_13]


### B. Operational Feasibility

- Most seizures detected in **first 24 h** in noncomatose patients; comatose patients may need **24–72 h** or longer. [^1_2]
- Requires:
    - Trained EEG technologists or ICU staff trained in electrode application
    - Neurophysiologist/neurologist for interpretation (or trained intensivists with qEEG support) [^1_14][^1_13]
- Feasible in tertiary ICUs; increasingly used in resource-limited centers with simplified systems and tele-EEG. [^1_1][^1_9]

***

## 5. Limitations and Challenges

### A. Interpretation-Related

- **Artifacts** from:
    - Ventilators, infusion pumps
    - Patient movement, shivering, nursing care
    - ECMO, CRRT, electrical interference [^1_15][^1_13]
- **Subtle ictal vs interictal patterns** in critically ill:
    - Periodic discharges (LPDs, GPDs) may be ictal or interictal
    - Requires expertise and clinical correlation [^1_15]
- **Lack of standardized criteria** for some patterns (e.g., what exactly constitutes NCSE in comatose patients). [^1_15]


### B. Resource and Logistical

- **Cost and equipment**: cEEG machines, electrodes, software, storage. [^1_14]
- **Human resources**: shortage of neurophysiologists/EEG-trained staff in many centers. [^1_14][^1_13]
- **Time-intensive**: setup, daily review, reporting. [^1_14]
- **Limited availability** in low-resource settings; often restricted to neuro-ICUs or tertiary centers. [^1_1][^1_14]


### C. Clinical / Evidence Limitations

- Many recommendations are **strong but based on low-quality evidence** (expert consensus, observational data). [^1_1]
- **Outcome benefit** of cEEG-guided management (beyond seizure detection) is still being defined for ischemia monitoring and prognostication. [^1_6][^1_13]
- Risk of **over-treatment** of electrographic patterns whose clinical significance is uncertain (e.g., some periodic discharges). [^1_15]

***

## 6. Other Usages / Expanded Roles

- **Encephalopathy grading**:
    - EEG background frequency, reactivity, continuity used to grade severity and track evolution. [^1_11][^1_12][^1_13]
- **Monitoring treatment response**:
    - Antiseizure drugs, anesthetic infusions, osmotic therapy, temperature management. [^1_11][^1_8][^1_4]
- **Research and quality improvement**:
    - Seizure burden, EEG biomarkers for outcome, drug effects on brain function. [^1_14][^1_13]
- **Educational tool**:
    - Teaching ICU staff about brain–organ interactions, sedation effects, seizure recognition. [^1_9][^1_13]

***

## 7. Quick Exam Pearls

- **Most ICU seizures are nonconvulsive** → cEEG is essential when mental status is unexplained or fluctuating. [^1_2]
- **Start cEEG urgently** if:
    - Convulsive seizure patient not back to baseline within **10–60 min**
    - Suspected NCSE in coma, TBI, SAH, sepsis, post–cardiac arrest [^1_5][^1_1][^1_9]
- **Refractory status epilepticus**: cEEG is mandatory to titrate anesthetic/antiseizure infusions. [^1_5][^1_6]
- **Therapeutic hypothermia**: cEEG during cooling and 24 h after rewarming to detect hidden seizures. [^1_11][^1_1]
- **Limitations**: artifacts, expertise requirement, cost, and some evidence still low-quality. [^1_14][^1_15][^1_13]
