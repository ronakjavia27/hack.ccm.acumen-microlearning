
## Invasive Intracranial Pressure (ICP) Monitoring in ICU

Invasive ICP monitoring remains the **gold standard** for accurate, continuous measurement of intracranial pressure in neurocritical care. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3] The two primary invasive methods are **external ventricular drainage (EVD/ventriculostomy)** and **intraparenchymal monitors**, with several alternative techniques available for specific scenarios. [^1_3][^1_4]

***

## Normal Values and Thresholds

- **Normal ICP**: 5–15 mm Hg (7–15 mm Hg in supine position) [^1_1][^1_5]
- **Treatment threshold**: ICP >20–25 mm Hg typically triggers ICP-lowering therapy [^1_1]
- **Critical threshold**: Sustained ICP >25 mm Hg associated with cerebral ischemia and herniation risk [^1_1]

***

## Indications for Invasive ICP Monitoring

**Strong indications (Class I–II evidence):**

- Severe traumatic brain injury (TBI) with GCS 3–8 and abnormal CT [^1_6][^1_4]
- Hydrocephalus requiring CSF drainage [^1_5]
- Subarachnoid hemorrhage with hydrocephalus or depressed consciousness [^1_5]
- Space-occupying lesions with mass effect [^1_5]
- Idiopathic intracranial hypertension (refractory cases) [^1_5]
- Reye's syndrome [^1_5]

**Contraindications:**

- Coagulopathy or anticoagulation (relative) [^1_5]
- Scalp infection at insertion site [^1_5]
- Brain abscess [^1_5]

***

## Methods of Invasive ICP Monitoring

### **1. External Ventricular Drain (EVD) / Intraventricular Catheter (Ventriculostomy)**

**Procedure:**

- Catheter inserted into lateral ventricle via burr hole (usually frontal approach) [^1_3][^1_7]
- Connected to external pressure transducer and drainage system [^1_7]

**Advantages:**

- **Gold standard** for accuracy — measures global intracranial pressure [^1_1][^1_2][^1_4]
- Allows **therapeutic CSF drainage** (critical in hydrocephalus) [^1_3][^1_4][^1_7]
- **In vivo recalibration possible** — maintains accuracy over time [^1_1][^1_4]
- Can infuse intraventricular medications (antibiotics, thrombolytics) [^1_2]
- Minimal zero drift [^1_1]

**Disadvantages/Limitations:**


| Limitation | Clinical Impact |
| :-- | :-- |
| **High infection risk** (ventriculitis: 3.4–32.2%, mean ~9–20%) [^1_8][^1_9] | Risk increases significantly after day 5; limits long-term use [^1_8] |
| **Hemorrhage risk** (10% intracranial/tract hemorrhage) [^1_1] | Especially concerning in coagulopathic TBI patients [^1_6] |
| **Technical failure** (5% malplacement or failure to tap ventricle) [^1_1][^1_9] | Difficult in small/collapsed ventricles or midline shift [^1_1] |
| **Most invasive method** [^1_4] | Requires ventricular puncture; higher procedural risk [^1_4] |
| **Catheter obstruction** by blood/debris | Loss of monitoring and drainage capability [^1_1][^1_6] |
| **Over-drainage complications** | Can cause aneurysmal rebleed or upward transtentorial herniation [^1_1] |
| **Restricted patient mobility** | Tethered system limits transport and rehabilitation [^1_10] |


***

### **2. Intraparenchymal ICP Monitor (Fiber-optic or Strain Gauge)**

**Procedure:**

- Probe inserted into brain parenchyma (usually frontal lobe, non-dominant hemisphere) via burr hole [^1_3]
- Measures local tissue pressure, transmitted as global ICP estimate [^1_1]

**Common devices:** Codman, Camino, Raumedic, Integra [^1_1][^1_10]

**Advantages:**


| Advantage | Clinical Relevance |
| :-- | :-- |
| **High accuracy** (comparable to EVD) [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3] | Reliable for treatment decisions |
| **Easier insertion** | Feasible even with midline shift or malignant edema [^1_1] |
| **Lower infection risk** (0.8–5%) [^1_8][^1_4] | Safer for prolonged monitoring (>5 days) |
| **No catheter obstruction risk** | Not affected by blood/debris like EVD [^1_1] |
| **Multiple placement options** | Can be placed in non-dominant frontal lobe or lesion side [^1_3] |
| **Combined monitoring available** | Can measure brain tissue oxygen (PbtO₂) and temperature simultaneously [^1_3] |

**Disadvantages/Limitations:**


| Limitation | Clinical Impact |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Cannot recalibrate in vivo** [^1_1][^1_10][^1_4] | Zero drift over time (small but cumulative) affects long-term accuracy [^1_2][^1_4] |
| **Measures local pressure only** [^1_1][^1_4] | May not reflect global ICP in heterogeneous pathology (e.g., focal mass lesions) |
| **No CSF drainage capability** [^1_3][^1_4][^1_7] | Cannot treat hydrocephalus therapeutically |
| **Brain tissue injury** | Small risk of parenchymal damage at insertion site [^1_3] |
| **Sensor displacement/dislodgement** | May occur with patient movement or transportation [^1_10] |
| **Higher cost** | More expensive than EVD systems [^1_2] |


***

### **3. Subdural Bolt/Screw**

**Procedure:**

- Bolt or screw placed through dura into subdural space [^1_3]

**Advantages:**

- Easier placement than EVD [^1_3]
- Lower risk of brain injury or hemorrhage compared to intraparenchymal/EVD [^1_3]

**Disadvantages/Limitations:**


| Limitation | Clinical Impact |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Moderate accuracy** (less than EVD/parenchymal) [^1_3] | May underestimate true ICP |
| **No CSF drainage** [^1_3] | Cannot treat hydrocephalus |
| **Moderate infection risk** [^1_3] | Higher than parenchymal, lower than EVD |
| **Cannot recalibrate** | Drift over time |


***

### **4. Epidural Sensor**

**Procedure:**

- Sensor placed between skull and dura (epidural space) [^1_3]

**Advantages:**

- **Least invasive** — does not penetrate dura [^1_3]
- **Lowest infection and hemorrhage risk** [^1_3]
- Easy to place [^1_3]

**Disadvantages/Limitations:**


| Limitation | Clinical Impact |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Low accuracy** [^1_3] | Significant underestimation of true ICP; not recommended for critical decisions |
| **No CSF drainage** [^1_3] | Purely diagnostic |
| **Affected by skull compliance** | Readings influenced by epidural space dynamics |


***

### **5. Subarachnoid Bolt**

**Procedure:**

- Catheter placed into subarachnoid space via lumbar or cranial approach [^1_3]

**Advantages:**

- Moderately easy placement [^1_3]
- Some models allow limited CSF drainage [^1_3]

**Disadvantages/Limitations:**


| Limitation | Clinical Impact |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Prone to blockage** [^1_3] | Catheter obstruction by debris/protein |
| **Moderate accuracy** (less than EVD/parenchymal) [^1_3] | May not reflect true ICP accurately |
| **Moderate infection risk** [^1_3] | Meningitis risk |

## Complications of Invasive ICP Monitoring

**Overall complication rates:**

- **Infection**: 0.8–20% (EVD highest, parenchymal lowest) [^1_1][^1_8]
- **Hemorrhage**: 1.2–10% [^1_1][^1_8]
- **Technical failure/malplacement**: 5–12% [^1_1][^1_9]
- **Sensor drift**: Variable (significant in parenchymal over time) [^1_1][^1_2]

**Specific complications:**

- Meningitis/ventriculitis [^1_5]
- Intracranial/intraventricular hemorrhage [^1_1][^1_5]
- Catheter obstruction (EVD) [^1_1][^1_6]
- Device malfunction or inaccurate readings [^1_6][^1_5]
- Ventricular collapse with tentorial herniation (over-drainage) [^1_1][^1_5]
- Seizures (rare) [^1_10]

***

## Key Clinical Pearls for Exams

1. **EVD is gold standard** when accuracy and CSF drainage both needed [^1_1][^1_2][^1_7]
2. **Intraparenchymal monitors preferred** in malignant edema, midline shift, or coagulopathy (lower hemorrhage risk) [^1_1][^1_4]
3. **Infection risk increases after day 5** for EVD — consider prophylactic antibiotic-impregnated catheters or early removal [^1_8]
4. **Parenchymal monitors drift** — zero drift cumulative over days; cannot recalibrate in vivo [^1_1][^1_2][^1_4]
5. **EVD contraindicated** in coagulopathy (relative) — correct coagulopathy first or use parenchymal monitor [^1_5]
6. **Normal ICP 5–15 mm Hg**; treat sustained pressures >20–25 mm Hg [^1_1][^1_5]
7. **Accuracy standard**: Invasive vs non-invasive should differ by ≤2 mm Hg (0–20 mm Hg range) or <10% (20–100 mm Hg) [^1_1]

***

## Landmark Guidelines and Evidence

- **Brain Trauma Foundation Guidelines**: ICP monitoring recommended in severe TBI (GCS 3–8) with abnormal CT (Class II evidence) [^1_4]
- **No Level I evidence** for mortality benefit, but widely adopted as standard of care [^1_6][^1_11]
- **Recent evidence** (2025): EVD and intraparenchymal monitors both carry hemorrhage and infection risks; choice depends on clinical scenario [^1_3]
