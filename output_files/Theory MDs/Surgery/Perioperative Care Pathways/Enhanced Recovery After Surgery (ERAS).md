
## Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS)

**Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS)** is a multimodal, multidisciplinary, evidence-based perioperative care pathway designed to attenuate the surgical stress response, maintain physiological function, and accelerate recovery through standardized interventions across preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative phases. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3]

***

## Definition and Core Concept

ERAS represents a **paradigm shift** from traditional perioperative care to a patient-centered, physiology-preserving approach. [^1_4][^1_5]

- **Goal**: Minimize surgical stress, maintain anabolic homeostasis, reduce insulin resistance, and restore baseline function rapidly. [^1_3]
- **Mechanism**: Bundled interventions targeting specific pathophysiological responses to surgery (catabolism, inflammation, immobility, opioid-related side effects). [^1_6][^1_7]
- **Scope**: Applicable across surgical specialties (colorectal, hepatobiliary, orthopedic, cardiac, urology, gynecology, thoracic, etc.). [^1_1][^1_8]

***

## Historical Background

- **Origin**: Introduced in **1997 by Henrik Kehlet** (Denmark), who proposed that small, evidence-based changes in perioperative practice could cumulatively improve outcomes. [^1_6][^1_8]
- **Evolution**: ERAS Society (international nonprofit) now develops and updates procedure-specific guidelines based on systematic evidence review. [^1_2]
- **Adoption**: Now considered **standard of care** in many surgical specialties worldwide. [^1_3]

***

## ERAS Pathway Components

ERAS protocols are organized into **three temporal phases**, each with specific evidence-based interventions. [^1_1][^1_5][^1_8]

### Preoperative Phase

| Intervention | Rationale | Evidence/Notes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Preoperative counseling \& education** | Reduces anxiety, improves compliance, sets expectations | Core ERAS element [^1_1][^1_5] |
| **Smoking \& alcohol cessation** (≥4 weeks pre-op) | Reduces pulmonary, wound, and cardiovascular complications | Strong evidence [^1_1] |
| **Optimization of comorbidities** (anemia, diabetes, cardiac, pulmonary) | Improves physiological reserve | Anemia screening/treatment recommended [^1_1] |
| **Nutritional assessment \& optimization** | Prevents/treats malnutrition, improves outcomes | High-risk patients benefit from prehabilitation [^1_1][^1_3] |
| **Carbohydrate loading** (clear carbohydrate drink 2–3 h pre-op) | Reduces insulin resistance, preserves muscle mass, decreases hunger/thirst | Replaces traditional overnight fasting [^1_6][^1_2][^1_5] |
| **Avoid prolonged fasting** | Prevents catabolic state, dehydration | Fasting from midnight is outdated [^1_2][^1_5] |
| **Avoid mechanical bowel preparation** (for colorectal unless specific indication) | Prevents dehydration, electrolyte imbalance | No benefit in most colorectal surgeries [^1_1][^1_5] |
| **No routine preoperative sedation** | Avoids delayed recovery, respiratory depression | Short-acting anxiolytics only if needed [^1_5][^1_9] |
| **Discontinue HRT/OCP** (4 weeks pre-op if high VTE risk) | Reduces thromboembolic risk | Individualized decision [^1_1] |


***

### Intraoperative Phase

| Intervention | Rationale | Evidence/Notes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Short-acting anesthesia** (propofol, remifentanil, sevoflurane) | Faster emergence, less postoperative sedation | Preferred over long-acting agents [^1_9] |
| **Multimodal, opioid-sparing analgesia** | Reduces opioid-related side effects (nausea, ileus, sedation) | Acetaminophen, NSAIDs, gabapentinoids, regional blocks [^1_7][^1_5][^1_3] |
| **Regional anesthesia/nerve blocks** (epidural, TAP, paravertebral) | Superior pain control, reduces opioid requirement, improves pulmonary function | Thoracic epidural strongly recommended in open abdominal surgery [^1_7][^1_9] |
| **Goal-directed fluid therapy (GDFT)** | Avoids fluid overload (edema, ileus) and hypovolemia (AKI, hypoperfusion) | Stroke volume optimization using dynamic parameters [^1_6][^1_5] |
| **Normothermia maintenance** (forced-air warming, warmed fluids) | Prevents coagulopathy, SSI, cardiac events | Core temperature >36°C [^1_1][^1_5] |
| **Minimally invasive surgery** (laparoscopic/robotic) | Reduces tissue trauma, pain, ileus, LOS | When feasible and oncologically safe [^1_1][^1_2] |
| **Avoidance of routine drains/tubes** | Prevents discomfort, infection, delayed mobilization | NG tubes, peritoneal drains, urinary catheters only if indicated [^1_1][^1_7][^1_5] |
| **Antibiotic prophylaxis** (timed appropriately) | Reduces SSI | Single dose or redosing per guidelines [^1_1] |
| **Thromboprophylaxis** (mechanical + pharmacological) | Reduces VTE risk | LMWH or UFH unless contraindicated [^1_1][^1_5] |


***

### Postoperative Phase

| Intervention | Rationale | Evidence/Notes |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Multimodal analgesia** (non-opioid first-line) | Minimizes opioid side effects, facilitates mobilization | Acetaminophen, NSAIDs, regional techniques [^1_1][^1_7] |
| **Early oral intake** (within 24 h, often day of surgery) | Stimulates gut motility, reduces ileus, preserves muscle | Clear liquids → solids as tolerated [^1_1][^1_6][^1_7] |
| **Early mobilization** (out of bed day 0–1, ambulation day 1–2) | Prevents atelectasis, VTE, muscle wasting, improves gut function | Key predictor of reduced LOS [^1_1][^1_7][^1_5] |
| **Avoidance/removal of NG tubes** | Reduces pulmonary complications, discomfort, facilitates feeding | Only for specific indications (vomiting, obstruction) [^1_1][^1_7][^1_5] |
| **Early urinary catheter removal** (within 24–48 h) | Reduces UTI, facilitates mobilization | Except in pelvic surgery or prolonged procedures [^1_1][^1_7] |
| **Nausea/vomiting prophylaxis** (multimodal antiemetics) | Facilitates oral intake, comfort | 5-HT3 antagonists, dexamethasone, ondansetron [^1_1][^1_7] |
| **Continued VTE prophylaxis** (extended in high-risk patients) | Prevents post-discharge VTE | Especially in cancer, orthopedic, pelvic surgery [^1_1][^1_5] |
| **Structured discharge planning** | Ensures safe transition, reduces readmissions | Criteria-based discharge, not day-based [^1_10] |


***

## Strongest Predictors of Success

Meta-analyses identify these ERAS elements as **most strongly associated** with reduced LOS and morbidity: [^1_7]

1. **Absence of nasogastric tube**
2. **Early mobilization**
3. **Early oral nutrition**
4. **Early epidural removal**
5. **Early urinary catheter removal**
6. **Non-opioid analgesia utilization**

***

## Clinical Outcomes and Evidence

### Benefits of ERAS Implementation

| Outcome | Effect Size | Evidence |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Length of stay (LOS)** | Reduced by **~2.35 days** on average | 2020 meta-analysis across specialties [^1_6] |
| **Healthcare costs** | Reduced by **~\$639 per patient** | Same meta-analysis [^1_6] |
| **Postoperative complications** | Reduced (ileus, SSI, pulmonary, cardiac) | Multiple RCTs and cohort studies [^1_2][^1_7][^1_3] |
| **Readmission rates** | No increase (or slight reduction) | Well-implemented pathways [^1_2][^1_10] |
| **Patient satisfaction** | Improved | Better pain control, faster recovery [^1_3] |
| **Mortality** | Trend toward reduction in some specialties | Especially colorectal, cardiac [^1_8] |

### Evidence Base

- **Study types**: RCTs, meta-analyses, large prospective cohort studies, registry data. [^1_2][^1_7]
- **ERAS Society guidelines**: Procedure-specific, regularly updated based on systematic reviews. [^1_2]
- **Value-based care**: ERAS is a model of evidence-based quality improvement with cost-effectiveness. [^1_2][^1_3]

***

## Implementation Framework

Successful ERAS implementation requires **systematic, team-based approach**: [^1_1][^1_2][^1_10]

### Key Steps

1. **Form ERAS team** (multidisciplinary):
    - Surgeons, anesthesiologists, nurses (pre-op, OR, PACU, ward), physiotherapists, dietitians, pharmacists, ERAS coordinator. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_10]
2. **Develop protocol**:
    - Adapt ERAS Society guidelines to local resources and patient population. [^1_1]
3. **Education \& training**:
    - All staff (including outpatient, ward, rehabilitation) must understand rationale and protocol. [^1_2][^1_9]
4. **Patient education**:
    - Preoperative counseling sessions, written materials, expectation setting. [^1_1][^1_5]
5. **Audit \& feedback**:
    - Track compliance with ERAS elements, LOS, complications, readmissions. [^1_2][^1_10]
6. **Iterative improvement**:
    - Address barriers (cultural resistance, resource limitations, workflow issues). [^1_1]

### Common Barriers

- **Cultural resistance** (surgeons/anesthesiologists accustomed to traditional practices)
- **Lack of coordination** between departments
- **Inadequate staffing** (nursing, physiotherapy)
- **Resource limitations** (regional anesthesia expertise, GDFT monitoring)
- **Patient factors** (comorbidities, social support, health literacy)

***

## Contraindications / Limitations

ERAS pathways are designed for **uncomplicated postoperative courses**. [^1_7]

**Not suitable** when:

- Patient requires **fluid resuscitation or vasopressor support** (hemodynamic instability)
- **Major complications** (anastomotic leak, sepsis, re-operation)
- **Prolonged ventilation** or ICU admission
- **Severe comorbidities** preventing early mobilization/feeding

In such cases, patients are managed outside the ERAS pathway until stabilized. [^1_7]

***

## Special Considerations in Critical Care

As an intensivist, you may encounter ERAS patients who **fail the pathway** and require ICU admission:

### ICU-Specific ERAS Considerations

- **Hemodynamic monitoring**: GDFT principles continue in ICU (avoid fluid overload, use dynamic parameters).
- **Analgesia**: Continue multimodal, opioid-sparing approach; consider regional techniques if not contraindicated.
- **Nutrition**: Early enteral feeding (within 24–48 h) even in ventilated patients if hemodynamically stable.
- **Mobilization**: Early passive/active mobilization as feasible (even in ventilated patients).
- **Delirium prevention**: Minimize sedation, maintain sleep-wake cycle, early extubation.
- **Catheter/drain management**: Remove as soon as clinically safe (urinary catheters, central lines, drains).

## Landmark Trials and Guidelines

### Key Studies

| Study | Population | Intervention | Outcome |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Kehlet H (1997)** | Colorectal surgery | Multimodal care pathway | First description of ERAS concept [^1_6][^1_8] |
| **ERAS Society guidelines** (various) | Multiple specialties | Procedure-specific protocols | Evidence-based recommendations [^1_2] |
| **Meta-analysis 2020** | Multiple surgical specialties | ERAS vs conventional care | LOS ↓2.35 days, cost ↓\$639 [^1_6] |
| **Multiple RCTs** | Colorectal, hepatobiliary, orthopedic | ERAS elements | Reduced complications, faster recovery [^1_7][^1_3] |

- **ERAS Society** (www.erassociety.org): Procedure-specific guidelines (colorectal, gastrectomy, cystectomy, hepatectomy, pancreatic, bariatric, orthopedic, cardiac, etc.)

## Exam-Oriented Points ★★

- **ERAS = multimodal + multidisciplinary + evidence-based** perioperative pathway.
- **Three phases**: Pre-op (counseling, carb loading, no bowel prep), Intra-op (GDFT, normothermia, opioid-sparing, regional), Post-op (early feeding, mobilization, no NG/catheters).
- **Strongest predictors of success**: No NG tube, early mobilization, early feeding, early catheter removal, non-opioid analgesia. [^1_7]
- **Outcomes**: ↓LOS (~2.35 days), ↓complications, ↓costs (~\$639/patient), no ↑readmissions. [^1_6]
- **Contraindications**: Hemodynamic instability, major complications, prolonged ventilation. [^1_7]
- **Implementation**: Requires dedicated team, education, audit, feedback. [^1_1][^1_2]

***

## Topic Pearls

- ERAS is a **physiology-based, bundled care pathway** targeting surgical stress response across all perioperative phases.
- **Carbohydrate loading** (not fasting) and **opioid-sparing multimodal analgesia** are cornerstones.
- **Early mobilization and feeding** are the most impactful postoperative interventions.
- ERAS reduces LOS by ~2.35 days and costs by ~\$639/patient without increasing readmissions.
- Successful implementation requires **multidisciplinary team, education, audit, and culture change**.
- Not suitable for patients with hemodynamic instability or major complications requiring ICU-level support.
- ERAS Society provides **procedure-specific, evidence-based guidelines** regularly updated.
