
## CSF Examination Results in Meningitis and Encephalitis

Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analysis is a cornerstone in the diagnostic workup of suspected central nervous system (CNS) infections. Characteristic patterns in opening pressure, cell count, differential, protein, glucose, and ancillary tests help differentiate bacterial, viral, tuberculous, fungal, and other etiologies. [^1_1][^1_2][^1_3][^1_4]

Below is a comprehensive, clinically oriented table summarizing typical CSF findings across major types of meningitis and encephalitis, integrating data from multiple authoritative sources. [^1_1][^1_5][^1_6][^1_3][^1_7][^1_4][^1_8]

## Typical CSF Findings by Etiology

| Parameter | Normal CSF | Acute Bacterial Meningitis | Viral (Aseptic) Meningitis | Tuberculous Meningitis | Fungal Meningitis | Herpes Simplex Encephalitis |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Opening Pressure** (mm H₂O or cm CSF) | 60–200 mm (6–20 cm) | **Elevated** (often >200–300 mm) [^1_9][^1_7] | Normal to **mildly elevated** (80–150 mm) [^1_7] | **Elevated** [^1_1][^1_4] | Normal to **elevated** [^1_1][^1_4] | Normal to **elevated** [^1_3] |
| **Appearance** | Clear, colorless | **Turbid, cloudy, purulent** [^1_5][^1_3][^1_4] | Clear or slightly cloudy [^1_5][^1_3] | Cloudy or viscous [^1_3][^1_10] | Clear or cloudy [^1_5][^1_3] | Clear; may be **bloody/xanthochromic** (hemorrhagic necrosis) [^1_3] |
| **WBC Count** (cells/μL or/mm³) | 0–5 (all lymphocytes) [^1_1] | **100–10,000** (typically 300–2,000) [^1_1][^1_9][^1_7] | **10–1,000** (rarely >1,000) [^1_5][^1_7] | **50–1,000** (often 100–500) [^1_6][^1_7] | **25–1,000** (often 100–500) [^1_3][^1_4] | **20–500** (usually <500) [^1_3][^1_7] |
| **Predominant Cell Type** | Lymphocytes (mononuclear) | **Neutrophils (PMNs)** (>80%) [^1_1][^1_9][^1_4] | **Lymphocytes** (may see early PMN predominance in first 24–48 h) [^1_1][^1_5][^1_7] | **Mixed** (lymphocytes + PMNs); lymphocytic predominance [^1_1][^1_6][^1_8] | **Lymphocytes** (mononuclear) [^1_1][^1_2][^1_8] | **Lymphocytes** (mononuclear); RBCs may be present [^1_3][^1_7] |
| **Protein** (mg/dL or g/L) | 15–45 mg/dL (<0.4 g/L) [^1_1][^1_4] | **Markedly elevated** (100–500 mg/dL) [^1_5][^1_9][^1_7] | **Normal to mildly elevated** (50–200 mg/dL) [^1_5][^1_3][^1_7] | **Markedly elevated** (200–2,000 mg/dL; often very high) [^1_3][^1_4] | **Elevated** (25–500 mg/dL) [^1_3][^1_4] | **Normal to elevated** (<100 mg/dL typical) [^1_3] |
| **Glucose** (mg/dL or mmol/L) | 45–80 mg/dL (2.6–4.5 mmol/L); >50–66% of serum [^1_1][^1_4] | **Low** (<40 mg/dL or <40% of serum; often <50%) [^1_1][^1_9][^1_8] | **Normal** (or slight decrease) [^1_1][^1_5][^1_8] | **Low** (<40 mg/dL or <50% of serum; often very low) [^1_1][^1_6][^1_8] | **Low** (20–40 mg/dL or <50% of serum) [^1_3][^1_4][^1_8] | **Normal** (typically preserved) [^1_3][^1_7] |
| **CSF:Serum Glucose Ratio** | >0.6 [^1_4][^1_11] | **Very low** (<0.4) [^1_9][^1_6][^1_4] | **Normal** (0.4–1.0) [^1_6][^1_11] | **Very low** (<0.3–0.4) [^1_6][^1_4] | **Low** (<0.5) [^1_4][^1_11] | **Normal** [^1_11] |
| **Lactate** (mmol/L) | <2.5 mmol/L | **Elevated** (>6 mmol/L strongly predictive) [^1_5][^1_6] | **Normal to mild** (0–6 mmol/L) [^1_5] | **Elevated** [^1_5] | Normal to mild elevation | Variable |
| **Gram Stain / Microbiology** | Negative | **Positive** in 60–90% (depends on organism load) [^1_1][^1_9] | Negative (culture sterile) [^1_10] | **AFB stain** low sensitivity; **PCR** preferred [^1_1] | **India ink** (Cryptococcus), **cryptococcal Ag**, fungal culture [^1_1][^1_3] | **PCR for HSV** (gold standard); culture low yield [^1_1][^1_7] |
| **Other Specific Tests** | None | Bacterial culture, multiplex PCR panel [^1_1][^1_9] | Viral PCR panel (enterovirus, HSV, VZV, etc.), IgM for arboviruses [^1_1] | Mycobacterial culture (≥30 mL CSF), Xpert MTB/RIF, interferon-gamma [^1_1] | Cryptococcal antigen, Coccidioides/Histoplasma serology, fungal culture (≥30 mL) [^1_1] | HSV PCR, EEG (temporal lobe changes), MRI (temporal lobe hyperintensity) [^1_7] |
| **RBCs** | Rare/absent | May be elevated [^1_5] | Normal [^1_5] | Elevated [^1_5] | Normal [^1_5] | **Elevated** (hemorrhagic necrosis) [^1_5][^1_3] |

## Key Clinical Pearls and Caveats

- **Early viral meningitis** may show neutrophilic predominance in the first 24–48 hours, mimicking bacterial meningitis; repeat LP or PCR can clarify. [^1_1][^1_7]
- **Partially treated bacterial meningitis** (prior antibiotics) may show lower WBC counts, lymphocytic shift, and sterile cultures; glucose often remains low. [^1_7]
- **Tuberculous and fungal meningitis** often present with **very high protein**, **low glucose**, and **lymphocytic pleocytosis**, but cell counts are typically lower than in acute bacterial meningitis. [^1_1][^1_6][^1_4]
- **HSV encephalitis** is distinguished by **RBCs in CSF**, normal glucose, and lymphocytic predominance; PCR is diagnostic. [^1_3][^1_7]
- **CSF lactate >6 mmol/L** is highly suggestive of bacterial meningitis, especially when Gram stain is negative. [^1_5][^1_6]
- **Cryptococcal meningitis** in immunocompromised hosts may show very low WBC counts despite high organism burden; cryptococcal antigen is critical. [^1_1]
- **Opening pressure** is often markedly elevated in bacterial, TB, and fungal meningitis; therapeutic LP may be needed in cryptococcal meningitis. [^1_9][^1_4]


## Diagnostic Approach Summary

1. **Suspect bacterial meningitis** if: neutrophilic pleocytosis (>80% PMNs), glucose <40 mg/dL or ratio <0.4, protein >100 mg/dL, lactate >6 mmol/L, turbid appearance. [^1_9][^1_6][^1_4]
2. **Suspect viral meningitis/encephalitis** if: lymphocytic pleocytosis, normal glucose, mild protein elevation, clear CSF; confirm with PCR. [^1_1][^1_5]
3. **Suspect TB/fungal meningitis** if: lymphocytic or mixed pleocytosis, very high protein, very low glucose, elevated opening pressure; send large-volume CSF for culture/PCR/antigen. [^1_1][^1_6][^1_4]
4. **Suspect HSV encephalitis** if: lymphocytic pleocytosis with RBCs, normal glucose, temporal lobe findings on MRI/EEG; HSV PCR is diagnostic. [^1_3][^1_7]
